## Is it designed? No

Two consoles are the least designed part of the game.

- **What exists is a sketch, written before the cable worked:**
  - The canon's section 13 has side by side: each plays their own world, the treaty shares key and
    tempo, the parts split so you are each other's third, and a clap fixes drift. Echoes can be
    traded on a card. And "if a link between units proves out", each sees the other's creature live,
    across the water.
  - `experience-fixes.md` section 10 adds fixes from the playthroughs: the treaty carved on the
    river post, the clap is the one wherever it lands, treaty fights subdivide the beat, you sing
    the notes you share, and the hush leaves a seat for a voice in the room.
  - Single lines elsewhere: "the treaty is two wheels pointed at one wedge" (`the-wheel.md`); "one
    holds and one sings, then swap" (`the-way-home.md`); with JJ's console linked, the third voice
    "can be JJ" (`the-page-and-the-place.md`).
- **Nobody designed a visit,** meaning your Hum walking in someone else's lake. In the canon each
  player stays in their own world.
- **"Visits are by copy" was my wording** in this morning's card doc. It meant a spare card carrying
  copies of takes, and it read as if it settled what a visit is. It doesn't. I've changed it to
  "letters" in `the-card.md`. In this doc a visit is what you describe.
- **The cable changes what's possible.** The canon's decision "two consoles that can't hear each
  other" was taken before the link was tested. Since 2026-11-13 we know two handhelds on one USB-C
  cable trade messages in half a millisecond with nothing lost (`docs/hardware-target.md`). That is
  enough for two consoles to run one world together.

## The idea in one breath

Alone, your past selves are your band. Together, the other person is. A friend in the room is a live
echo, and an echo is yourself from another day. So anything two people can do together, one person
can do with an echo, and the game stays whole alone.

## A visit, from the couch

_Imagined, to show what it could be like._

Two handhelds on a couch, a short cable between them. Each screen shows its own lake. In each lake
there is now a second small figure, drawn in the other's colour: the other one's Hum, where they are
in their own world, live. Call, and a ring of light runs out; theirs comes back.

You both hold R1 and point at the same wedge. A four-count plays on both speakers, you clap on the
one, and two songs become one song from two speakers: one console sings roots and fifths, the other
the thirds. That's the canon's treaty. Over the cable the two never drift, so nobody has to clap
again.

Then you walk your Hums to the same spot, each in your own lake, face each other and hold one note
together. Between two voices on one note there is a standing wave that holds still, and this one
hangs in the air like a doorway made of light. One of you steps through.

On the other side it's their day, their key, their garden. Your home is still yours, though, so
their garden looks and sounds different through your eyes. The boulder that is kin to them, and
weightless, is a fifth above you, so it falls toward you. The creature that keeps away from them
comes close to you. A knot of two strands becomes two jobs: you take one strand, they take the
other, and you strike together on the beat the strands share. When their swell runs out of breath,
yours carries the cadence home.

At dusk the river takes you back. Their world keeps what you did in it, and if they want, your walk
stays in their lake as a blue echo. Both books get the evening as one page with two voices on it.

## Every way two consoles could meet

The possibilities, from the farthest apart to the closest.

1. **Letters.** A take is only its inputs, a few kilobytes. On a spare card, by hand or by post, a
   friend's takes walk your lake in blue, and yours walk theirs (canon section 13, `the-card.md`).
   Nothing is live. It's a few kilobytes of someone, played back.
2. **Side by side.** In one room, each in your own world. The treaty shares key and tempo, the parts
   split across two speakers, and each sees the other's Hum in their own lake. Without the cable it
   drifts and you clap back in; with it, one clock and no drift.
3. **A visit.** One world, two Hums: the host's lake, and the guest as themselves. Described above,
   with the rules below.
4. **Drip's seat.** The second console plays Drip instead of a second Hum: the drop that rides on
   Hum's head and keeps a living time. Drip's player keeps the time, points the way with a ring of
   light, sings the thirds and holds a note under Hum. There's nothing to steer through the Glass
   Hills. It's for a parent with a child, for someone who has never held a controller, or for anyone
   who wants to be in the music without the thumbs. Alone, the game plays Drip, as it does now.
5. **The water you share.** After the ending. The canon's duet sends the Mirror Pool downstream to
   the ocean, and the fourteenth morning opens lands tuned by thirds that never repeat. Two players
   who both get there could share that water: one stretch of new lands that lives on both cards and
   grows when the two of you are linked. It would be the only world that is both of yours. Thirds
   are the interval the other person brings (canon: "you are each other's third"), so the lands of
   thirds are where it belongs.
6. **Contests.** Your multiplayer favourites are mostly contests, with Minecraft Dungeons the co-op
   one (brainstorm 6.2). Three that come out of the same rules, light enough for a calm game:
   - **Trading fours.** Musicians' own contest: four bars each, back and forth. The garden's
     creatures drift toward whoever's phrase comes home best, and the round ends in one cadence you
     both release.
   - **The tug.** Each puts home on a different wedge, and every creature is pulled by its interval
     to both of you. When the song ends, count who holds more kin.
   - **The pocket duel.** One hit, and fast: dodge into the pocket and strike on the beat. A hit
     turns you grey, and the winner sings the cadence that brings you home.
7. **More than two.** A USB hub could join one host and several guests. A band of five consoles
   would be one per vowel, bass to treble. Nothing about it has been tried.
8. **Half a world each** (doesn't fit). Shape 2's idea: each console holds half of everything and
   the world is only whole when both play. Shape 2 set it aside because playing alone would be half
   a game, and that still holds.

## The ultimate, as I'd build it

All of it except half a world each:

- **Three distances:** letters when you're apart; side by side in one room, everyday; a visit when
  you want to be in one world.
- **Two seats:** a second Hum, or Drip.
- **One contest,** trading fours, because it's how musicians compete and play together at once. The
  tug is a maybe. The duel is the one that risks the calm.
- **After the ending,** the water you share.
- **More than two,** if a hub works.
- **No second console needed** for anything. Every door, fight and land can be done alone.

The rules of a visit:

1. **The host's world, the guest's self.** The day, the key, the lands, the creatures and whatever
   changes are the host's. The guest brings their own Hum: their home on the wheel, their voices,
   their breath, and up to four of their echoes, retuned into today's key as echoes always are.
2. **Two homes, one world.** Each screen and each speaker is one player's ears. A creature's shape
   and pulse are its interval to your home, so the same creature is a hoop on one screen and a
   pretzel on the other. Where it goes is one thing for both: it drifts toward the one it's kin to
   and away from the one it's across from. Put home on the same wedge (the treaty) and you see the
   same world. Hold different wedges and the gap between you is something to use.
3. **An echo can always stand in.** No door, fight or land needs a second console. Together is
   easier, warmer and louder.
4. **Call and response first.** Most of what two players do together is a phrase and an answer, not
   a unison. That works over the cable now, and it will still work over the internet, where a friend
   arrives a bar late (`docs/other-platforms.md`).
5. **Nothing is lost when the cable comes out.** Both consoles hold the whole visited world. If the
   link breaks, the guest's Hum turns blue, finishes its phrase as an echo and waits, humming in
   tune, as the canon's broken echoes do. The host plays on, and the guest's console takes the river
   home.
6. **Both books get the page.** A visit is one day on two pages: the host's in the host's book and a
   copy in the guest's, both voices on it.
7. **Same game on both.** Two consoles run one world only if they run the same version. If they
   differ, the cable says so first, and the newer one can copy itself over (the program is a few
   megabytes) if both players agree.

## What two can do in one world

All of these come from rules the game already has. The right-hand column is how one player does the
same thing alone.

| together | alone |

| **Two homes on one thing.** Kin to one of you, a boulder is weightless; a fifth from the other, it falls toward them. One lifts, the other draws it in. | move home between the lift and the pull |
| **One strand each.** A knot is two strands that stumble. Each takes one, and you strike together on the beat they share. The Great Knot's five against seven: one holds the five, the other the seven, and you clap where they meet. | the wheel puts both strands on one home, as in the wheel toy |
| **Question and answer.** A door that wants a phrase that stops halfway, on the fifth, and an answer that comes home. | your echo asks, you answer |
| **Carrying the cadence.** When one swell runs out of breath, the other's carries the release. | a longer breath |
| **Hearts in step.** Hum near each other and your steadiness steadies theirs (canon section 10: "your heartbeats drift into step"). | the vine, when it comes round |
| **One holds, one sings.** A drone under a tune, then swap (`the-way-home.md`, the Reed Mouths). | your echo holds |
| **Lending voices.** The host lends a voice the guest hasn't met yet, for the visit. | meet it |
| **Two bands.** Two Hums and eight echoes: ten voices for a chord. | five voices |
| **The hush seat.** One console drops its top line so a person in the room can sing it out loud (`experience-fixes.md`). | sing along anyway |

## The river and the loop, with two

The loop (`the-whole-game.md`, "The loop and the river") was designed for one player. With two:

- **Each of you keeps your own loop:** your mornings, your echoes, your river, your Grid and your
  Wolf. Nothing the other does turns your wheel.
- **Side by side, at dusk,** both rivers sing at once. Over the cable they sing the stretches you
  spent in the treaty as a duet: your melody and their thirds.
- **The river post has room for two.** The treaty is carved on it (`experience-fixes.md`, 10).
  Choose Onward together and your mornings stay in step; choose apart and they drift. A morning
  apart you still share six notes, three mornings apart four, and straight across two, which stay an
  open question until one of you sails over.
- **A visit happens in the host's day.** The guest's own day stops where they left it, sun and all,
  and carries on when they come back. A visit doesn't turn the guest's wheel, so their echoes don't
  age while they're away.
- **At the host's dusk the river sings the day with both voices.** Then each of you chooses what to
  keep:
  - The host can keep the guest's walk as one of their four echoes. It walks the host's lake in the
    guest's colour, on every Repeat of that day and every day after.
  - The guest can keep their own walk from the visit. It is retuned into their own key like any
    echo, and walks their lake as a self that has been somewhere their world hasn't.
- **A day you shared can be lived again.** On a Repeat the friend's echo walks it again, the same
  path and the same notes. Or the friend comes back live, and since two people never retrace a day
  in step, nothing pales: the Grid can't grow on a visit.
- **A friend's echo ages like your own,** by your mornings travelled. On its eleventh dusk it steps
  in front of you like any other:
  - Let it go, and the water keeps it: the vine can hum it, a pool plays it, the tenth morning rains
    it.
  - Turn away, and it joins the Wolf. Taming the Wolf then includes letting it go.
  - Sing it again, and if your friend is linked that dusk they can sing it with you, so the new take
    has both of you on it.
- **Dreams can join your paths.** A shortcut traced in blue at dawn can run from where you walked to
  where your friend walked in your lake.
- **Each world's vine belongs to its own player.** In a visit the host's vine hums the host's tunes,
  not yours. Your vine waits in your world.
- **Letters are echoes too.** A friend's take that comes on a card walks your lake in blue and ages
  by your mornings like any other.

## Sizes

What the hardware says, from the cable test on 2026-11-13 (`docs/hardware-target.md`) and the wheel
toy measured today.

| what a visit needs | how much | what the cable did |
|---|---|---|
| both players' hands, every step | 24 bytes a step at 60 steps a second: about 1.4 KB a second each way | 1,400-byte packets, 200 a second for 10 seconds, none lost |
| an answer inside a step | under 16.7 ms | round trip 0.47 ms typical, 6.1 ms worst, over 5,050 packets |
| the host's world when the guest joins | the wheel toy's whole world is 4.7 KB, and its sound engine 97 KB. The game's will be larger; a few megabytes at most (an estimate) | a high-speed USB link, 480 Mbit/s |
| battery | | the black drew 413 mA linked and 397 mA before, 4% more |

- **Lockstep.** Both consoles run the same world from the same two players' hands. Before each step,
  each waits for the other's hands. At half a millisecond the wait is invisible, and even the worst
  round trip measured fits inside a step. Measured today: the same take makes the same world and the
  same sound on different processors, and in WebAssembly and C builds too
  (`docs/hardware-target.md`). Over the cable both consoles are the same model, which is simpler
  still.
- **Two sound cards, two clocks.** Each console's sound runs from its own crystal. Crystals commonly
  differ by up to about 50 parts per million, which over a twenty-minute day is about 60 ms: a flam.
  That hasn't been measured on these units. The guest follows the host's clock over the cable and
  stretches its own sound by an inaudible amount.
- **Who can host.** Only the black, so far. The purple needs the same device-tree change the black
  got (`probe/tools/set-dtb.sh`). Then either can host.
- **Power.** The hosting console switches its port's 5 V on itself, and it must never do that with a
  computer on that port (`docs/hardware-target.md`).
- **Not tried yet:** a link that stays up for hours, the purple as host, and pulling the cable
  mid-session. The rule above (nothing lost) assumes the link drops cleanly.
- **Picture and sound.** Each console draws and sounds the world for its own player, with one more
  Hum and up to four more echoes than alone. The wheel toy's own work is 0.7 ms of a 17.1 ms frame.

## What to test first

A visit toy: the wheel toy's garden on both handhelds over the cable, in lockstep, with two Hums,
two homes and the parts split across two speakers. It would answer:

1. Does the world read clearly through your own home on your own screen, with someone else's Hum
   changing what's near?
2. Does the standing-wave doorway feel like doing something together?
3. How far apart do the two units' sound clocks run, and does following the host's clock stay
   inaudible?
4. What happens when the cable comes out?
5. Do two speakers sound like one song?

It needs the black to host, so it waits until you've finished testing on it, or until the purple has
the dual-role device tree.

## On other platforms

The same design carries over. Two Decks or two Switches on one network do what the cable does. One
TV with two pads suits Drip's seat best, because Drip rides on Hum's head and needs no second
camera. A friend far away arrives a bar late, as a live echo in canon with you.
`docs/other-platforms.md` has the details.
