# The way home: mornings eight to twelve

Brainstorm, 2026-11-14. Nothing here is decided. In the seven playthroughs of
`thirteenth-morning.md`, the sun rises over five lands after the Seam with one voice among them and
nothing to face (`playthroughs/what-they-reveal.md`). This document fills them. It leans on
`the-wheel.md` throughout, and it gives a home to four things no diary used: Break, a pool's memory,
the echo band in a fight, and the beloved anywhere but the garden.

---

## The shape of it

- **The first half of the ring is going out.** Each morning a new voice, a new mood, a new land,
  further from the garden.
- **The second half is coming back,** and it should not be more of the same. On the way out you
  learn to bring things home. On the way back, the thing coming home is home itself, and it is
  coming back a hair off.
- **None of the five mornings is a boss to beat.** Each is one thing to face, and one way of coming
  home that the first half didn't teach. Fierce makes each a real test of timing. Gentle lets each
  be sat with.

### The garden's note comes back

The garden's note left the wheel on the third morning. On the eighth it rises again at the dark end,
a comma sharp, and walks one wedge a morning toward home:

| morning | land | the garden's note sits under | so that day is about |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | the Aster Steps | the darkest (Break) | a voice that cracks |
| 9 | Verbena Hollow | fierceness (Strike) | the close that misses |
| 10 | the Rain Steps | tenderness (Remember) | what you let go |
| 11 | the Reed Mouths | cool soul (Flow) | being the ground |
| 12 | the Porch | drive (Drive) | the evening before |
| 13 | the Garden | sunny home | home, and not home |

The moods were named long before this table was worked out. They fit the lands: tenderness over the
rain, flow over the delta, drive on the last evening.

### Two suns

- **The moment.** On the eighth morning the sun comes up beside itself: two discs a hair apart, for
  half a second, and then one. Each morning after, the two take a little longer to agree. On the
  twelfth, at the Porch, it is three full seconds. On the thirteenth, over the garden, they don't.
- **Underneath.** At the Seam, F♯ and G♭ are agreed to be one note. Past it, the day is still
  climbing by pure fifths and is spelled in sharps, while the lands on this side are spelled in
  flats. On a piano they are the same keys. In truth they are a comma apart. Each dawn the land
  gives way and takes the day's note, a small treaty. The lands far from home are the most tempered
  and give way at once. The lands near home are tuned pure and give way slowly. The garden is home,
  and it doesn't give way at all.
- **How it sounds.** A second or two of beating that closes into the dawn chord, a little longer
  each morning (clip 29). It is the only place the comma is heard before the ending, it is always
  brief, and until the last morning it always resolves.

## The eighth morning: under glass

- **The moment.** The Aster Steps are terraces of the garden's own flower, on the far side of the
  world. On the top terrace, under a dome of chrome and glass, is your garden. It is exact: the
  spigot, the hose coiled as it lay before the knot, the asters in rows. Nothing in it moves. You
  open the wheel and there at the dark end is a green you know. You point at it and call, and Hum's
  closed *mm* cracks. Through the crack, for one beat, comes an open sound. It is the first time Hum
  has made one.
- **What you face.** The dome. It is tuned to the garden exactly as it was. You are tuned to the
  garden as it is going to be, a hair warmer. Hold your note against it. The difference between the
  two shakes the glass in a fast shiver, a second, two, and it gives. Inside, the asters turn out to
  be real. They have gone to seed, and the seed blows down the terraces and takes you with it.
- **What it means, unsaid.** The Grid has already built what it will offer on the thirteenth
  morning: home, sealed, the same for ever. You meet the offer five mornings early, and you break it
  with the only thing the copy doesn't have, which is the change in you.
- **The wheel.** Break wakes here. Standing home on the darkest wedge puts your one note a half step
  above home, the closest and the harshest place it can be. It costs steadiness each time: Hum's
  tone wavers afterwards. The canon called Break "rare and costly" without saying why. It is costly
  because it is your own voice cracking. It is also how a closed hum starts to open.
- **Underneath.** The day's key is seven fifths up from the garden's, and its seventh note is five
  more: twelve fifths, the garden's note a comma sharp. Hold two notes a comma apart and they beat
  three and a half times a second at middle C.
- **Gentle and fierce.** On Gentle the Greys that tend the dome only walk their path, and two bars
  of holding is enough. On Fierce they march on you in step, the patrol has to be broken with swing
  first, and the hold needs a full breath taken between their bars.
- **How it sounds.** Clip 29 has this shiver in its dawns. The crack in Hum's voice is one for a
  later sketch.

## The ninth morning: the close that misses

- **The moment.** Dusk in violet thickets. Across the valley the vine is humming something you let
  go a week ago, a little wrong. Between you is the pack, in two colours: the Hollow's blue and the
  Rain Steps' azure. You do what has worked a hundred times. You wear an azure one grey, stand
  beside it, hold, and let go on the one. It falls toward home and lands next to it, doubled,
  howling. You try again. It misses again.
- **What you face.** Getting across, and then not fighting. Stand with the vine and breathe with
  them until they come into one outline. They sing one note, and it is green: the garden's. On the
  wheel, blue and azure and green, the threads close into a triangle for the first time. Both halves
  of the pack have something between them to tune to, and each moves a hair. Hold the swell under it
  and let go on the one. The whole pack lies down as a chord.
- **What it means, unsaid.** The one who remembers you sings your home into the place you are most
  afraid of. And the oldest lesson of the first half, stand beside it and it comes home, has a place
  where it fails. Force won't close it. A third voice will.
- **The wheel.** A chord is a triangle: two neighbouring wedges and one four round. Your own fifths
  give you the two neighbours. The third corner always comes from someone else. From this morning,
  with the vine or a voice beside you, triangles are a new kind of door.
- **Underneath.**
  - The Hollow's tuning makes its thirds sweet by narrowing its fifths, and that leaves one fifth
    far too wide to use, the one between G♯ and E♭. It is 35.7 cents wide, and it is what musicians
    named the wolf. It lies exactly between these two lands (clip 11 has it).
  - A♭, C and E♭ are a major chord, and the garden's note is its third.
  - Four of your fifths overshoot the vine's third by 21.5 cents. Priya measured it: 408 against
    386.
- **Gentle and fierce.** On Gentle the vine comes to you. On Fierce the pack goes for the vine's
  sound: when they are struck the vine slips out of focus and has to be breathed with again, so the
  crossing is pockets and throws, and the hold has to be guarded.
- **How it sounds.** Two notes that almost make a fifth and howl, then the same two with a third
  between them, moved a hair, ringing (clip 31).

## The tenth morning: what you gave comes back

- **The moment.** The Rain Steps are terraces where clouds rest. One cloud has kept everything. It
  is so full it has sunk onto the top terrace and can't rain, and below it the steps are dry and the
  river doesn't run. You find its breath, which is slow, four bars in and four out, and you breathe
  with it. You stand on its amen side and hold the swell as it sags lower. You let go on the one. It
  rains.
- **What comes down.** Every puddle on the terraces holds a few seconds of a day you let go, playing
  in blue. The more you have given to the water, the more there is to see. If you have let nothing
  go, it rains the cloud's own memory: the lake from above, and your path round it so far in its
  colours.
- **What it means, unsaid.** The cloud is doing what the game has asked of you, and you are the one
  who helps it. What you gave away was never lost. It went up, and here it comes down.
- **The wheel.** The garden's note sits under tenderness today. Stand home there and every pool on
  the Steps shows the garden on the first morning.
- **Who is here.** The caravan, which trades in what is let go. Puffball, whose spores hang in the
  rain, so that each drop that strikes one rings.
- **Underneath.** A held note that sinks and settles is the amen stretched out. Water going up from
  a lake and coming down as rain is the lake's memory circling (`memory-in-rhythm.md`, ideas 4 and
  5). The river this frees raises the lake.
- **Gentle and fierce.** On Gentle there is nothing to dodge. On Fierce the cloud's lightning comes
  in four against your three for the whole hold, and each strike you slip in the pocket is one bar
  less to wait.
- **How it sounds.** Clip 17 has the amen beside the full close.

## The eleventh morning: be the ground

- **The moment.** A delta at low water. Thousands of reeds, each one singing, none together: a chant
  with no floor under it. You have one note. Put home under it and hold. One reed finds your note
  and stands on it. Then the ones beside it. Then a whole bank, and a mouth of the river opens and
  water moves.
- **What you face.** Holding. Wind tries to push you off the note, and on Fierce so do patrols.
  While you hold the swell you can't strike, so your echoes and your voices strike for you: the band
  you chose at the river fights while you stay. You dodge, and you keep singing. A blow shakes the
  note, and one bank of reeds scatters.
- **What it means, unsaid.** All game you have been the one with only one note. A drone is one note,
  and it is the most generous part there is. Everyone else stands on it.
- **The wheel.** Put home down once, on the wedge where the garden's note sits today, and don't move
  it. This is the morning the wheel teaches staying.
- **Underneath.** This land is tuned in pure fifths, and its music has always lived on drones and
  open fifths (`shape-3-the-lake-ring.md`, Part 5). A held note under moving voices is the oldest
  accompaniment in the world.
- **Two consoles.** One holds and one sings. Then swap.
- **Gentle and fierce.** On Gentle the wind only leans on you and the reeds lock by themselves, bank
  by bank. On Fierce there are three mouths, and each wind pushes harder against the beat than the
  last.
- **How it sounds.** Voices wandering, then a low held note under them, and they settle into a chant
  over it (clip 30).

## The twelfth morning: the evening before

- **The moment.** Leaves coming down beside a porch with its light on. Across a narrow water is the
  garden, close enough to see the spigot. A rocking chair on the porch is still rocking. All day,
  everything leans one wedge to the left, and nothing lands.
- **What you face.**
  - **The oldest echo.** If you have carried a take all the way from the first morning, this is its
    eleventh dusk. It steps in front of you on the porch steps, doubled, and waits one bar
    (`experience-fixes.md`, fix 1).
  - **The chair.** Someone sat here. Push it on its own beat, the smallest push at the right moment,
    and the porch's pool shows them in blue for one phrase. Who they are is Morgan's to say.
  - **The night with no ending.** The river carries you, but tonight its song stops on the chord
    before home and stays there until morning. It is the only night of the twelve that doesn't
    close.
- **The wheel.** The garden's note is under drive, the wedge beside home. Open the wheel and it is
  plainly two greens now, leaning.
- **Underneath.** A phrase that ends on the chord before home is the half close. The Porch is also
  the land the garden's amen comes from: every dawn in this game is an amen, yesterday's key
  settling into today's, and tomorrow's is the Porch settling into the garden.
- **Gentle and fierce.** The same for both. This is the one morning with nothing to win.

## What Fierce players get

Theo wanted more than three bosses. The way home has no bosses, and each morning still has a real
test on Fierce:

| morning | the test |
|---|---|
| 8 | break a marching patrol with swing, then hold a full breath between their bars |
| 9 | cross a pack that hunts the vine's sound, and guard a hold |
| 10 | stay in the pocket through four against three for a whole long swell |
| 11 | keep one note through three winds while your band fights for you |
| 12 | nothing; and the oldest echo, if you kept it |

The Great Knot, the Metronome and the Wolf at the Seam are as they were.

## Not yet connected

- **Is no boss for five mornings a hole?** The tests above may not be enough for a player who came
  for bosses.
- **The dome.** Glass breaks at its own note, not at one a comma away. The shiver is the picture of
  the difference, not physics.
- **The pack tuning to the vine** needs a look that reads without words: each wolf's doubled outline
  closing as it moves.
- **The eleventh morning leans on echoes.** A player who keeps none has only the voices. It may need
  a floor.
- **The chair and the blue figure** are left empty on purpose.
- **Two suns** adds a moment of beating to five dawns. It is brief and it resolves, but only
  Morgan's ears can say whether it is a pleasure or a tax.
