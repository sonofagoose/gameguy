# Theo, 16

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History: an imagined player of the older thirteen-morning design
> (2026-11-13). Not a real player, and not the design now. What the game is: `design/the-game.md`.

> The hum game is the only boss game that ever made me sit back, let go of my best run, and leave a
> flam open on purpose.

## Day 1

Rafi from the bus put it on my card. "It's a music game," like that helps. 12:40am, under the
blanket, volume on two bars so Mom can't hear.

Black. A drip. That's it. I checked the battery light. On the speaker I couldn't tell where it was.
Low on the screen, a bone-white blob in reeds, two black eyes with a glint in each, the size of my
pinky nail, breathing. I almost quit to the PSP emulator.

Mashed everything. R1 makes it go *mm* and a ring of light runs over the ground: reeds, a coiled
hose, gone. Again: way off, a teal ring came back. Walked over on the left stick. Mumbling, like
someone through a wall. Clicked the right stick and the camera slid into the blob's eyes: a drop
hanging off a spigot, and a line drawing spinning between us that wouldn't close.

Hold R2 to hum, let go on the drop's tick. We were flamming. I know flams. Mr. Pell yelled about
flams all semester before I quit drumline. Two tries and we were clean: the drawing snapped shut and
a lower note came out of nowhere between us. "Oh. You're in." The drop is deadpan. Respect.

Then dawn, green, flowers like skyscrapers, a knot in the hose that moves. Hold R1, time goes thick,
a star-wheel opens; right stick left, R2 at a flowerpot, and it floats. Three little knots come at
me stumbling, five against three. Felt it, couldn't count it. The fork (left button) rings on the
beat even when I'm early; the game moves it there. So it's playing itself? But dodge is the right
button, where rolling is in Elden Ring, and a dodge on the beat slows everything: three hits, three
notes, one chord. Witch Time. I held R2 on the finisher till the breath ran out. It landed anyway,
soft, like it got bored of me. Then water, and for a second someone blue on the pool's edge. Gone.

## Day 2

Bus. My buds won't pair (no Bluetooth) and the speaker loses to the engine. Muted, it still throbs
on the beat. One eye shut. Gas-station earbuds.

At dusk last night a river carried me home and sang my day back, backwards from the knot, like a
killcam made of my own hits. Keep up to four takes as blue ghosts; pick Gentle or Fierce. Fierce. A
post with two carved signs: dots, live the day again; bracket, go on. Bracket. Morning came up
yellow-green, same music lifted, like the key change in a last chorus.

## Day 4

OK.

Dusk, edge of the violet valley: wolves. Black, doubled outlines, red eyes, the only red on the
screen. Their howl is two notes rubbing, wah-wah-wah, exactly me and the next bass drum a hair apart
on a unison.

Fierce is tight. Then I held the pocket a whole phrase and a drum came in every bar: kick, snare,
something like tenors, cymbals. That's a drumline, assembling. L1 and R1 together and the music
drops, and all five wolves reset and lunge on the same beat. One dodge. ONE. Slow-mo, strike strike
strike strike, a chord, all grey, hold R2, let go on the one.

They fold into pups. The pups follow me. The pups DRUM.

I have a drumline. Lock-on moved to my middle finger so my index can clap. 2:10am. Mom's alarm goes
at 4:50, so I have rules. Broke them.

## Day 6

Doorstop is an iron wedge holding a gate open for nobody. One word a day. Today: "Fine." Bass 5
energy. (Biggest drum. That was me.)

In the Chrome Nave (a car's insides, church-sized, all chrome and pink) I bound two radio knobs and
a seatbelt buckle to my pulse, drove the knobs three against two, and it walked. Like a drunk table.
Rode it into a Grey patrol and ground-pounded off the top with Doorstop.

A take is just button presses, so I put it on Rafi's card, and now my drunk table walks his lake in
blue. He said you're not supposed to do that. There's no supposed to.

## Week 2

The Metronome. Under the bridge city, everything purple, then the arena locks and goes clean: no
grain, colour in flat steps, geometry snapping like a PS1 game. Every dodge I throw, it's already
there. It learns you.

You don't die. The sun just goes down on you, the river sings your day back, and your day is losing.
So: repeat. Same route, same plan, five nights. Grey paths wear into the ground where I walk, and
every time there are more Greys on the bridge. Elden Ring lets me run it back eighty times. This
punishes practice. Battery died at 2:40 mid-fight. I lay there thinking about deleting it.

## Day 12

Drip's tick is lazy. Swung. I've been straightening it out in my head for two weeks, the way I did
everything Mr. Pell played: early, on top. "Sit back in it, Theo." I thought he meant slower.

He meant late.

Took a different road so the paths stopped going grey. Under the bridge I put every hit a hair
behind, on Drip's time instead of the Metronome's. It couldn't read me. Its lines started wobbling,
the grain came back, the tick went da-DUM, da-DUM. The Metronome swung. My pups came in under it.
Held R2 with my palms sweating on the shell, let go on the one, and it landed so hard I said "YES"
out loud at 1am and the dog barked.

Kept the take. Not letting that one go.

## Week 3

Eleven days after I kept it, at dusk, my Metronome run walked out of my band, howled two seconds of
that sour rubbing, and left for the Wolf. It took my best run.

Went home for the Great Knot, the whole hose: five against seven. They only meet every thirty-five,
and you clap that one. Counted out loud under the blanket. The vine was there, humming my tunes back
wrong, like Mom singing in the car. Didn't stay.

Lake's still low, so no tune ever finishes. Like a sneeze that won't come.

## Week 4

Went down into the crack for my run. The Wolf is every take I never let go. It dodges early, like me
before. You can't beat yourself. You let each take go, that howl goes quiet, and there's the run,
blue, walking into the water. Metronome one last.

Freed the last river I could find, and a tune actually finished. Didn't know I needed that.

Then the thirteenth morning. The garden, but the green leans warm, like somebody nudged a filter.
Everybody's there: Drip, Patio, Doorstop ("Hi."), the white cat, Jeanne, the fly, my pups, the vine.

Did the Grid's offer first, because sealing sounded like winning. The credits loop in grey, perfect,
the cleanest the game ever looks. Watched four loops. Horrible. Pressed Start.

The river gave me the morning back. Duet: you and the vine hold the gap. Two notes almost one,
swelling, wah... wah... a flam that won't close, and everything in my hands wanted to close it. You
can't. You hold it. Then a third note under both of us, low, that neither of us was singing. The
pool went downstream.

Not writing anything else about that.

## Week 5

Band room at lunch, first time since I quit. Rafi showed me C on the piano. From the lowest one I
went up a "morning" at a time by ear, the open gap. Twelve mornings and you run out of piano exactly
on a C. The game says twelve mornings comes home a hair warm; the piano says home. But the piano's
gaps shimmer, a tiny bit, like inks almost in, and the garden's don't. So the piano cheats a little
on every gap so it can close. The whole piano is the Seam.

Mr. Pell asked if I'm doing spring. Said maybe.

## Week 7

Past the lake now, lands that don't repeat. Fierce, chasing my own ghosts. Three bosses wasn't
enough. Traded a take at the caravan for a note that opens the sky, and held the Bell Meadow's note
up and up till it turned into light, the meadow's exact orange. Rafi says I'm lying. I have the
take.

On the bus Rafi and I swap one earbud each and do the treaty: both hold R1, point at one spot,
four-count, clap on the one. His game sings the thirds. Mine's fine alone, just open; with his it
goes warm. When they drift, I call the clap.

Mom's radio played a Remember song. I said so. She asked what that means. I don't have another word.

Last night I sat Hum down on the shore, the fly landed on his knee, and I just listened. Like ten
minutes.

Spring sign-ups are Monday.

**Worked:**

- The pocket and the clap made sense at once to a player who knows Witch Time, and a drum joining
  each bar did the job of a combo meter by ear, with nothing on screen.
- Because the Greys and the Metronome break only when hit behind the beat, his rushing, on-the-click
  drumline hands became the thing he had to unlearn, so the hardest fight taught the lesson his
  instructor never got across.
- Losing to the sunset instead of dying, with the river singing the loss back, stung without a
  corpse run, and the backwards replay after a win felt to him like a killcam made of his own hits.
- Wolves that fold into drumming pups gave a trophy hunter trophies that play, and his pack became
  the groove under his best fight.
- With no score to show, takes that are a few kilobytes of inputs gave a competitive player a way to
  brag and to prove it: a walking contraption on a friend's lake, a sky secret nobody believed.
- The comma landed because the game had spent weeks training him to close flams, and asking him to
  hold one open was the one thing that could reach him.

**Broke:**

- The opening asks for patience he doesn't have: two minutes of black and a drip nearly lost him to
  the emulator, and a drip 'to the left' means nothing on a mono speaker at two bars of volume.
- Snapping his early strikes onto the beat made the first fight feel like the game was playing
  itself, and the skill ceiling stayed hidden until the river offered Fierce at the end of the first
  day.
- The Grid's grey paths hit the run-it-back loop his whole gaming life is built on: for five nights
  it felt like punishment for practice rather than for sameness, and he nearly deleted the game
  before he worked out the difference himself.
- The beloved is at the garden every third day and he was always out fighting, so the heart of the
  story was a blurry vine he walked past, and the duet reached him through drumming rather than
  through them.
- Three bosses was thin for someone who plays for bosses, and the game needs ears in a way his bus
  can't give: his earbuds are useless with no Bluetooth, and the speaker loses to the engine.

**Wish:**

- Make the Grid's rule clear on the first repeat: show that grey wears in only where you do the same
  thing, so an action player reads it as a dare to vary rather than a fine for practising.
- Put the beloved where action players go: have them turn up at a fight somewhere on their triangle
  and sing the third under one of his cadences, so the duet isn't with someone he walked past.
- Show the skill ceiling before the first river: one moment in the knot fight where an early strike
  sounds plainer than one on time, so players like him hear that the snapping is a floor and Fierce
  is waiting.
