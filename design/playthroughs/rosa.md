# Rosa, 34

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History: an imagined player of the older thirteen-morning design
> (2026-11-13). Not a real player, and not the design now. What the game is: `design/the-game.md`.

> It's the only bit of my day that ends properly: it sings it back short, I keep four takes, and the
> rest walks into the water.

**Day 1. Staff room, 3:40am**

Bought this clone at 3am last week for Pokémon Crystal on breaks. My nephew added this, "because
it's chill".

Bisi asleep in the recliner, so one earbud in, one ear on the corridor. Black screen. A drip, close.
Low down, a bone-white lump in reeds, two dark eyes with a glint in each. Breathing. I checked it
wasn't me.

Pressed A out of habit; it hopped sideways. Pressed everything, like a new pump nobody's trained you
on. R1 made it hum, *mm*, and a ring of pale light ran out over the ground: reeds, a coiled
hosepipe, gone. Far off, a teal ring came back. First thing all week that answered instead of
asking.

Walked to it with the left stick. It answered muffled and late, like next door's telly. One line
came up: click the right stick. Like clicking a pea, but the camera slid into its eyes: a drop
hanging under a tap, never falling, and between us a fine white line going round, never closing.

Hold R2 to hum, let go on the drop's tick. I used to count drips per minute for a living; four goes.
Two sounds became one, the line closed, and a low note came up between us that neither of us was
singing. "Oh. You're in," said the drop, flat as the shipping forecast, and fell on the lump's head
and stayed.

I laughed out loud. Bisi woke up. Bed 4 alarmed. Start, pocket.

**Day 2. The 8:10 bus**

Two dawns: one out of the bus window, one coming up green on Hum's held note. Asters like
cathedrals, the hose in a knot, the knot moving.

Held R1: everything went syrupy, and a string star opened round me, two wedges lit. Right stick at
the left one and it all went Christmas advert, snow starting.

Three little knots came off the big one, lurching like a funny-looking strip. Y swings a tuning fork
that lands on the beat whatever I do: cheating, in my favour. A as one lunged, and everything
slowed: three hits, three notes, a chord. Held R2 while it swelled, sweet, till my finger ached,
then let go. It landed like my key in the door after a twelve-hour shift.

The knot fell open, water ran, and for a second a blue someone sat on the pool's edge. Then nobody.
Missed my stop.

**Day 4. Bed, 9:40am**

Blind down, Dev's side still warm. He's a postie; we hand the bed over like a shift.

First dusk. A river carried Hum home and sang my day back: half a minute of my own little tunes,
running backwards from the knot, so I heard which presses did it. My takes stood on the bank in
blue. I kept four. The rest walked into the water.

That's the bit I never get. Handover isn't an ending; it just gives your night to someone else. Oh,
I thought, like the drop. I'm in.

Gentle or fierce? I'm a night nurse. Gentle. Then a post with two carvings. One, two dots and a fat
line, was choir: Mrs. Kettering's pencil, go back, do it again. Took the other.

Fell asleep in the night music; woke at 1:50 with it humming on my chest. Two hours straight: a
lie-in.

**Day 8. Staff room, 2am**

Every morning comes up in a new key, a lift like a Westlife key change, and the light goes green,
lime, custard. I never get a whole day in one go: morning on a break, afternoon on the bus, dusk in
bed. After three nights on I'd no idea why Hum was up a watering can.

Talk is X. I keep pressing A, which is dodge, so Hum leaps away from Box Elder, a chatty sapling, in
terror.

At the garden, a flowering vine I can't get in focus, blurred and doubled like my contacts at 5am.
Hummed with it and for a second it went sharp. It hummed yesterday's tune back slightly wrong, like
Dev with whatever I sang in the shower. With it there, my blue band stopped sounding like a church
and started sounding like a hug.

Back every third morning, like the Lapras on Fridays in Crystal. I can manage that.

**Day 11. Bus**

The Glass Hills ring when the wind goes over. An iron doorstop holds a gate open up there for
nobody. One word a day. Today's: "No."

Higher up, a gate wants three of me singing at once: me, plus two takes from other days, standing
where I stood. I'd let the right take walk into the water; I choose at the river with my eyes shut.
Three days of setup, repeat days I think. An hour of game; a week of my breaks. When I repeated,
grey lines wore into the ground behind me. Marked down for being boring by the thing making me be
boring.

I have a rota. I'm not doing two. Played Crystal for nine days.

**Day 20. Bus, 8:15am**

Lost Mrs. Hallam at ten past four. Did the things you do. Opened the window.

Didn't want Crystal. Opened this to sit. D-pad down: Hum sits and listens, and a fly lands on its
knee, every time. Then round to the Porch, unlit today, leaves coming down. Crossing over, one note
in the music went soft, like someone dimming one light. Kept that take.

That night Hum dreamed, and at dawn a way round the Glass Hills was drawn in blue, one I'd never
walked. It did my puzzle in its sleep. Teach me.

**Week 4. Bed, 10am**

The Porch take went today, eleven mornings on: stepped out of my band at dusk, howled a few seconds,
went south to the Wolf. Old takes fade, but pale blue on a 3.5-inch screen on the 8am bus isn't a
warning. Would I have let it go if it had said? No. Still.

**Week 5. Staff room, 4am**

Bay 3 has two sats probes beeping at nearly the same rate. They drift apart, stumble, line up for a
few beats, drift again. I wait for the line-up now.

Mr. Pryce in bed 9 hums when he can't sleep. Tonight I hummed under him without thinking, the low
one that makes it warm. He stopped, looked at me, said "You're flat," and went to sleep.

No levels, no numbers; the lake's my exp bar. Now it's up, the tunes finish. They used to stop a
step short, like my sister on the phone.

**Week 6. Bus, to the depot**

Rode the cool one, top of the star, a jazz bar at closing, across to the Seam: a bridge city,
purple, slightly off, all of it joined up. Down in the crack, the Wolf: doubled, red-eyed. Its howl
was every take I never let go, my Porch walk among them. I knew my own tune.

On Gentle you don't beat it, you comfort it. We use that word on the ward, for when we stop trying
to fix someone. Hold R2 while it swells, let go on the one, and the note that lets it rest comes out
of you. You only choose when. One howl at a time. Mine came out blue and walked into the water under
the bridge. Took its time.

Stayed on to the depot. The driver asked if I was alright. I said I'm a nurse, which isn't an
answer, but he took it.

**Week 8. Sofa; Dev's off too, once in three weeks**

Thirteenth morning: the garden again, but the green was off, warmer, like a photo of home with a
filter on. Everyone there at once.

Three offers. I took the Grid's first: same day forever sounded like annual leave. Grey credits, the
same bars, perfect, round and round. Restful for a minute, then like a ward with every bed made and
nobody in them. Pressed a button; the river offered the morning again.

So, the duet: me and the vine, holding the gap between us. Two notes almost one, swelling in and
out, and I knew it: bay 3, the probes. Then a third note between us, home, that neither of us was
singing.

Dev looked up from his phone. "Do that again."

You can't. That's the point, apparently.

**Week 11. The 8:10 bus**

The mornings don't repeat now. Each night: up, down or stay. I pick stay a lot and nothing goes
grey, so it never minded staying, only staying the same. Hum doesn't go *mm* any more; it goes *uh*,
like someone singing at the sink. Dev says so do I.

Never opened that gate. Never worked out the clap. Still press A to talk.

I do the river in my head now. Four takes from the shift: "You're flat", Bisi's tea, sun on the
flyover, the probes lining up. The rest walk into the water. Works one morning in three. Better than
melatonin.

**Worked:**

- Suspend anywhere, with only the music and the sun as a clock, let a player whose breaks end with a
  call bell stop mid-phrase at no cost, which is the only reason she got past the first night.
- The night river is the design's best gift to a shift worker: a half-minute replay, a keep-four
  choice and the rest walking into the water gave her the ending her real shifts lack, and she
  carried the ritual off the screen and into her mornings.
- Meeting voices by getting in phase made the first verb one this non-musician was already good at
  (she has counted drips and read rhythm strips for a living), and that ear grew, untaught, into
  hearing two monitors beat against each other and finding a harmony under a patient's hum.
- The lake's level gave a player who needs to see progress a visible exp bar, and the almost-endings
  resolving as it rose let her feel a cadence without ever meeting the word.
- Building the Wolf from unreleased echoes, and letting Gentle end it by comfort, turned her worst
  moment (losing a take she had kept for eleven mornings) into her best, because what she lost came
  back so she could let it go properly.
- The Grid's offer tempted exactly the person it should: she took it because the same day forever
  sounded like annual leave, and a minute of perfect grey looping taught her the whole theme without
  a word.

**Broke:**

- Echo-chord gates need planning across several days, but the keep-four choice comes at the river
  when she is falling asleep, so one drowsy pick turned a single gate into an hour of setup (a week
  of her breaks); she quit for nine days and never opened it.
- Repeating a day to rebuild the take that gate needed wore grey paths into the ground, so the game
  scolded her for sameness its own puzzle demanded, and nothing let her tell chosen repetition from
  habit.
- The eleven-day rule gave no warning she could perceive: fading blue on a 3.5-inch screen, in
  daylight, on a moving bus, carries nothing, so she learned the rule only by losing the take.
- Face buttons by position put talk on the top face (printed X) and dodge on the right (printed A),
  so two decades of Pokémon sent Hum leaping away from every creature she tried to speak to, and
  eleven weeks in the habit hadn't gone.
- The twenty-minute, song-shaped day never once played whole for her, so its form was invisible to
  her, and coming back after three nights on she had no idea where she was or what she had been
  doing.

**Wish:**

- When a player comes back after a long gap, let Drip hum the last few minutes back in ten seconds,
  a pocket version of the river's replay that ends facing where she was heading, so someone who
  plays in fragments is never lost and still needs no menu.
- Put the stakes of the river's choice in the ear: a take the world has asked for (a gate's chord, a
  plate to hold) should ring with that chord when she looks at it, and from about the eighth day a
  hoarded echo's dusk hum should carry a brief edge of howl, so neither the week's setback nor the
  loss arrives as a surprise.
- Grow grey paths only from sameness nobody chose: a Repeat taken to rebuild a take that a known
  puzzle needs should not grey the ground the way drifting through the same day does.
