# Priya, 27

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History: an imagined player of the older thirteen-morning design
> (2026-11-13). Not a real player, and not the design now. What the game is: `design/the-game.md`.

> I came to find the bottom of it, and it handed me a proof that there isn't one.

**Day 1**

Saturday, old flat, on the floor between moving boxes. Earbuds, notebook #9. Bought a forty-quid
handheld for this one game.

Black. A drip, close, in my left ear. Low on a screen the size of a playing card, a bone-white lump
in reeds, two dark eyes with a glint in each. I could hear it breathe. No title, no text.

I pressed R1 because my finger was on it. The thing went *mm* and a ring of pale light ran over the
ground: reeds, a coiled hose, gone. Again. Far off, a teal ring came back.

Left stick toward it; the voice was smeared, a neighbour through a wall. Clicked the right stick and
the camera slid into the lump's eyes: a drop under a spigot, never falling, and between us a fine
line figure, turning. A Lissajous figure, three lobes by two. A fifth.

Hold R2, let go on the drop's tick, press again on the next. Six tries. Then the flams pulled into
one sound, the figure stopped dead, its inks snapped into register, and a third note bloomed under
us, lower than either. "Oh. You're in," said the drop, flat as a timetable, and fell onto my head. I
laughed out loud. Margin: *difference tone?*

Dawn came up green: asters like cathedrals, a knotted hose, the knot moving. Phone tuner, left over
from orchestra, on my held note: C, plus zero. Baseline. I held R1 and a wheel bloomed, twelve
points strung into a star. Right stick to the left wedge and the music opened like a window.

Then knots, five against three. Left face strikes, right face dodges into a slow pocket where three
strikes make a chord, R2 holds the swell until the downbeat. I mashed the strike off the beat on
purpose; it still landed on the beat. Everything I do sounds good, and I don't trust that yet. Rez
with a textbook taped underneath?

The hose sprang open, the pool rose a finger's width, and for a second a blue figure sat on its
edge. Eleven questions.

**Day 4**

Tess's sofa, ours since Thursday. Speaker this time. Tess was chopping onions, humming the garden
tune with a fourth where the fifth goes. I told her. "It's a hum, Priya."

Glass Hills, the D morning. A crystal boulder blocked a ravine. Struck, it rang an A. Second wedge,
the sunny mood: it slid toward me. Third, the bluesy one: it went weightless and I shoved it off the
ledge like an empty box.

That's when it clicked. Moods aren't spells. Each wedge is a note, and a mood is where I put home:
in D the first wedge stands on G, the second on D, the third on A, seven neighbouring points of the
star, bright to dark by fifths. The boulder only cares about the interval between us: kin
weightless, a fifth above falls toward me. Tomorrow the ring turns a notch and that boulder becomes
kin to the sunny one. The physics has a date in it. The puzzle isn't where; it's when.

Wrote THE WHEEL IS A SLIDE RULE across a page. Tess asked what was funny. "Logarithms." She went
back to the onions.

**Day 9**

Saturday, nine hours, cable in.

Question: does the gate under the garden bells open at noon, or when the bells meet? (Three, four
and five beats meet every sixty: Chinese remainder theorem on a rope.) That meant living one morning
three times, changing only when I arrived. By the third Repeat my path was grey, the grain gone,
colours banded flat. The Grid grows on sameness, and a controlled experiment is sameness on purpose.
I'm being marked down for method. Outer Wilds never minded me living the same loop forty times.

It reads my footsteps, not my reasons. Fourth run, I zig-zagged, hummed moods I didn't need, went
the long way past the white cat, and the ground stayed alive. Randomise the nuisance variables. It
works, and it's a tax on every experiment. The Grid is a code reviewer that reads the diff, not the
intent. (It's the bells.)

**Week 2**

On call; played between pages, which this game is weirdly good at. Chrome Nave: every key its own
colour, says the songbook. My tuner agrees; my ears don't.

My best take howled. The Great Knot, five against seven, every clap on the downbeat they share: the
cleanest thing I've done here, kept every night because it was perfect. At dusk it stepped out of my
band, outline doubled, red eyes, howled for four seconds, a beating that set my teeth on edge, and
walked south toward the crack. Nothing warned me. I was furious for an hour.

Then the maths. Eleven fifths up is a fourth plus the comma: the wolf. Lovely, except it howled on
the A♭ morning, eight fifths from the C morning I sang it, a minor sixth within two cents of sweet.
My Repeats counted as days. The howl is a timer, not the interval. First number in this game that's
decoration.

Let the other three takes go. Tess asked about the box marked SELL? with my violin in it. Not yet.

**Week 3**

Last boxes unpacked, bar one. The vine was at the garden, as every third morning. Blurred and
doubled until I matched its breath, then sharp as a pen line, it hummed yesterday's Glass Hills tune
back to me a little wrong. Focus narrows the sound to what you look at, which makes a phone tuner
usable. My third yesterday: 408 cents. The vine's: 386. The songbook's one line: *the syntonic
comma*.

Mrs. Halloran used to tap my left hand with a pencil. "Lower, it's a third." Then, in scales:
"Higher, it leads." I thought she was being fussy. She had two tunings in her head, a comma apart,
and I had a pencil mark.

Later I gave the Caravan an echo for a note off the wheel, thirty-one cents flat of the nearest
piano key: seven over four. It opened the sky. I held an A and climbed, ticking doublings in the
margin; at forty it arrived as orange light. 440 × 2⁴⁰ hertz is 620 nanometres. I checked twice,
then just watched.

**Week 4**

The Metronome took three evenings; it only lost when I swung.

Down in the crack the Wolf is every echo you never released, my Great Knot claps still perfect
inside the howl. Each swell held on R2 and let go on the downbeat resolves one outline into the
water. Mine went last, on an open fifth.

**Week 5**

Sunday, 6:40 a.m. I set an alarm so the thirteenth morning would be a morning. Tess asleep. Kitchen
floor, earbuds, tuner on my knee, save backed up to the laptop, because I am who I am.

The sun came up over the garden and the green was off, warmer, like a photo with the white balance
nudged. Everyone was there: the vine, Jeanne, the white cat, the fly on the lump's knee. Tuner on my
held note: C, plus 23. I'd computed 23.46 in week one. I said "ha" to an empty kitchen.

My notebook had the order: Grid, Wolf, duet; bad endings first, like a grown-up. I did the duet.
Held R2 beside the vine and the comma swelled between us, two tones almost one, the slow wobble of
an A string before the beats stop. I didn't look at the tuner. Then a third voice, sung by neither,
not lower this time but between. The Mirror Pool went downstream.

Afterwards: C, plus 12. Half a comma. The vine had held the first morning's C all along. Home is now
3⁶/2^9.5, there's a root two in it, and no path of pure intervals will ever land on C again. Not a
story beat: a proof. I wrote QED and cried a bit, recorded here for completeness.

**Week 7**

Restored the backup for the Grid's ending: the seam sealed, credits looping in grey. Left it running
overnight, in case. Recorded two loops off the headphone jack, flipped one, summed: silence, down to
my laptop's hiss. Flow's trick. No secret in it; that's the horror. Skipped the Wolf's. Copied the
duet save back and deleted the backup.

Now I walk the lands past the lake, tuned by thirds. Threes and fives never multiply out to a power
of two, so it never repeats. First game I can't finish. I don't want to.

Took the violin out of the SELL? box and tuned it the old way, fifths by ear, waiting for the wobble
to stop. Played a third on the top strings and let my upper finger sink until it rang, and under it,
faint, a third note, lower than both. Tartini claimed it in 1714. Drip got there first. Tess was in
the kitchen humming the garden tune, still a fourth where the fifth goes. Her version. I left it.

**Worked:**

- Drawing the interval as a figure between two voices let her read a 3:2 off the screen in the first
  five minutes, before any rule was spoken, and that bought a sceptical player's trust.
- Making each mood a position on the circle of fifths, with physics driven by the interval between
  things, put the calendar inside every puzzle, so 'when' became the variable and the wheel read as
  a slide rule.
- Keeping the comma exact let the central claim survive an outside instrument: B♯ read C +23 on her
  phone tuner, and setting the post-duet home at half a comma made 'never C again' something she
  could prove.
- Retuning a remembered tune into the singer's own tuning made the vine's 'a little wrong' a real
  21.5-cent syntonic comma, which explained her old violin teacher's corrections and got the violin
  out of its box.
- Teaching release by consequence (the hoarded take howled, then turned up inside the Wolf) changed
  a completionist's habits more than any instruction could have, down to deleting her save backup.
- Focus narrowing the sound to one voice made a phone tuner usable, and the Grid's ending being
  truly identical (two recorded loops cancelled to silence) kept even the bleak ending honest.

**Broke:**

- The Grid growing on sameness taxed the controlled experiment: repeating one morning with one
  change wore grey paths into her world, so every test had to open with a performance of variety,
  where Outer Wilds never charged her for a repeated loop.
- Echo age counted days lived rather than fifths moved, so after her Repeats the hoarded take howled
  at a near-sweet minor sixth, and the one rule built on the wolf interval turned out to be a timer.
- The well-tempered lands' colour per key was measurable on her tuner but inaudible on the mono
  speaker and on earbuds, so that stretch of the tuning history never registered as its own sound.
- For the first hours, auto-tuning and beat-snapping meant she could not play a wrong note to test
  anything, which read to a Witness player as possible shallowness until the interval physics
  surfaced on day four.

**Wish:**

- Have the Grid measure sameness of choices and outcomes rather than footsteps, so a Repeat that
  deliberately changes one decision counts as a different day and experiments stay free.
- Count an echo's age in fifths moved, not days lived, so the eleven-day howl always lands on the
  wolf interval it stands for.
- Let each well-tempered land play one phrase in two keys side by side, at a border or a bell, so a
  key's colour is heard on this speaker and not only measured.
