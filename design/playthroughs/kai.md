# Kai, 9 (with notes from his mum, Lena, 40)

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History: an imagined player of the older thirteen-morning design
> (2026-11-13). Not a real player, and not the design now. What the game is: `design/the-game.md`.

> It's the one where you sing at the scary stuff till it turns into puppies, and the river's mean
> but it's sort of right.

*Kai talks, Mum types.*

**Day 1**

Birthday present from Uncle Dev. Played it in the car home in the dark, headphones on (Mum's rule).

It starts all black. Then a drip, right by my left ear. I took that earphone out. I thought the car
was leaking.

At the bottom of the little screen, a white thing in some grass. Two black eyes with a shine in
each. You can hear it breathing. I call him Mm because that's all he says.

B jumps. R1 makes him go *mm* and a ring of light runs out over the ground. Nobody answered. I did
it again and a blue-green ring came back from far away.

I walked to it with the left stick. It was a voice but squashed, like next door's telly through the
wall. I nearly gave up. I pressed EVERY button. Then I pushed the right stick IN (I didn't know
sticks were buttons) and we zoomed into his eyes.

It's a drop of water under a tap that never falls. Between us, a drawing made of one line goes
round, wobbling. I held R2 and Mm held his note and it went *duh-dum, duh-dum*. I let go on the
drop's tick, pressed on the next, and it went DUM, one sound, and a low note came in the middle that
neither of us was singing. Words: "Oh. You're in." The drop fell on his head and stayed. It's his
hat now. His name is Plop.

Then morning, and everything went green. Flowers bigger than our flats. A hosepipe with a knot that
MOVED. I said "snake" out loud. Mum said what snake.

Hold R1 and everything goes slow and boomy and a star comes round him. I pushed the right stick left
and the music went sparkly. A flowerpot floated and I rode it like a boat. Y hits with a fork and it
always lands on the boom, even when I press early. When I miss it doesn't sound bad, just less. At
the end you hold R2 and let go on the big boom. The snake turned into a loop and water came out, and
a blue someone was sitting by the pond. Then gone.

It goes warm in your hands like the class hamster.

**Day 2 (Lena)**

*He says there are no words in it. There are, a line at a time. He skips them, so everything has his
names instead. In the bath he hummed a little tune, mouth shut as always, then the same tune again,
higher. "That's tomorrow."*

**Day 5**

Wolves come at sunset. They look like a photo when someone moved, two on top of each other, with red
eyes. The howl goes *wow-wow-wow* and you feel it in your teeth. I was in bed with headphones. I
went and got Mum.

She sat on my bed. I clicked the left stick and Mm went dark and quiet (I think they can't find you
then). One found us anyway. We're on soft (Mum set it at the river), so I didn't hit it. I held R2
and sang at it. It lay down. Then it folded up like paper and it was a PUPPY. With a drum. It
follows me and drums when I walk.

Nothing dies in this game. Things turn into other things.

His name is Biscuit. I don't need a real dog now. (I do.)

**Day 9**

Doorstop is a doorstop, but iron and fat, holding a gate open for nobody. He says one word a day and
I write them down: OPEN. NOBODY. HEAVY. YOU. HM. (Hm isn't a word. I wrote it anyway.)

He sings the same note as Mm but in the basement. The line between them makes an 8. Doorstop plus
the floaty one lifts a rock as big as a car. Big voice, big stuff.

Twiggy (the little tree) talks SO much and the mushroom gossips about I don't know what. It's all
words. I press X till they stop. Twiggy sings good though.

At forks there's a white cat. Sit with it and it sings you both ways. I sit with it even when I know
the way.

The vine comes to the garden every third day and hums my tune from yesterday back wrong, like
Grandma singing along to the radio. You only see it properly when we're singing the same. Then our
heartbeats go together.

**Day 11 (Lena)**

*At dinner he drummed three with one hand against two with the other and didn't lose it. "That's
Biscuit and that's Pepper." I tried. I can't. Then: "Happy and sad are the same notes. It's just
where you land." Driving, the two indicators ahead of us blinked together for a bit. "They're in,"
he said. He has a word now.*

**Week 2**

Blue Mm is gone.

At night the river takes you home and sings your day back (it's long). Blue Mms stand in the water,
all the times you did stuff. You can keep four. The rest walk into the water. I always keep four,
obviously. Blue Mm from the first day was my best one. He's the one who met Plop.

He was going paler every day. I thought he was sick, so I did the top of the star on him, the
healing one. It didn't work. Tonight at sunset he stopped walking with us and howled, a horrible
wobbly one, two notes fighting. Then he ran away over the water, south, to the crack.

I KEPT him. That's the opposite of doing it wrong.

I threw it on the sofa. Not hard.

**Week 2 (Lena)**

*Five days and he won't touch it. "The river's mean." I played it after bedtime, looking for a
reason. Nothing says. A long-eared girl at a caravan warns you about luck, that's all. I think it
wants him to let them go. I didn't tell him. It isn't my game.*

**Week 3**

Went back because Biscuit and them were waiting. (Mum says it's paused. They're WAITING.)

The 27 caravan came. They want blue Mms. I gave them three. That's not letting go, it's trading,
like cards. They gave me a note that isn't on the star, and it opens the sky.

Lights is fairy lights I found hanging off a red bulb on the scary night road. It flies like a kite
and pulls Mm up.

We went up and up till the lake was a little ring. I held R2 the whole way and the note went higher
and thinner till I couldn't hear it, like a dog whistle. Then the whole screen went orange. No black
anywhere. Just orange.

It's the same orange as the bell flowers. I checked with my pencils. I think every note has a real
colour and the sky is where you find out.

**Week 4 (Lena)**

*On the fridge: the lake as a ring of twelve colours, green at the top, warming one way, cooling the
other, purple at the bottom with a crack through it. I sent Dev a photo. "That's the circle of
fifths. Did he copy it?" He didn't. He walked it.*

**Week 5**

The mornings went all the way round. This one was the garden again but the green was warmer, like
when you come back from holiday and the house smells different. Same house.

Everyone was there. Nineteen, with the pups. The vine wasn't blurry for once.

There were words. I didn't read them. First I went to the howl, because Blue Mm was in it, I could
hear his bit. Everything went clean and pretty and the lake ran down the crack like a bath. Then it
was just me and the music. It was still nice music. I put it down and did my homework.

Next day the river gave me the morning again. This time I went to the vine and we sang like always.
Then it went wobbly: two notes nearly the same, *wah, wah, wah*, getting bigger. Wobbly means not
in. I wanted to fix it. The vine didn't, so I didn't. Then a third note came out of the middle. Not
me, not the vine. Mum came in from the kitchen.

Then the pool went down the river to the sea.

**Week 6**

It doesn't go round anymore. Every night you pick up, down or stay, and the new places past the lake
never come back. My new map doesn't join up. It keeps going off the page. Mum gave me tape.

Mm doesn't go mm anymore. He goes *uh*, like his mouth's open. His name's still Mm though.

Doorstop's list is 31 words. Mum says it's a poem. It's not, it's his words.

**Week 6 (Lena)**

*Singing in the bath tonight. Not humming: mouth open, the vowel and everything. I didn't say
anything in case he stopped.*

**Worked:**

- Everything lands in key and on the beat, so pressing every button (which is how a nine-year-old
  explores) sounded like music rather than failure, and a miss that sounded 'not bad, just less'
  kept him trying through a rough opening.
- Nothing dies: a wolf folding into a drumming pup turned what scared him at bedtime into what he
  collected, and gentle mode let an animal-loving child win by singing instead of hitting.
- Doorstop's one word a day was the only text he read by choice, because a single word on a daily
  rhythm suits a slow reader, and it became a list he kept for six weeks.
- Sound and picture as one let a child who draws learn theory by eye: the octave was 'the same note
  in the basement' that drew an 8, the sky's orange matched the Bell Meadow, and his map of the lake
  was the circle of fifths before he knew the phrase.
- Drip's 'You're in' gave him a word for phase that he took out of the game (two cars' indicators
  were 'in'), and the hold-and-release he learned on Drip carried straight into every cadence.
- The two big lessons landed through things a child already does: trading ('that's not letting go,
  it's trading') got him to release echoes, and the duet was just singing with the vine as he had
  every third day, which he reached on the river's second offer.

**Broke:**

- The eleven-day rule failed him: the fading read as sickness, so he tried to heal his first echo
  with Flow, and when it howled and left, the cause was eleven days back and the only hint was
  Jeanne's line about luck, so it felt like punishment for loving something and he stopped playing
  for five days.
- The opening hangs on R3 with nothing on screen to show it; he found the stick-click only by
  pressing every button after nearly giving up, and a less stubborn child stops at the voice through
  the wall.
- Box Elder, Puffball and Jeanne live mostly in text, so for a slow reader they were characters to
  press X past, and he never learned anyone's real name.
- The thirteenth morning's three offers can't be told apart without reading, so his first ending was
  the Wolf's, reached by attachment rather than by choosing; only the river's second offer rescued
  it.
- The dusk wolves, heard through headphones in a dark bedroom, frightened him enough to fetch his
  mum; without her on the bed, the moment the game clicked could have been the moment he quit.

**Wish:**

- Make the eleven-day fate visible and make release look like giving: let an echo nearing its
  eleventh day begin to double like a wolf and turn toward the seam, and let a still pool show
  released echoes in sun-print blue afterwards, so letting go reads the way the Caravan's trade did.
- Teach focus without words at the first voice: when Hum stands by the muffled sound, let the camera
  start leaning toward his eyes and finish the glide only on R3, or draw a small stick-click glyph
  in the line ink.
- Let each of the thirteenth morning's offers sing itself ahead as you approach, the way the white
  cat previews a fork, so a child who can't read knows he is choosing before he chooses.
