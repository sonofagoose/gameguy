# Ash (26) and Bea (23), sisters

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History: an imagined player of the older thirteen-morning design
> (2026-11-13). Not a real player, and not the design now. What the game is: `design/the-game.md`.

> For six weeks on one couch we were each other's third, and the best notes in the room came from
> neither console.

**Day 1 · Ash**

Couch, both volumes up. Black. A drip, close, then the same drip a hair later, to my left, from
Bea's purple one: a tap with an echo. Low on the little screen, a bone-white lump in black reeds,
two dark eyes, a glint in each. It breathes.

R1: a plain *mm*, a pale ring running out over reeds and a coiled hose, gone. Nothing. Again. Far
off, a teal ring answers. R3 slides the camera into its eyes: a drop under a spigot, a fine line
loop turning between us, not closing. Hold R2, let go on the drop's tick. I was early three times;
Bea got it first go. The loop shut, a third sound bloomed lower than either of us, and one line:
"Oh. You're in." It had told me nothing, and I trusted it.

**Day 1 · Bea**

Ash had to show me which one R2 is, and that sticks click. But the drip I just had: you breathe with
it, like coming in on a cue.

Then green, flowers taller than churches. Hold R1, right stick left, and the whole world goes *ooh*;
R2 at the flowerpot and it floats. Steering it onto the awning I could not; I mostly looked at the
sky. Ash did that bit. Then the knot. Fighting is mostly listening: Y on the beat, A when one
lunges. Then you hold R2 and the music swells and keeps swelling and *you* decide when it lands.
It's the last chord of the last song, conductor's hand up, nobody breathing. I held it till my
finger shook and let go on the one. The knot sprang open, water ran, and I said "oh" out loud, like
the drop. Gentle, please.

**Day 3 · Ash**

Lunch, alone; nobody has spoken to me since nine. Fourth morning. Held R1 and just watched: the star
of twelve points, today's wedges lit along the top, and behind them the ring a notch round from
yesterday. The powers stay under my thumb; the world turns behind them.

Post-it: each morning is +7 on a clock of 12. Seven and twelve share no factors, so it visits every
land once before home. Select, hold X on the star: *the circle of fifths*. Fine. Back of the
Post-it: if those are pure fifths, twelve of them overshoot seven octaves by about 1.4%. Home comes
back sharp. I thought I'd found a bug. I think it's the plot.

**Day 4 · Bea**

7:52 train, standing, the purple one warm in my hands like a mug. Headphones, so the drip moves
round my head.

Met Box Elder in the Alfalfa. It mumbles like a radio between stations until you breathe with it,
then never stops talking, then sings the tune. I sang along under my breath and a man in a suit
moved down the carriage.

It's a singing game that can't hear me sing. No mic; Hum has one note and the game picks the rest. I
know that's the point. Eight hours of phone voice ahead, and I still wanted it to hear me.

Sat Hum down to listen (D-pad). A fly landed on its knee. Missed my stop.

**Day 6 · Ash**

First evenings were two radios, her key and mine. The songbook had the fix: the treaty. Both hold R1
and point at the same point of the star, but she's three mornings behind, so her star sits three
notches round and "left a bit" was useless. We did it by colour. "Yellow." "Which yellow." "*The*
yellow." Four ticks on both speakers, a hair apart; we clapped, L1 and R1, on mine, because I'm
older.

Then the parts split: mine roots, fifths and my tune, hers thirds and hers. Mine alone had sounded
finished. Then her thirds came in from forty centimetres away and I heard it had been bright, not
warm. Each console is mono. The couch is stereo.

Every fifteen minutes the beat goes flammy (two clocks, no network), sooner when a fight speeds your
heart. We clap again. The clap is our NTP. Lovely, and in fights a chore.

**Week 2 · Bea**

The Glass Hills. Doorstop is up there holding a gate open for nobody, and between us are crystal
ridges you glide across: hold the bottom button, steer with one stick, fix the camera with the
other, all at once. I fell fourteen times between two stops. Gentle makes the beat wider. It doesn't
make the ledges wider. The crystal rang beautifully every time I fell off it.

It stayed in my bag four days.

Then Ash did the ridges on hers and left the take on the spare card in the egg cup: a few kilobytes
of her. Next morning a blue Hum walked into my Glass Hills humming her tune in my key, and I jumped
on her hum. Doorstop's word for the day: "Finally."

**Week 2 · Ash**

Seventh morning: under the Seam's bridge, the Metronome, a chrome pendulum the size of a tower
block. It locks the arena to its grid (colours band like an old GIF, the grain goes, everything
clean) and it learns you. L2 held, thumb on Y and A, dead on the beat, because that's me. It
predicted me eleven times. Fierce keeps your best takes to beat, so I repeated the day the same way,
grey paths wore into the ground, and I felt told off. Pick one, game.

Bea, other end of the couch, not looking up: "You're square." Then she sang it the way it should go,
lazy, every second note late. I played it her way and it broke. The river sang the fight back
backwards from the win, and the first thing I heard was the late note.

**Week 3 · Bea**

The thing in the air. Ash didn't believe in it. Treaty on green, volumes full, consoles nearly
touching, up by our faces like they had a secret. She held her note, I held my third, we let go on
the same one.

Under both, lower than either, someone was humming in the next flat.

Ash said fridge. Turned hers down: gone. Up: back. Six times, because she's Ash. Then she went very
quiet. I hummed it back to her. It was home, the note the whole chord stands on, and neither console
was playing it.

It needs full volume, a hand from your ears, and knowing what to listen for. I don't care.

**Week 4 · Ash**

Days apart: Bea's at her girlfriend's, looking at flats across the river. At lunch her commute walks
my shore in blue, swung, and I follow it for no reason. When the vine comes round, every third day,
it's the only third I get.

Under the Chrome Nave I built a one-bit adder out of humming: on the beat is 1, off is 0, three hums
vote, half a beat late is NOT. Two hours. Nobody asked. You can hear it carry.

I'm repeating the twelfth morning, differently each time, until she catches up.

**Week 4 · Bea**

Ash's adder sounds like a samba, so I sang over the carry bit. Then I clicked my left stick (hush:
my Hum goes dark and quiet) and sang the third myself, out loud, over her roots and fifths, for the
whole afternoon bridge.

It still can't hear me. Neither console knew. Ash did.

At choir I can't unhear it now: without the piano, a held chord rings like the garden; with it, it's
the bridge city, everyone connected, a little busy.

**Week 5 · Ash**

Confession: I didn't wait. One Tuesday lunch I took the Grid's offer, to see, and liked its perfect
grey loop more than I'll admit. The river offered the morning again.

Saturday, after pancakes, we clapped in on green and went in together. The green was warm, like bad
white balance. I'd done that sum on day three; it didn't help. Everyone was there, even the fly, and
the vine in focus for once.

You hold the gap with them: two tones almost one, swelling. I let go first. Mine resolved into a
third voice, home, sung by neither of us, and I sat in it while hers ached a metre away, a few
seconds longer. Then both pools went downstream, a beat apart.

**Week 6 · Bea**

Now each night it's up a fifth, down a fifth, or stay. Ash goes out past the lake, into lands tuned
by thirds that never come round. I pick stay, and on the train I sit Hum down and let the fly have
the knee.

The flat's signed: March, across the river. Ash checked, and my Tuesday commute is six kilobytes.
That fits in an envelope. We'll clap in when I visit.

The busker at the station plays everything in Flow. Ash says it's called Dorian. It's called Flow.

**Worked:**

- Hue by fifths made the treaty work across different mornings: the sisters' stars sat three notches
  apart, but 'the yellow one' named the same station on both, so colour did the job a note name
  would have done.
- Splitting the parts between consoles made the second speaker audibly necessary without thinning
  either game: Ash heard her own mix go from bright to warm when Bea's thirds arrived from the next
  cushion, rather than being told it.
- Making timing the core verb flipped the household's usual skill gap: the singer who had barely
  held a controller locked Drip in first try and held the cadence like a choir's last chord.
- The Metronome punishing predictability, then the river replaying the win backwards, taught swing
  to exactly the player who needed it and let her hear which late note won.
- Storing takes as a few kilobytes of input turned a spare card in an egg cup into a channel for the
  days apart: Ash's echo carried Bea over ridges she couldn't cross, and Bea's swung commute walked
  Ash's shore.
- Leaving the third tone to the air, unscored and unannounced, made it a discovery the sisters
  checked for themselves, and the moment the sceptic went quiet.

**Broke:**

- Third-person traversal is a gate no musical skill opens: Gentle widens the beat but not the
  ledges, so Bea fell fourteen times in the Glass Hills, shelved the game for four days and got
  across only by following her sister's echo, which means 'alone, the game is whole' wasn't true for
  her.
- A singing game on a handheld with no microphone can never hear the best singer in the room, so the
  best sound of the playthrough, Bea singing the third over Ash's roots, counted for nothing in
  either game.
- Fierce's best takes to beat invite exactly the same-way repetition that grows the Grid, so the
  optimiser met two rules pulling opposite ways and read the grey paths as a scolding rather than a
  consequence.
- The treaty is hard to find and hard to keep: it lives in the songbook, so the first evenings were
  two radios in two keys, and because a fight quickens only that player's heartbeat tempo, the couch
  flams whenever either sister fights and the re-clap turns from ritual into chore.
- The third tone in the air is real but marginal on these speakers: it needed both volumes at full,
  the consoles nearly touching a hand from their ears, and a trained ear to point it out, so a pair
  without a singer would probably never hear it.

**Wish:**

- Let Gentle widen the body as well as the beat (wider landings, longer glides, or a held note that
  carries Hum along a sung path) so a singer can cross the Glass Hills with her own thumbs.
- In a treaty, let a fight speed up the music by subdividing the shared beat rather than changing
  the tempo, so two consoles stay in phase through combat and the clap stays a ritual.
- Make the hush a part for the room: in side by side, let a player drop only her console's harmony
  line while Drip keeps quiet time, so whoever is holding it can sing the third out loud on purpose.
