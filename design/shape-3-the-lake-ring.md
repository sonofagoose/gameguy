# Shape 3: the circle, the lake and the lands

Brainstorm, saved 2026-11-13 at Morgan's request ("please preserve all the ideas you just brought
forth"). Nothing here is decided. It gathers two rounds of conversation that came after
`shape-2-volta.md`:

1. Morgan's correction that the game must sound good to human ears, his wish that the circle of
   fifths be the main thing the player uses, and his question "is a garden really the best place
   for this?"
2. His questions about equal temperament and cross-frequency coupling, lands that each embody a
   temperament, teaching "**all music theory**" passively, and whether sound and picture can be
   more intertwined than any game has attempted.

It keeps what Morgan liked from the earlier shapes: the 3D world and camera, the wheels, the voices,
combat, and from Volta the loop that spirals. The look is in `look.md`. The listening tests are in
`listen/` (see `listen/README.md`).

## Part 1: for human ears

### The correction

The Volta draft was designed from the arithmetic. Claude can't hear, and a game that wobbles out of
tune for fifteen minutes would wear anyone out. Morgan: "think about what a human would experience
... most of the game sounding out of tune is just going to be so fucking irritating."

### The rules for the ears

- **It sounds good by default.** Chords are tuned pure, so they ring sweeter than a piano. Locking
  in with someone or something makes that ring the reward.
- **Dissonance is a moment, not a place.** It's the tension before a hit lands or a phrase
  resolves, the way film music uses it, and it always goes somewhere.
- **The comma is felt, not heard.**
  - Each day comes back a fifth higher, which sounds like a lift (the key change in a last chorus),
    not like detuning.
  - You learn that you can never come home exactly from the world and the story, not from wobbling
    notes.
  - The few times you actually hear the gap are deliberate, a few seconds long, and earned.
- **Echoes always play in tune with you.** Their age shows in how they look (fading blue) and in the
  part they take (older ones play lower and softer), not in sourness.
- **Whatever you play lands musically.** Like Rez snapping your shots onto the beat, the game keeps
  you in key and in time. The skill is choosing what and when, not hitting a pitch with a
  thumbstick.
- **Room to breathe.** Mostly the world's own sounds, with music blooming when you do something,
  like Breath of the Wild's sparse piano in the field.

Claude can't hear, so before a sound idea is committed to, Morgan's ears judge a short sketch of it
(`listen/`).

## Part 2: the circle is the main thing you use

The circle of fifths is the one tool you use, and the Tears of the Kingdom-style abilities live
inside it.

- **Opening it.** Hold R1: time slows and the wheel opens.
  - Seven wedges are lit: the notes of the key you're in. Five are dark.
  - This is the wasatch dial: seven lit, one home.
- **Picking a mood.** Your home can sit on any of the seven lit wedges. Where it sits is your mode,
  and a mode is a mood.
  - One end of the lit arc is the brightest mood, the other the darkest.
  - Precisely: in a fixed key, home at the flat (counter-clockwise) end of the arc gives Lydian,
    the brightest; home at the sharp end gives Locrian, the darkest.

| mood | mode | power |
|---|---|---|
| brightest, wonder | Lydian | **Lift:** float, rise, levitate things |
| sunny, home | Ionian | **Build:** bind things together |
| bluesy, driving | Mixolydian | **Drive:** make things move (engines, wheels, a dash) |
| cool, soulful | Dorian | **Flow:** slip through, ride water, heal |
| tender, sad | Aeolian | **Remember:** rewind things, call your echoes |
| fierce | Phrygian | **Strike:** force and fire |
| darkest, unstable | Locrian | **Break:** shatter, unmake (rare and dangerous) |

- **Using it.** Flick to a mood and release, and your music changes mood with you, always in key.
  You feel it as wonder or fury, not as theory, and the game never needs the Greek names.
- **The dark wedges** are borrowed chords, the ones film scores borrow for heroic moments. Flicking
  out there is a big, short, costly move: a super.
- **The voice wheel (L1)** picks who sings, and that multiplies everything.
  - Lift with a deep voice raises a boulder; Lift with a high one raises you.
  - Strike with a deep voice is a ground-pound; with a high one, a piercing shot.
  - That's Tears of the Kingdom's "few rules, many combinations".
- **It's also your compass.** The lit arc slides round as you travel (see Part 3).

## Part 3: the world, a ring of lands around a lake

### Is a garden the best place?

- **For home, yes.** Morgan's poems put the garden at the centre ("It's your garden ... the things
  you make and grow", "a garden in my field"), and growing fits a loop: what you carry back each
  night, you plant.
- **For the whole world, probably not the meadow at seed height**, for human reasons:
  - The joy of Tears of the Kingdom is seeing something far off and going there. At seed height,
    grass blocks the view; in the meadow test a tunnel had to be cleared through it just to keep
    the creature visible.
  - Grass is also the most expensive thing that test draws.
  - Small-in-a-garden has been done a lot (Pikmin, Grounded).

### The proposal: a ring of twelve lands around a great lake, with the garden on the shore

- **You can see the whole world.** From anywhere on the rim you see across the water to the far
  shore, the mountains and your garden. You always know where you are, and there's always somewhere
  you want to go.
- **The ring is the circle.**
  - Each land round the rim is the next key.
  - Neighbours share most of their notes, so the music shifts gently as you cross.
  - The far shore is the most different key: a long walk away but a short sail across. That's the
    circle's hidden shortcut (the tritone substitution) turned into a boat ride.
  - The wheel's lit arc points at where you are on the ring: it's your compass.
- **Rivers run home.** Every land's river runs down to the lake, and at the end of the day the river
  carries you home: "together we can make it home safe downstream". That's the loop.
- **The seam is a crack in the rim** where the lake drains away: "a crack in the wall where TIME
  enters".
- **The lake's level decides whether the music can come home**, wasatch's own rule. When the lake is
  low, phrases end on beautiful almost-endings (deceptive cadences): longing, not wrongness. Bring
  the water back and the world resolves.
- **Above and below.** Clouds and rain overhead (the water cycle, a loop that never quite repeats),
  and the lake's mirror beneath.
- **Why water.**
  - Water is everywhere in Morgan's writing: the river, the ocean, the rain, the pool that wants to
    return to the ocean.
  - Two of his favourites, Sunshine and Wind Waker, are water games.
  - Water and sky are also the cheapest beautiful things this chip can draw.

## Part 4: equal temperament and cross-frequency coupling

### What the brain does

The brain runs rhythms at many speeds at once, and the slow ones organise the fast ones
(cross-frequency coupling):

- **Speech.** A 4–8 Hz rhythm follows syllables and a 25–35 Hz rhythm follows the sounds inside
  them, nested together (Giraud and Poeppel's model; its details are argued over).
- **Memory.** One model says short-term memory holds about seven items because about seven fast
  cycles fit inside one slow one (Lisman and Idiart). It's contested; some estimates put capacity
  nearer four. Seven notes in a scale is a fun coincidence to play with, not a claim.
- **Spacing.** Rhythms couple at simple ratios and stay independent at awkward ones. One proposal
  (Pletzer and colleagues, 2010) says the brain's rhythm bands sit a golden ratio apart, the ratio
  least able to lock. Others (Klimesch) argue for doublings, which are octaves.

### Where equal temperament comes in

- **Equal temperament never locks.** Apart from the octave, its intervals are irrational, so they
  never lock: you can go anywhere, but nothing quite rings.
- **Pure ratios do.** They lock and ring, but they can't travel.
- **So:** equal temperament is independence and just intonation is coupling.
- **The sunflower in Morgan's poem** grows by the golden angle, the most irrational turn there is,
  so its seeds never line up and still pack perfectly.

### Coupling as the physics behind the wheel

- **You can only move, lift or calm what you can couple with.** Your note has to sit at a simple
  ratio to it, and the simpler the ratio, the stronger your hold: unison, then octave, fifth, third.
- **Slow rhythms gate fast power** (phase–amplitude coupling, as game feel):
  - A strike lands hardest at the right moment in the beat.
  - A sung note swells on your breath.
  - The day's key frames everything.
- **The world is one ladder of nested rhythms:** the day, the phrase, the beat, the syllable, the
  note, the colour of light, each riding the slower one, the way a brain listens.
- **Love, in these terms:** two rhythms that lock at a simple ratio and stay two.

Fifteen ideas for memory and computation in these rhythms (replay on the night river, pools that
remember, logic in the beat, small pushes that swing giants) are in `memory-in-rhythm.md`.

## Part 5: lands as temperaments, the ring tells the history of tuning

- **Home (the garden): just intonation.** Everything rings. But pure tuning drifts the further you
  travel, so home is perfect and small.
- **Round the rim in either direction, the lands get more tempered, the way history did:**
  - **The fifths land (Pythagorean):** crystalline and open. Its thirds are harsh, so its music
    lives on drones and open fifths, and sounds ancient and strong that way.
  - **The sweet-thirds land (meantone):** lush and warm, gorgeous in nearby keys, with one cursed
    valley where the wolf lives.
  - **The well-tempered lands:** every key has its own colour, gentle near home and tense far away
    (Bach's world).
- **Opposite home, at the seam: the treaty city (equal temperament).**
  - It's built as a bridge over the crack in the rim, where F♯ and G♭ are agreed to be the same
    note: "FAKE THE CLOSURE" made into architecture.
  - The river of time runs under the bridge.
  - Everyone meets there; everything is a little off and everything connects.
  - You can sail straight to it across the lake.
- **Beyond the ring:**
  - **The sky:** the untempered harmonic series. Wind, horns, the blue seventh.
  - **The shimmering grove:** bronze tuned in pairs a few hertz apart so they beat on purpose, the
    way Balinese gamelan does it (the beating is called ombak, "wave"). The one place where wobble
    is the beauty.
  - **The deep:** a land with no octave (Bohlen–Pierce: thirteen steps of a 3:1). Home never comes
    round again; the strangest place.
  - **The islands:** finer equal temperaments (19, 31, 53 notes to the octave), treaties that almost
    work.
- **The ear rule holds.** Each land plays the music that sounds best in its own tuning. Strain shows
  only at borders and in a few deliberate places, like the wolf's valley.
- **Care with real traditions.** The lands are built from tuning mathematics, not costumes of real
  cultures; the songbook credits the traditions.

## Part 6: teaching all of music theory, passively

### How it teaches

- **Do it before you know its name.** The Witness teaches every rule without a word.
- **Names wait to be asked for.** noodles' rule: "the theory lives in the controls".
- **One idea, one look and one sound,** everywhere in the game.
- **The loop brings ideas back in new keys,** the way spaced practice works.
- **Each land introduces one new idea** and recombines the old ones.

### Where each idea is learned by doing

| idea | where you learn it by doing |
|---|---|
| pitch, octave | how high you are; the three layers of the world |
| intervals | the shape drawn between two voices; what you can couple with |
| harmonic series, timbre | the voices and their vowels; the sky |
| consonance and dissonance | the ring versus the wobble; inks in or out of register |
| scales and modes | the seven moods that are your powers |
| keys, the circle of fifths | the ring of lands; the wheel; travel |
| chords and voicing | your echo band; binding things together |
| function and cadence | resolving enemies; doors; the lake coming home |
| modulation, borrowed chords | crossing borders; the dark wedges (your supers) |
| sevenths, ninths, extensions | holding longer for deeper effects (noodles' ladder) |
| rhythm, metre, tempo | the pulse of combat; your heartbeat |
| syncopation, swing | fighting the Grid |
| polyrhythm | lives that meet every twelve days; machines that walk |
| counterpoint and canon | your echoes singing your own tune, offset in time, like a round |
| form | each day as a song |
| transposition, inversion, retrograde | abilities: octave travel, the mirror, rewinding |
| tuning, temperament, commas | the lands themselves |
| acoustics | resonance, standing waves, the Doppler effect, interference: the world's physics |
| notation | the world's old script |

## Part 7: sound and picture as one thing

### Has a game done this?

Parts of it, yes:

- **Rez and Tetris Effect** make sound and picture feel like one sense.
- **Toshio Iwai's Otocky and Electroplankton** make playing into composing.
- **Proteus** is an island whose music comes from what you walk past.
- **Loom** casts spells as melodies, and Zelda's ocarina works time with songs.
- **Wandersong is the closest:** a bard who sings with the right stick on an eight-note colour wheel.
  Its puzzles are mostly copy-the-tune, and reviewers found neighbouring notes hard to hit. That's a
  warning for this game's wheel: big targets, generous aim.

What isn't known to have been done anywhere: a game where sound and picture are the same numbers,
where tuning systems are the physics of places, or one that teaches the whole of music theory.

### What "canonical" has to mean

One model of frequencies, phases and ratios that both sound and picture are drawn from, never two
things matched by hand.

| quantity | heard as | seen as |
|---|---|---|
| pitch class | note | hue |
| octave | register | height and size |
| amplitude | loudness | brightness |
| ratio between two things | interval | the figure drawn between them |
| near-miss between two things | beating | flicker, inks out of register |
| simplicity of a ratio | ring | saturation, inks in register |
| harmonic recipe | timbre, a voice | material, surface |
| phase and coupling | rhythm locking | things moving together |

Sixteen short clips for Morgan's ears, each tied to a question above. The list and questions are in

What was checked by measurement (Claude can't judge the sound itself):

## Not yet connected

Notes on where these ideas don't fit together yet. These are Claude's notes, not decisions.

- **Two wheels.** Shape 1's key wheel (twelve keys, major outside, relative minor inside) and this
  shape's mood wheel (seven lit modes in the current key) are different uses of the circle. Which
  one R1 opens, or how they share it, is open.
- **Lands and keys.** Are the twelve lands twelve keys, with temperaments spanning several lands, or
  is each land one temperament? The gradient from pure home to the treaty city suggests
  temperaments spanning several keys.
- **Volta's calendar and the ring.** Does each day move the lit arc a fifth, and does the ring's
  geography shift with it?
- **What the Grid means now.** Since the game sounds in tune by default, the Grid can't mean "in
  tune". It means sameness: lifeless, unvarying, every day identical.
- **Coupling and combat.** How coupling strength, the beat and the moods combine into numbers.
- **The world's size on this hardware.** The meadow benchmark still hasn't run on the handheld.
