# Experience fixes: what seven players broke, and what changes

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History: fixes to the older thirteen-morning design after seven imagined
> playthroughs (2026-11-14). Most of what it fixes (the eleventh dusk, grey days, ageing past
> selves) belongs to pieces the direction drops or parks. Two of its rules carry on in the
> direction: out of tune is never shown as blur, and at most one line of words. What the game is:
> `design/the-game.md`.

Brainstorm, 2026-11-14. Nothing here is decided. Morgan's ask: "design experience fixes". The input
is `playthroughs/what-they-reveal.md`, the critic's report on seven imagined playthroughs of
`thirteenth-morning.md`. The order below is the report's ranking of flaws, worst first. Two fixes
grew into documents of their own: `the-wheel.md` (nobody used the wheel in a fight) and
`the-way-home.md` (mornings eight to twelve were empty).

The players are imagined, so these are fixes to what the design says, not to how it feels in the
hand. Each one that makes a sound has a listening clip for Morgan's ears.

---

## 1. The eleventh dusk

**What happened.** Five players lost a take they loved with no warning they could read. Kai thought
his first echo was sick and tried to heal it. Walt, a widower, heard a sermon and put the game in a
drawer. Priya found the rule was a timer: her take howled eight fifths from where she sang it, at an
interval that isn't the wolf.

### You watch it rise

- **Echoes are on the wheel.** Each kept take is a blue dot on the wedge of the day it was sung, and
  the ring turns a notch each morning (`the-wheel.md`, "The wheel is a sky").
  - On its third morning the dot sets, and spends five mornings under the wheel.
  - On its eighth morning it rises again at the dark end, and it comes back doubled: two blue dots a
    hair apart.
  - It crosses one wedge a morning. On its twelfth morning, eleven fifths from where it was sung, it
    sits on the wedge beside home.
- **That wedge is the one that leans home.** Everything else there falls a fifth and arrives. This
  one is a comma short and can't. That is the wolf, and you have watched it walk toward your door
  for four mornings.

### It tells you where you are looking

From its eighth morning, the echo itself changes, in the Wolf's own signs:

- **Its outline doubles,** in two crisp inks.
- **At dusk it turns its head toward the water.** A child reads "it wants to go", not "it is ill".
- **At the river, a red glint in its eyes.** The river is the one screen no player skipped.
- **Its tune ends with a second of beating** that falls into the dusk chord. It is brief, and it
  resolves (clip 23).

### It counts mornings travelled

A Repeat doesn't turn the wheel, so it doesn't age an echo. The howl always comes at eleven fifths,
the interval it stands for. Priya's "first number in this game that's decoration" becomes true
arithmetic again.

### It comes to you first

On its eleventh dusk the echo steps in front of Hum and waits one bar. There are three things you
can do.

- **Let it go.** Hold the swell and release on the one. It resolves into one outline and walks into
  the water.
- **Sing it again.** Sing along with it for a phrase. Today's singing becomes one of today's takes,
  a new dot on today's wedge, and the old one goes to the water. It is the same day remembered, a
  little different, the way the vine hums your tunes back. Kai's instinct to heal his echo was
  right, and now it works.
- **Turn away.** It howls for a few seconds and goes south to the Wolf. It can still be let go
  there, as before.

### What you let go is not gone

- **It goes into the water, and the water carries it.**
  - The vine can only hum back what you let go. Keeping everything starves the one who remembers
    you.
  - Any still pool plays a released take for one phrase when you sing the pool its note.
  - On the tenth morning it rains them (`the-way-home.md`).
- **Kept echoes are with you. Released ones are there.** Kept, a take walks and fights beside you
  all day. Released, it is safe for good and can be called up at any pool.

### Jeanne's compass, not Jeanne's line

Walt took "don't ride your luck" to be about the boat. Jeanne says nothing now. Her compass needle
swings to your oldest echo and stays on it.

### Why this answers each of them

| player | what broke | what answers it |
|---|---|---|
| Kai | fading read as sickness; healing failed | it looks toward the water; singing it again works |
| Walt | a sermon about letting go, aimed at a widower | no lesson is delivered: there is a choice at the moment, and letting go is a gift to someone |
| Priya | a timer | mornings travelled, so the interval is true |
| Rosa | pale blue on the bus is no warning | doubling, a red glint at the river, a beat she can hear with her eyes shut |
| Theo | lost his best run | he can sing the run again as today's, and keep that |

**What stays.** The loss is still possible, and the Wolf is still every echo you turned away from.
The diaries show the Wolf giving four of five players their take back to release, and that worked.

## 2. The Grid

**What happened.** Four players were told off for method. Rosa repeated a day because a gate needed
setup. Theo and Ash repeated to beat a boss. Priya repeated to run an experiment. "It reads my
footsteps, not my reasons."

**The change in a sentence.** The Grid is not the same day twice. It is a day nobody was in.

- **Underfoot, as you do it.** On a Repeat, wherever you retrace the earlier telling in step (the
  same place in the same bar), the ground pales under your feet while you watch. Step off the line
  or change your timing and it stops. It reads as a dare to vary, which is what Theo asked for.
- **The river decides.** At night on a Repeat, the river sings both tellings of the day together.
  - Where they match you hear one voice.
  - Where they differ you hear two, in harmony.
  - If the day ended differently in any way (one more creature brought home, a different door, a
    take beaten, a different take kept), all of that day's paling washes out.
  - Only a day that changed nothing sets grey.
- **Greys walk only on set grey.** So the Grid's soldiers appear where you went through the motions,
  and nowhere else.
- **Grey lifts.** Live that day once with a different ending and its grey is gone.

### What follows

- **Experiments are free.** One changed decision is a different ending.
- **Practice that gets anywhere is free.** A best take beaten is a different ending.
- **Setup is free.** Keeping a different take is a different ending.
- **The paling is a tool.** It shows exactly where today matched the earlier telling, which is the
  control Priya wanted.
- **The first Repeat teaches it.** The river sings two voices that night, and the stretches in
  unison drain while you listen (clip 28).

**What stays.** The Grid is still sameness, swing still breaks its soldiers, and its offer on the
thirteenth morning is still there to take.

## 3. Mornings eight to twelve

**What happened.** After the Seam the sun rises over five lands with one voice among them and no
boss. Only the Porch is named in any diary.

**The change.** The second half of the ring is the way home, and it is where the wheel's other half
is learned. Each of the five mornings has one thing to face, and none of them is a boss to beat. The
full design is in `the-way-home.md`. In brief:

| morning | land | what you face | what it gives a home to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | the Aster Steps | a copy of your garden under glass; your voice cracks | Break |
| 9 | Verbena Hollow | the pack, with the vine on the far side of it | the beloved in a fight; chords as triangles |
| 10 | the Rain Steps | a cloud that kept everything | pools that remember; what you let go, come back as rain |
| 11 | the Reed Mouths | a delta singing with no ground under it | your echo band; your one note as the ground |
| 12 | the Porch | the evening before | the ending that doesn't end; the oldest echo at the door |

## 5. The body: Gentle widens the world

**What happened.** Bea fell fourteen times in the Glass Hills and needed her sister's echo to cross.
Gentle widened the beat and not the ledges. Walt's hand ached from holding lock-on. Kai nearly never
found the stick click that the first minute depends on.

- **Landings snap the way notes do.** On Gentle, a jump that is close lands. The game already places
  every note in key and every strike on the beat; this is the same promise kept in space.
- **A sung path.** In wonder, hold R2 toward a ledge your call has touched, and Hum rides the note
  across. Timing replaces steering, which is the skill Bea had from the first minute.
- **The glide opens with a tap** and closes with a tap, and the camera follows where you are going.
  Nothing needs a held face button and the camera stick at once.
- **Lock-on latches.** Tap L2 to lock and to change target; it lets go by itself when nothing is
  near. Holding L2 is only the guard. Both index fingers are free for the clap.
- **Focus by stopping.** Stand still facing a voice for one bar and the camera leans in. Move, and
  it leans out. Leaning in to hear someone is what stopping means. The stick click still works, and
  ignores the stick's wobble for a moment around the click.
- **Either button talks.** When nothing threatens, the right face talks as well as the top one. Rosa
  spent eleven weeks leaping away from people.

## 6. Eyes and ears

**What happened.** Walt spent three nights believing his eyes had gone, because out of tune was
drawn as blur. He took the first figure on faith and needed a magnifier for the one line of text.

- **Out of tune is never blur.** It is two crisp copies in two different inks, sliding against each
  other on the beat, while the colour drains. Failing eyes do not pulse in time. Nothing in the game
  is ever soft-edged to mean something.
- **Type is large.** One line, at most 26 letters, 40 pixels tall, which is 4.4 mm on this panel.
  "Oh. You're in." is fourteen letters.
- **Hum's note is its lantern.** Hum's own hum lights a small circle round it in every night land,
  so the Highway at 2am can be walked by sight as well as by calling.
- **A seeing option:** heavier figure lines and a lifted floor for blacks. It changes nothing else.
- **Nothing essential is in stereo or deep bass.** The first drip's direction is also a ripple on
  the ground. Low voices are already built from upper harmonics.
- **Each well-tempered land plays one phrase in two keys** at a border bell, so a key's colour is
  heard on this speaker and not only measured on Priya's tuner.

## 7. Words

**What happened.** Kai pressed past Box Elder, Puffball and Jeanne, and could not tell the three
offers apart. "It's all words. I press X till they stop."

- **Every line is sung,** in the speaker's vowel, and the tune carries what the words mean. The one
  line of text is a caption for the song.
- **The seven moods are seven answers** (`the-wheel.md`). When someone sings at you, flick a mood
  back and they answer that. Doorstop's one word a day depends on how you greeted him.
- **Box Elder talks too much, and now it is funny.** Answer in any mood and it stops talking and
  sings the tune.
- **Puffball's gossip is drawn.** Its spores hang in the air as a little map of where things are
  today.
- **The three offers are three gestures:** the clap, the step and the hold (`the-wheel.md`, the
  thirteenth morning). The white cat sits at this fork too, and each way sings itself ahead.
- **Drip hums you back in.** After days away, Drip hums the last few minutes in ten seconds, ending
  with Hum facing where it was going. This was Rosa's wish.

**What stays.** Names still wait to be asked for. Kai's own names for everyone (Plop, Twiggy,
Biscuit) are the design working.

## 8. The beloved on the road

**What happened.** Theo was always out fighting and walked past the vine. Six players saw the blue
figure on the pool's edge and none learned who it was.

- **Their triangle has two far corners,** and they are where the fighting is: the Stage on the
  Highway, and Verbena Hollow at the edge of the wolf's valley.
- **You and they share a sun every fourth morning.** They step by thirds and you step by fifths, and
  four of your steps are one of theirs. The arithmetic puts you both under the same sun on mornings
  one, five, nine and thirteen: the garden, the Stage, the Hollow, the garden.
- **The fifth morning, at the Stage.** Under the one red bulb, they sing the third beneath one of
  your cadences, in the middle of a fight. Your band turns warm for the first time, on a stage, with
  your loops stacked behind you.
- **The ninth morning, in the Hollow.** They are across the pack from you (`the-way-home.md`).
- **They hum what you let go** (fix 1).
- **Their point is on the wheel,** doubled until you are in tune with them, so you always know which
  land they are in today.

**Still Morgan's.** Who they are, what they are called, and who sat on the pool's edge. The blank is
working: six players each heard someone of their own.

## 9. The river's choice

**What happened.** Rosa chose her four takes with her eyes shut, let the wrong one walk into the
water, and lost a gate and nine days.

- **The ring floats on the water** with your echoes on it. Hover a take and you hear the chord it
  would make with the ones you are keeping.
- **A take the world has asked for is spoken for.** If a gate you have met wants it, it stands a
  step apart, lit in the gate's colour, and rings the gate's chord when you look at it. It doesn't
  walk into the water unless you send it yourself.
- **The pools are the undo.** A released take can be sung up at the nearest pool for one phrase,
  which is enough to hold a plate or finish a gate's chord.
- **It can be done by ear.** The takes step forward one at a time with the day's song, and you press
  on the ones to keep.

## 10. Two consoles

**What happened.** Ash and Bea's first evenings were "two radios, her key and mine". The treaty was
in the songbook, so nobody found it. Ruben clapped on two and nothing happened. Fights sped one
heart and the couch went out of time.

- **The treaty is carved on the river post** on the first night: two rings touching.
- **The clap is the one, wherever it lands.** Whoever claps sets the downbeat.
- **Treaty fights subdivide the beat** where a fight alone would speed the heart, so two consoles
  stay in step through combat.
- **You sing what you share.** Players on different mornings have different notes. In a treaty each
  game leaves out the notes the other doesn't have. A morning apart you share six notes; three
  mornings apart, four. Straight across the lake you share two, and they are the restless pair, a
  question that stays open until one of you sails over.
- **The cable.** Two units linked by one USB-C cable round-trip in half a millisecond with nothing
  lost (`docs/hardware-target.md`). Over the cable there is one clock and no drift, and each sees
  the other's Hum across the water. So far only the black unit can host.
- **The hush leaves a seat.** In a treaty, clicking the left stick drops your console's top line and
  keeps Drip's time, so whoever is holding it can sing that line out loud. The game still can't hear
  Bea, but it can make room for her.

## 11. The ending's sound

**What happened.** Every diary hears the comma as a slow swell. At middle C a comma beats 3.57 times
a second, and on this speaker low notes are made of upper harmonics that beat faster still. The
canon's central sound may not exist as described, and nobody with ears has checked.

- **Make the beat the pulse.** At 107 beats a minute, a comma at middle C beats exactly in eighth
  notes. Start the two voices together on a downbeat and the wobble is in time: it is the music's
  own pulse, made by the gap.
- **The third voice comes in between,** at the pitch halfway between the two, which is what two
  near-unisons sum to. With it there, the pulse halves and softens into quarter notes: a heartbeat.
  Priya heard "between" and Theo heard "under"; the arithmetic sides with Priya.
- **The fourteenth morning keeps that tempo.** The world's new heartbeat is the gap you chose to
  keep.
- **Morgan's ears decide.** Clip 24 is this. Clip 3 is the same comma with no pulse round it.

## What stays

- You never choose a note, only a moment and a home.
- The bleak endings stay takeable. Six players took one first and came back, and that taught them.
- No screen furniture. Everything new here lives on the wheel or in the world.
- One line of text at most.
