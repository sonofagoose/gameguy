# The wheel in the hand

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History (2026-11-14). The direction keeps from it the four ways home
> (`the-game.md`, "Coming home, four ways"), the walk home the long way, standing where a knot's
> strands meet, and the rule that how far round the wheel a thing sits from home is what it is to
> you. Its seven moods as fixed powers, its ring of twelve that turns each morning, and borrowing
> that falls back after a phrase are not the direction's (`the-game.md`; `the-wormhole.md`, "Outside
> the key is a wormhole").

Brainstorm, 2026-11-14. Nothing here is decided. Morgan's ask, after the seven playthroughs: "use
the wheel ;)". No diary opened the wheel in a fight, nobody used a borrowed chord, and the game the
diaries describe is rhythm, voices and a river (`playthroughs/what-they-reveal.md`). This document
makes the wheel the tool you use for everything, and above all in a fight.

It changes nothing about how the wheel sits under the thumb in `thirteenth-morning.md`: seven moods
that never move, the ring of twelve turning behind them a notch each morning. It adds what the wheel
is for. Its four gestures are the ones Morgan already built in noodles, and its arrow is wasatch's.
The fixes for everything else the diaries broke are in `experience-fixes.md`.

---

## The idea in one breath

- **You can sing one note. The wheel decides where home is.**
- **Everything else has a note of its own.** What it is to you (kin, leaning toward you, warm, far
  away) is how far round the ring it sits from home.
- **So the gesture means one thing everywhere:** where is home? For you, and for whoever is in front
  of you.
- **The poem's two choices are the two wheels.** "We are faced with two choices: WHERE, and HOW HIGH
  home is." R1 is where. L1, the voices from bass to treble, is how high.

### What went missing

Shape 1 had it: "You flick to the key a fifth below theirs, so you're the home they ache for, and
they stumble toward you." Then the wheel became moods inside the day's key, which is right for the
thumbs, and the rule "what happens between two things is the interval between them" stopped reaching
the fights. Priya found it again by herself on day four: "Moods aren't spells. Each wedge is a note,
and a mood is where I put home. THE WHEEL IS A SLIDE RULE." This puts her reading at the centre.

## Five moments

### 1. The first knot: stand where they meet

- **The moment.** Three small knots come at you, stumbling. Each is two strands tugging against each
  other, and you can't find where to hit. Hold R1. Time thickens and the stumble comes apart into
  two slow pulses, one in threes, one in fives. On the wheel each strand is a point, and a thread
  runs from each to the same wedge on the left, where the threads cross. Point there and let go. Now
  both strands land on your heartbeat: they were never stumbling, they were dancing in three and in
  five over a one that nobody was singing. You sing it. Strike on your own pulse and they come
  undone.
- **In the hands.** Hold R1, point left, release. It is the same flick that floated the flowerpot a
  minute earlier, and you have only two wedges awake, so there is nothing to get wrong.
- **Underneath.** The strands sing C and A. Five against three, sped up, is the interval between
  them. Both are overtones of one low note, F (its third and fifth), and F is the wedge on the left.
  The pulse two rhythms share and the root two notes share are the same thing. One reading of "Two
  pins and a generator": two notes, and the root that makes both.
- **How it sounds.** A clatter that turns into a groove when the low pulse comes in under it (clip
  22).

### 2. A pack at dusk: home the long way

- **The moment.** Five of them, three colours, circling. You dodge in the pocket and ring one until
  it drains to grey. It leans. Hold R2: the swell starts, the wheel opens under your thumb, and the
  grey one's point tips toward the wedge on its left. Put home there. You could let go now and it
  would come home. You don't. On the downbeat you nudge the stick one wedge left: it comes home in
  passing, the two whose colour you were standing on go grey, and the swell is bigger. Next
  downbeat, left again. Then let go on the one. All five fold up at once, and the chord that lands
  has every one of them in it.
- **In the hands.** One held finger and three nudges of the thumb, each on a downbeat. No aiming: in
  a swell, a nudge left or right moves home one wedge.
- **Underneath.** Each step is a fifth falling home, and the place you land becomes the next thing
  that leans. It is the strongest motion in tonal music (ii–V–I, and the longer walks jazz calls
  turnarounds). In noodles this is the strum: dragging across wedges.
- **How it sounds.** One chord resolving, then three in a row, then the walk through the whole key
  (clip 18).

### 3. The third morning: borrowing home

- **The moment.** The sun comes up yellow over the Glass Hills. You open the wheel and the green
  wedge has gone: the garden's note set this morning, and it sits just under the bright end, dark.
  Point down at it anyway and let go. For one phrase the world borrows home. The chord opens under
  you like a door somebody left open, asters come up through the crystal round your feet, and
  everything rings the way it did on the first morning. Then it folds back into the day by itself,
  and the return lands like an arrival.
- **In the hands.** The same flick, aimed below the horizon.
- **Underneath.** In D, the chord on C is the flat seventh, one of the borrowed chords popular music
  uses most, and it has always sounded like looking back. Here it is literally yesterday's note.
  Nobody in the diaries used a borrowed chord, because it was a super with a price. The first one
  should be home, and free.
- **How it sounds.** A phrase that steps out of its key for one bar and comes back (clip 20).

### 4. The eighth morning: a green you know

- **The moment.** You have been away a week. At the dark end of the wheel, where new notes rise,
  there is a green you know. Look closely and it is two greens a hair apart: the garden's, and one a
  little warmer. Beside it sits a blue dot, doubled the same way: the take you have carried since
  the first morning.
- **Underneath.** The garden's note left the wheel on the third morning and comes back on the
  eighth, a comma sharp, as the seventh note of the day's key. From here it walks toward home one
  wedge a morning, the way your oldest echo does. The thirteenth morning stops being a twist. You
  watch it come for five days on the tool in your hand.

### 5. The thirteenth morning: the whole turn

- **The moment.** Beside the vine, hold the swell. The wheel opens. Step right on the downbeat, the
  way the mornings went, one land a bar. Each land you set right answers as your thumb passes it:
  glass rings, a bell-flower swings, it rains for a bar. Twelve bars, the whole walk in one breath.
  On the twelfth step you are back where you began, and the wedge under your thumb is doubled: your
  note and the vine's, a comma apart.
- **In the hands.** Three things you have done all game, and each is one of the three offers.
  - **Clap** (L1 and R1), and the two snap to one. Every wedge shifts a hair, the ring closes, and
    it is perfect and grey. That is the Grid's offer.
  - **Step again**, and keep stepping. Each note is pure, each is further, and the lake drains
    behind you. That is the Wolf's.
  - **Hold.** That is the duet.
- **Why.** Kai took his first ending without knowing he had chosen, because the offers were words.
  Here they are the clap, the step and the hold, each carried to its end. The clap is not made a
  villain by this: it is the treaty, and the treaty is good. The Grid's offer is the treaty made
  total and permanent.
- **How it sounds.** Clip 25 is the walk and the arrival. Clip 24 is the hold.

## What is on the wheel

- **Seven lit wedges across the top,** from nine o'clock to three: wonder, sunny home, drive, cool
  soul at the top, tenderness, fierceness, and the darkest at the right. They never move.
- **Five dark wedges below.** These are the notes that are not in today's key.
- **The ring of twelve notes,** each in its own colour, turning one notch each morning behind the
  moods.
- **Points,** for anything with a note. Which ones show depends on what you are doing.
  - In a fight: the creatures near you.
  - Standing still: your echoes, the garden's note, and where the vine and the caravan are today.
  - At the river: your echoes, and the shape a gate has asked for.
  - In focus: the one thing you are looking at.
- **Threads** from home to each point. The figure drawn at the creature itself is the same interval,
  as in `look.md`.
- **The lean.** A grey creature's point tips toward the wedge it wants. This is wasatch's arrow,
  "where the valley is pushing".
- **The star.** The rim joins notes a fifth apart. The star's strings join notes a half step apart.
  Both are ways home (see "Four doors").

### The wheel is a sky

Each morning one note sets at the bright end and a new one rises at the dark end. So every note has
a week on the wheel:

| morning of its week | wedge | what it is that day |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | the darkest | newly risen, restless: the note that leans hardest toward home |
| 2nd | fierceness | |
| 3rd | tenderness | |
| 4th | cool soul | |
| 5th | drive | the note beside home |
| 6th | sunny home | home |
| 7th | wonder | its last and brightest morning |
| then | under the wheel | set for five mornings |

The game begins with the garden's note on its sixth morning. It is home for one day, wonder for one
more, and then it sets.

Your echoes set and rise the same way, as blue dots on the wedge of the day they were sung.
`experience-fixes.md` uses this for the eleventh dusk.

## Four gestures, from noodles

| in noodles | here | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| tap a wedge | hold R1, point, release | **stand:** home moves there on the next beat |
| hold a wedge for its extensions | hold the swell (R2) | **grow:** each bar held adds a colour to the chord, the seventh, then the ninth |
| drag across wedges to strum | in a swell, nudge the stick on a downbeat | **walk:** home moves one wedge, and what you pass comes with you |
| drag the rim to carry home | the morning; Jeanne's compass; after the ending | **carry:** the lit arc itself moves |

Tap R1 without holding is still the call. In a fight the call rings every creature near you in its
own note for a beat, so you can read a pack before you touch it.

## One rule, read off the ring

A creature wears the colour of the land it comes from and sings that land's note. Count wedges from
home to its point:

| where it sits | what it is to you | in a fight |
|---|---|---|
| on your wedge | kin | it keeps your time; its blows turn on your guard |
| one to your right (a fifth above) | it leans toward you | it falls toward you; a cadence brings it home |
| one to your left (a fifth below) | you lean toward it | you fall toward it; a cadence sings it to sleep |
| three or four round | warm | your notes give it colour instead of taking it |
| straight across | far | your blows throw it, and its blows throw you |

Two more, for those who go looking: two wedges round, you pass through each other; five round, along
a string of the star, is the half step.

The short version, for a nine-year-old: **beside it, it comes home. On it, it's with you. Across, it
flies. A third round, it warms.**

Nothing here is needed to land a hit. The fork still rings on the beat whatever home is. The wheel
decides what the hit means, and how the fight ends.

### Each relation has a job

- **Kin gives the unison guard a home.** No diary used the guard. Now there is a reason: be a
  creature's note and the window to turn its blow is wide. Against anything else it stays as tight
  as it was.
- **Across makes room.** When you are surrounded, put the biggest one straight across and your next
  strike sends it over the reeds.
- **Warm is how a Grey comes back.** A Grey has no point on the ring. It has been tempered until it
  is only the land's own note with nothing left over, so it leans nowhere and no cadence touches it.
  Swing still breaks its step. Then stand a third from where it stands and strike: colour comes back
  hit by hit, its point appears, and it is a lost creature again that wants to go home.
- **Beside is how everything ends.**

### Rhythm is the same rule

- **A creature's lunges ride its own pulse,** and its pulse against yours is its interval to home,
  slowed down.
- **Near is simple.** Kin lunges on your beat. A neighbour moves in three against your two. A third
  moves in five against your four. The far side almost never meets you.
- **So standing beside something also untangles its timing.** The pocket opens more often. That is
  what happened with the first knot.
- **Fierce players can do the opposite.** Stay far, read a hard rhythm, and dodge on its pulse
  instead of yours.

## Bringing it home

Hits drain a creature's colour. Grey, it leans. Hold R2: the swell builds and the wheel is open
under your thumb for as long as you hold. There is no need for R1. Release on the downbeat.

### Four doors

Where home is when you let go decides which cadence lands. All of them are in tune. None is a
failure.

| home is | what lands | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| one to its left | the full close | it rings open and transforms; anything on the wedge you landed on goes grey and leans in turn |
| one to its right | the amen | it settles and sleeps; this is Gentle's comfort |
| two to its right | the turn | it lands somewhere it didn't expect, and for one phrase it is kin and fights beside you |
| at the far end of either string | the slide | it comes home by a half step, quietly; nothing else is disturbed |
| anywhere else | a plain ending | it stays grey, a little calmer |

In theory's words these are the authentic, plagal and deceptive cadences, and the leading tone and
tritone substitution. The game never says so.

Because both neighbours work, the floor is low: stand next to it. Which neighbour, and when to use a
string, is the depth.

### Home the long way

- **Step instead of releasing.** On a downbeat, nudge the stick one wedge left. The creature comes
  home in passing, the swell moves on, and anything standing on the wedge you left goes grey and
  joins.
- **A breath is a number of homes.** At first a breath holds three: the classic three-step walk
  home. The first time you land a walk of a given length, your breath grows to hold one more. Growth
  comes from doing, so Imani is not kept on triads for six days.
- **Each step adds a colour** to the chord that finally lands. Three homes is a plain chord, four
  has its seventh, five its ninth.
- **Seven is the whole lit arc,** from the darkest wedge to wonder.
- **The ferry makes eight.** The two ends of the lit arc are straight across from each other, the
  one such pair inside any key. From wonder you can jump to the darkest and keep walking, so a walk
  can leave home, cross every mood and come back. Walt read the far shore as a ferry on day five.
  This is the same ferry, under the thumb.
- **Amen walks go the other way.** Stepping right, each creature is sung to sleep in turn. It is the
  gentle chain, and it is the direction the mornings go.

### What it leaves

The mood you bring a creature home in is the gift it leaves. One rule, not a table per creature:

| brought home in | it leaves |
|---|---|
| wonder | an updraft; it floats where it stood and you can stand on it |
| sunny home | company: it falls into step with you (a wolf becomes a pup that drums) |
| drive | speed: it rolls, and carries you |
| cool soul | water: breath back, steadiness back |
| tenderness | a memory: the nearest pool shows what it was |
| fierceness | sparks: the next creature goes grey at once |
| the darkest | it shatters, and opens what was shut nearby |

Which moods are possible for a given creature depends on where its note sits today. The same wolf
comes home as a drummer one morning and an updraft the next. Fights have a date in them, as Priya
found puzzles do.

## Under the wheel: borrowing

The five dark wedges are the notes that have set. Borrowing is reaching under the horizon.

| dark wedge | which note | on the first morning |
|---|---|---|
| eight o'clock | the one that set this morning | the Reed Mouths |
| seven | set two mornings ago | the Rain Steps |
| six | three ago | Verbena Hollow |
| five | four ago | the Aster Steps |
| four o'clock | the one that rises tomorrow | the Seam |

- **What it does.** Home visits that note for one phrase. Everything relates to you from there, and
  the land comes with it: borrow the Rain Steps and it rains. Then home returns by itself, and the
  return rings.
- **What it costs.** The breath of that phrase, and nothing else. It needs a breath of four. The
  first borrow, home on the third morning, is free.
- **What it is for.**
  - **Reach.** A creature whose home has set can only be brought home by going there. Wolves come up
    from under the wheel at dusk.
  - **Weather.** Each land brings one thing: rain, a ringing ridge, a bell-flower's swing, 2am.
  - **Longer walks.** A walk can step off the end of the lit arc and keep falling into yesterday.
  - **Tomorrow.** The four o'clock wedge is the note that rises in the morning. Borrowing it sounds
    like the sky getting light.
- **Later in the journey the garden is down there too.** From the third morning to the seventh, home
  is a thing you borrow.

## One note, seven homes

Hum's one note is the day's note, and it never changes. What changes is what it is. This is why the
powers are what they are:

| mood | your note is home's… | so it… | power |
|---|---|---|---|
| wonder | fifth | sits above home and floats | Lift |
| sunny home | own note | holds | Bind |
| drive | fourth | hangs, wanting to move on | Drive |
| cool soul | seventh | slides over the top | Flow |
| tenderness | minor third | is small and close | Remember |
| fierceness | minor sixth | aches | Strike |
| the darkest | half step above | grinds against home; the voice cracks | Break |

Clip 26 holds one note while home walks under it. If the seven sound like seven feelings about the
same note, the mapping is earned. If they don't, it is arbitrary and should change.

## The wheel everywhere else

- **Walking.** At a border one wedge changes colour. The lake map in the songbook is the wheel at
  the size of the world.
- **Things.** As in the canon: kin is weightless, a neighbour falls, across repels. The boulder that
  slid toward Priya on the D morning is kin the next day.
- **Talking.** The seven moods are your seven answers. When someone sings a line at you, flick a
  mood back and they answer that. Nothing to read (see `experience-fixes.md`).
- **The river.** The ring floats on the water with your echoes on it. Choosing which to keep is
  choosing which dots stay lit.
- **The vine and the caravan.** Each is a point on the ring at the land they are in today, so the
  wheel is also the schedule.
- **Two consoles.** The treaty is two wheels pointed at one wedge.
- **After the ending.** The lands past the lake are tuned by thirds. The wheel gains its second
  direction: a half push of the stick steps by a third, where a full push steps by a fifth.

## Gentle and fierce

- **Gentle leans for you.** Hold the swell without steering and, after one bar, home drifts beside
  the grey creature on its amen side. A child never needs the stick, sees where home went, and can
  take over when ready.
- **Fierce asks for the full close,** rewards long walks, and brings packs whose notes are scattered
  so the path has to be found.
- **A strike you time yourself rings fuller** than one the game placed: the same note with its
  octave and a brighter light. Four players took the first fight for the game playing itself. Now
  the ceiling is audible in the first minute (clip 27).

## For the hands

- **Sticks on this device** report about 57 times a second, with zero resting noise and full range
  (`docs/hardware-target.md`).
- **Wedges are thirty degrees.** Wandersong's eight-note wheel, at forty-five, drew complaints about
  hitting neighbours. So:
  - the stick snaps and holds the last wedge until it is clearly in another;
  - hovering changes the music at once, so the ear confirms before the release commits;
  - in a swell there is no aiming at all, only nudges.
- **Fingers.** R1 and the stick, or R2 and the stick: an index finger and a thumb. Never R1 and R2
  together. The clap needs both index fingers, so lock-on latches (see `experience-fixes.md`).
- **Not by colour alone.** A point's place on the ring, its throb and its sound all say the same
  thing.
- **On the screen.** The ring sits round Hum in the lower third. One-pixel lines read as pen lines
  at this density.

## What to test first

1. **In the ears.** Clips 17 to 31 in `listen/`. Each asks one question.
2. **In the hands.** The toy in `toys/wheel/`: the dark, Drip, the wheel, three knots, then three
   creatures on a run of fifths. A scripted player has run it through on the black unit; nobody
   has played it by hand yet. It should answer:
   - Does standing where they meet feel like finding something?
   - Does a nudge on the downbeat feel like power?
   - Can thirty-degree wedges be hit on these sticks without looking?
   - Does the picture stay readable when every creature's rhythm changes as home moves?

## Not yet connected

- **Is it a chore?** Games that colour-code enemies to stances can feel like sorting. The guards
  here: every hit works whatever home is, either neighbour ends a fight, and Gentle leans for you.
  Only hands can say whether that is enough.
- **Rhythm that changes with home.** It follows from the rule, and it may be too much to read in a
  fight. The fallback is to keep each creature's rhythm fixed and let the wheel change only what
  cadences do.
- **Where notes come from.** "A creature sings its land's note" is simple. Whether packs want mixed
  notes, and how, is open.
- **The gifts** need one design per mood that works on every creature.
- **The breath.** One breath pays for the wheel, the swell, sprint and glide. How a long walk
  competes with a dodge is unbalanced.
- **The clap as the Grid's gesture.** It may read as the game accusing its own best move.
- **Your band's shapes.** If your own echoes can only sing roots, fifths and ninths (so the third
  always comes from someone else), gates can only ask for open shapes. Whether that is enough for
  good gate puzzles is untested.
