# The wormhole

_Part three of `the-game.md`, written 2026-11-25 by Claude. Morgan: "my absolute favorite teacher
I've ever watched is Jaron Lopez, he talks about the 'wormhole' method and does the 'chords that
vibe' serious and much more.. how much can you gain with this knowledge and where might it apply in
a elegant way that compounds?" He sent a frame from one episode: C minor 11 and E♭ minor 11 on a
piano roll, each note marked with what it is in its chord._

_I did not know this teaching, so I looked. I read the machine-made captions of seven of his
episodes: the Wormhole Method and its second episode, the 6 Places a Chord Can Go, Shapeshifter
Notes, Twin Dominant Chords, Altered Chords, and How to Make Chords Melodic. I cannot hear or see
the videos, and captions get names wrong, so what follows is my reading of his words. The ideas
credited to him are his. What the design does with them is my call, and Morgan's to change or
strike._

## What he teaches

- **Normal chords stay in one key.** He means above all I, IV, V and vi. He likes them and finds
  they end up "hypnotizing me into a certain mood".
- **The wormhole method.** Take one chord and go to any other. "We're not worried about key. We're
  not worried about scale." "We just teleport from one place to another."
- **There are only six places a chord can go.** A root can move by one to six half steps. Up and
  down by the same amount are "twins, but not identical twins", because each is the other upside
  down. Six half steps is the one that lands in the same place either way.
- **The root is who a chord is.** "The root is the core identity of a chord", and the kind of chord
  is "just the costume the root is wearing". So a progression is first of all how its roots move.
- **Three tools weld any two chords together.**
  - A common tone, a note both chords have. He calls it "your harmonic and melodic hovercraft", and
    in one example "an unwavering laser beam through the harmony, refusing to budge".
  - Contrary motion. Notes that move the same way are heard "like a school of fish". Notes that move
    against each other are "unique characters with opposing motivations".
  - A counter melody, low down: "the main melody's top advisor".
- **Shapeshifter notes.** "Do your notes shapeshift?" A note held across a change of chord becomes a
  different part of the new chord. He ranks the parts on "the vertical harmonic elevator": the root
  is the ground floor, then 3, 5, 7, 9 and 11. "The higher the floor, the more colorful the note."
- **Voicing is where a chord becomes yours.** The ear takes the top note as the tune, so he chooses
  top notes first. He packs neighbouring notes into clusters, and puts two notes a half step apart
  inside a chord for tension. He likes stacks of fourths and fifths.
- **Dominant chords come in pairs.** "Dominant chords never show up alone. They arrive as twins": G7
  and D♭7 hold the same two restless notes, B and F, with their jobs swapped.
- **Altered notes are home reaching in.** The sharp and flat fives and nines on a dominant chord are
  usually notes of the home scale. He hears them as the chord "trying to get closer to home before
  you actually resolve".
- **Then it gets a groove.** Chords land on the "and" of four. Quiet notes are "felt more than
  heard". Silence is "a creative tool".
- **And it gets varied.** The ear is a pattern detector: an exact repeat lets it stop listening, and
  one small change wakes it again. Of the two kinds of progression he says the chromatic "becomes
  increasingly unsettling" and the diatonic "increasingly predictable", and that each is the cure
  for the other.

## What it changes

In order of size.

### 1. Outside the key is a wormhole. It is not a loan.

- **Part one had it wrong.** Rule 7 said a chord outside the day's key "costs breath, it lasts a
  phrase, and it always falls back into the key". That is the classroom's view of chromatic chords,
  and it is the view his whole method exists to leave behind.
- **The new rule.** Point the wheel at any of the twenty-four cells and go there, and stay as long
  as you like. Nothing has to fall back.
- **Why it still sounds like your day.** Hum never lets go of his note. He is the shapeshifter, the
  hovercraft, the laser beam that refuses to budge. In the frame Morgan sent, the top B♭ is the
  seventh of one chord and becomes the fifth of the next. That B♭ is what Hum is.
- **What comes with you through the hole** is whatever the two chords share. That was already the
  rule for footing in part two. His method says to use it everywhere, with no key required.
- **The triggers and the wheel now have different jobs.** L1 and L2 walk the day's own ring, the
  normal chords. The wheel on R1 is the wormhole. His own image for it is "throwing a wrench into
  the spokes of this harmonic wheel".
- **And the day wants both.** A game that only walked its ring would hypnotize in exactly the way he
  describes. One that only teleported would unsettle. The ring is where home is. The wormhole is
  what keeps the ear awake.

### 2. The elevator is a better rule for Hum's body than my table

- **His idea.** A held note sits on some floor of the chord under it, and the higher the floor the
  more colour it has.
- **What the wheel adds.** With Hum's note fixed, the floor depends only on which cell is under him,
  and it goes in order. Going flatward from home his note is the 1, then the 5, the 9, the 13. Going
  sharpward it is the 11, then the low 7, the low 3, the low 13, the low 9.
- **So the lift is not one chord.** It is the first stop of an elevator that keeps rising for three
  cells flatward. The lean is the first stop of an arm that keeps tightening sharpward, into the
  altered notes he describes as home reaching in.
- **The candidate rule.** Flatward, Hum gets lighter and more vivid a floor at a time. Sharpward, he
  hangs, then pulls harder. That is one dial for all twelve roots, where part one had six separate
  words. I would try it before keeping my table.
- **Sketch l is this for the ears:** one note on top that never moves while the roots go flatward
  under it, so it is the 1, the 5, the 9, the 13, the 3 and the 7 in turn.

### 3. A wide chord is a band of colour, and the bridge can be seen

- **His favourite chord is six cells in a row.** The notes of C minor 11 are E♭, B♭, F, C, G and D,
  which are six neighbours on the wheel.
- **So moving it keeps what still overlaps.** Slide a band of six by some number of cells and it
  keeps six less that number. Every example in his episodes comes out this way:

| he goes from C minor 11 to | cells apart | notes kept | the ones he names |
|---|---|---|---|
| B♭ minor 11 | 2 | 4 | C, E♭, B♭, F |
| E♭ minor 11 | 3 | 3 | E♭, B♭, F |
| A♭ minor 11 | 4 | 2 | E♭, B♭ |
| D♭ minor 11 | 5 | 1 | E♭ |

- **In this game colours go round the wheel,** so that chord lights a smooth band of six
  neighbouring colours, and a plain triad lights two neighbours and one far colour. Before you jump,
  the cells you would keep are the colours the two bands share. The bridge is on the screen.
- **It grows with Hum.** The wider his chord, the more it overlaps with far places, so the more
  holes are open. His voice opening is what opens the map.
- **The first hour already has his sound.** Part one starts with open fifths because Morgan's ears
  called them ancient and strong (by my reading of his misfiled notes). A stack of fifths or fourths
  is also one of his favourite textures. So there are two ways for a chord to grow: round the wheel,
  by stacking fifths into wider bands, and up, by opening the overtones. The first needs no third at
  all.

### 4. Root, costume and voicing are the three hands

- **Root motion is where:** the triggers and the wheel.
- **Costume is how far Hum's breath has opened:** the swell on R2, a floor a bar.
- **Voicing is who sings on top and who sings underneath:** the D-pad, which part two called the
  poem's "HOW HIGH".
- **The top note is the tune.** Whoever sings highest is what the ear follows. Keep that line in
  home's notes while the chords teleport underneath, and, as he puts it, the teleport folds into the
  background.

### 5. One new tool: the counter melody

Part two had common tones and contrary motion and did not have this. It belongs to the bass voice in
the band: a second line, low down, that walks from one chord's note to the next and answers the tune
on top.

### 6. His grooves are the vocabulary for "When"

- **The change on the "and" of four is the lean, in time.** Part two said that acting a hair before
  the beat leans. His chords arrive an eighth early and pull the next bar toward them, and the kick
  moves with them.
- **Notes "felt more than heard".** The triggers on this handheld are on or off, so loudness has to
  come from something else: how long a press is held, and where in the beat it falls.
- **The last sixteenth of the bar** is where he would put a quiet note if he could add only one,
  because it carries the loop round.
- **Silence.** He leaves a gap at the end of a chord on purpose. Part two's rests giving breath back
  is the same idea.

### 7. The night's eight bars must not be an exact repeat

His point about the pattern detector applies to what the river sings back and to everything the
world plays by itself. Two bars become four by one small change, and four become eight by another.

## Why it compounds

Each of these makes the others bigger. A wider voice makes a wider band. A wider band shares more
with far cells, so more holes open. Every hole puts Hum's note on a new floor, so every place he
reaches changes what he is. And each new friend in the band is another voice for the top line or the
counter melody that makes the jump sound meant. One held note is what all of it turns on, and that
note was already the centre of the game.

His stated aim for his own tools is that "when your musical tools know the rules, all you have to do
is break them". That is also a fair description of what this game is for.

## The arithmetic

Checked by script on 2026-11-25.

- **His six places and the wheel.** A root motion of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 half steps is 5, 2, 3, 4, 1
  or 6 cells round the wheel. Up and down are the two directions round.
- **Minor 11 chords.** C, E♭, G, B♭, D and F are the cells from three flatward of C to two
  sharpward. Two such chords whose roots are n cells apart share 6 less n notes: 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 and
  none.
- **His four examples.** With C minor 11: B♭ minor 11 shares C, E♭, F and B♭. E♭ minor 11 shares E♭,
  F and B♭. A♭ minor 11 shares E♭ and B♭. D♭ minor 11 shares E♭.
- **Hum's C over each root.** Flatward: C the 1, F the 5, B♭ the 9, E♭ the 13, A♭ the 3, D♭ the 7,
  G♭ the raised 11. Sharpward: G the 11, D the low 7, A the low 3, E the low 13, B the low 9.
- **The twins.** G7 has B as its third and F as its seventh. D♭7 has F as its third and B, written
  C♭, as its seventh.
- **The frame Morgan sent.** C minor 11 voiced C, G, B♭, D, E♭, F, B♭, then E♭ minor 11 voiced E♭,
  A♭, B♭, D♭, F, G♭, B♭. Both B♭s and the F stay. D and E♭ are a half step apart in the first, and F
  and G♭ in the second.
