## What the audit's tensions point at

I read the audit as a map of things that cannot all be true at once. Its "winner" lines measure
against an order of files that Morgan never held. Two notes on its facts:

- **Its theory item 6 comes to the wrong conclusion.** It says eleven fifths is not the wolf. Eleven
  pure fifths fold to 521.505 cents, and the Pythagorean wolf fifth is 678.495 cents. They add up to
  1200: one is the other turned upside down. So an echo eleven fifths behind you does make the wolf
  with you, as the old Volta table said. (C up to E sharp is also an augmented third, not an
  augmented fourth.) The sweet-thirds wolf, 737.637 cents, is a different animal, as the audit says.
- **Its eleven "missed mechanics" are mostly one thing.** The D that cannot please both its
  neighbours, the wolf you could place, and the three ways a major chord turns minor are all views
  of the grid that fifths and thirds make together. Shape 1 named that grid on the first day, and it
  was then put off until after the ending.

### 1. The two wheels are one wheel

The audit's sharpest contradiction is that the wheel is two things: seven moods inside a key
(`the-wheel.md`), and twelve keys with their relative minors (shape 1, and the wheel as now built).
The build shows twelve major letters over a model that has seven homes, so the letter D plays D
minor.

They are the same wheel. Put the seven homes of C's notes on the ordinary two-ring circle of fifths:

| home | its mode | its chord | where it sits | the mood's name so far |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F | Lydian | major | outer ring, one to the left | wonder |
| C | Ionian | major | outer ring, here | sunny home |
| G | Mixolydian | major | outer ring, one to the right | drive |
| D | Dorian | minor | inner ring, under F | cool soul |
| A | Aeolian | minor | inner ring, under C | tenderness |
| E | Phrygian | minor | inner ring, under G | fierceness |
| B | Locrian | diminished | the thin edge past E minor | the darkest |

Three bright cells on the outside, the three under them, and an edge. That is how noodles lights a
key, as the first brainstorm describes it: IV, I and V on the outside, ii, vi and iii beneath them,
and the diminished chord as a thin wedge "living on the sector's edge". And it is wasatch's rule:
the notes in play are seven places on the circle, and home is which of them you stand on.

- **Proposal: light the wheel as noodles lights it.** Under the thumb that is three directions and
  two depths, which is the half push Morgan already asked for (`SCRATCH.md`). The moods he confirmed
  by ear stay as they are; only their places change. The letters become true.
- **The same picture is the map.** If the lands are the outer ring and what is underneath is the
  inner ring, then where you stand is which cells are lit. "The actual entire world is a gigantic
  circle of fifths" (Morgan, `SCRATCH.md`) becomes literal, and the stick at rest is the middle of
  the lake. A picture of it was drawn for Morgan with this note and is not in the repository; in
  letters, standing in the Garden:

```
      by day, on the shore:      F     C     G
      at night, underneath:      Dm    Am    Em    B°
                                    the island
```

- **Walking is changing key.** Cross into the next land and the lit patch slides one cell. One note
  changes (F becomes F sharp), and what was "drive" from the Garden is "sunny home" once you are
  there. A mood is then a lean toward a neighbouring place and hour: wonder is the Porch's home
  heard through the Garden's notes.
- **The patch is an instrument on the first evening.** With two cells awake you already have home
  and the amen. With three you have the three chords that carry most songs. This is where
  "performance from the first evening" could live: you never pick a bare pitch, and you always pick
  a home. Nothing can sound wrong, and everything is chosen.

It would have to be true in the hands that six cells and an edge can be played without looking. That
is experiment 2.

### 2. Underneath

Morgan's newest thread puts each major land's relative minor underneath it. The audit is right that
the old deep (the mirror) is a different thing, and it worries about 24 worlds. Three things already
in the notebook say what underneath could be.

- **A relative minor is the same notes with another home.** Morgan's style book says "the car at 2am
  and the garden at noon are the same figure, seen at different hours". The atlas already gives
  every land a noon face and a 2am face.
- **`the-wheel.md` already made that home the one that remembers:** tenderness, home on the sixth,
  the power called Remember.
- **The river already sings the day back.**

**Proposal: night is the relative minor of the day.** Underneath is the same land heard from its
other home. The river carries you down through it, and its song is your day with home moved a pure
minor third lower (5/3 against 2, 315.641 cents down). That makes travel, sleep and dream one trip,
which is what Morgan described, and it needs no second set of lands. Whether it works is only a
question for ears: does the same tune over the lower home sound like the day remembered? That is
sketch b.

Two true things come with it, for later:

- **Underneath is a real direction, and it disagrees with round.** The A a pure minor third under
  the Garden's C is 5/3. The A three lands round the shore is 27/16. They are 21.506 cents apart
  (81/80, the other comma). So the inner ring is not the outer ring's notes again: it is its own
  ring, lower by a hair.
- **There are three ways down, and each moves one voice.** In place: C major to A minor, G rises to
  A. To the right: C major to E minor, the Alfalfa's night, C falls to B. Through the mirror: C
  major to C minor, E falls to E flat, and that is the night of the land three round to the left.
  The mirror does turn C's chord over into C minor, so the Mirror Pool could be that third door. The
  audit's "four different structures" would be three doors and one drawer (the country with no
  octave).

### 3. The comma is not a date, and not a monster

The notebook has the comma arrive on the thirteenth morning, and it gives the two ways of living
with it two villains and three endings. The audit says this flattens temperament into moral
opposites, and I agree. Three proposals take its place.

**a. Heard as silence. Home stops answering.**

Law 1 says how much two notes share: all of it when they match, 98% at 2 cents, under a tenth at a
comma (`spec/laws.md`; the width of 15 cents is a number I chose, to tune). So walk the whole shore
by pure fifths, true at every step, carrying your own tuning and taking no step down at the Seam,
and you come back to the Garden 23.460 cents warm. Call, as you did on the first evening. The garden
answers at 8.7%. It stays almost dark.

That is the first verb of the game turned over, and it is Morgan's own line from The Generative
Self: "I keep missing home / by something I can't measure". No sound in it is out of tune. The comma
is heard as an absence, which may be the answer to the held comma that is "really close" and not yet
landing.

Come down half the comma and the garden answers at 54%. If someone else comes up half, the two of
you are in tune with each other, and each of you is half lit to your own. The poem's "meet in the
middle" then has numbers. That is sketch a.

This needs no calendar. It happens whenever a player closes a lap, in either direction, and a lap
one way undoes a lap the other.

**b. Felt as work. The remainder is an engine.**

Take the oldest turnaround there is: C, A minor, D minor, G, and home. Tune every chord pure, and
let each keep a note of the one before. Home comes back at 80/81, which is 21.506 cents low. Every
chord was in tune, and you sank. Four laps is 86.025 cents, nearly a half step. Play it the other
way round (C, G, D minor, A minor, C) and you rise by the same amount. On the treaty's notes the
same four chords come back exactly, and nothing moves.

So a circuit of pure chords is a ratchet. In a world where height is pitch, it is a pump. The poem
says "ache IS the engine", and The Generative Self says "A closed system has nowhere to go". Here
both are literal: the pure circuit does work because it does not close, and the tempered one closes
and does none.

- **Different circuits are different gears** (heights at 100 m to the octave, the notebook's number,
  to tune):

| circuit | steps | each lap moves home | as height |
|---|---|---|---|
| eight fifths down, then a pure third down | 9 | 1.954 cents down | 0.16 m |
| C, A minor, D minor, G, each keeping a note of the last | 4 | 21.506 cents down | 1.79 m |
| the whole shore by fifths | 12 | 23.460 cents up | 1.96 m |
| three pure major thirds up | 3 | 41.059 cents down | 3.42 m |
| four pure minor thirds up | 4 | 62.565 cents up | 5.21 m |

- **On the wheel, the turnaround is a small loop of the thumb:** here, under, under and left, over
  and right, here. It is a crank. Turn it one way and the thing you are holding sinks; the other way
  and it rises.
- **It keeps the ear rule.** Each chord rings pure. Only where you end up has moved.
- **The human moment** is doing the same loving thing every day, in tune at every step, and finding
  yourself somewhere else.

Sketch c is the turnaround, four laps pure and two on the treaty's notes. Experiment 4 is the crank
in the hand.

**c. The treaty is ground, not a villain.**

Pure ground rings fully, and it carries you off. Treaty ground answers everything a little less (98%
on a fifth, 43% on a third) and keeps you level, so everyone can meet on it. Both are tools. Neither
is a sin. Which one you stand on can be chosen step by step: at a border, step pure and keep the
ring, or give two cents and stay home.

### 4. The island, and the ice

The atlas has no island; the audit says so. But the poem puts the treaty in the middle: equal
temperament is "a 'center' of the space", and "One surface, upon which all agree and the blooming
people can meet in the middle".

- **Proposal: the island is the middle of the wheel.** It is where the stick rests, the same
  distance from every land, and the one place where all twelve lands' music can sound together, a
  little dulled. That would make it the place where what you keep from each land can be played as
  one piece, which is what the last freedom, composing, needs. The Seam can stay on the far shore as
  the place where the shore's two ends miss.
- **The ice is Morgan's image, and I do not know what it is to him.** Two readings, both proposals.
  As a thing to do: ice is a plate, and a plate shows still lines when it is sung to, so the frozen
  lake is the puddle bridge at the size of the world, and the way across depends on what you sing.
  (At the puddle he trusted the lines and then asked "what's this even for?" This would be what it
  is for.) As what it means: the frozen part of the lake repeats exactly, the dry part forgets, and
  the flowing part remembers and changes. A lake that is low and partly frozen then holds both of
  the notebook's dangers at once: nothing comes home, and nothing changes.

### 5. Depth as one law, not a list

The audit asks whether 96 lives and 288 endings are a checklist under another name. Partly they are.
Many of the lives are good because the help they want is something music does: a steady pulse, the
note two rhythms share, a third from someone else. Others are errands (the sock, the clapper).

**Proposal: the depth is his sister's sentence, as a law. The world keeps what you play.** Anything
that can ring keeps the rhythm of what reached it for a while, and gives it back in its own voice,
in tune with home. Move home and everything you have laid down changes chord with you. How long
things remember is the lake's level, which the notebook already says ("a low lake forgets"). The
long shape of the game is then silence becoming song: nothing remembers at first, and at the end the
whole world is singing what you gave it, and you go round harmonizing. A knot is a memory that got
tangled.

It would also make the wheel's effect heard everywhere, which is what his eight minutes with the
tangles were missing. Whether a garden full of your own bars is a band or a mess is experiment 3.

### 6. Put together

One paragraph, all of it a proposal:

You wake in the dark with one note. You call, something answers, you fall into step with it, and the
page stands up. From then on the world is an instrument that keeps what you play. It is laid out as
the wheel in your hand, so where you stand is which chords you have, night is the same land heard
from underneath, and walking is changing key one note at a time. Pure ground rings and carries you
off. The middle of the lake is where everyone can meet, a little dulled. Walk all the way round,
true at every step, and your own home stops answering you, by something you can't measure. What you
do about that, alone or with someone, is the ending. After it you can put home anywhere.

That last line is the poem's two choices as the last freedom: WHERE home is, and HOW HIGH. If the
lands are tuned as a chain of fifths from wherever home is, then moving home moves the seam.

## The game in one paragraph

You are a small thing with one note. The world is laid out as the wheel in your hand: an island in
the middle of a low, half-frozen lake, twelve lands round the shore under the sky, and under each
land the same land in the dark, with home a third lower. Everything in it can ring, and keeps what
you play. You make music from the first minute, by moving, calling, singing and striking in time,
and by choosing what is underneath. Your one note opens upward into its own overtones as you meet
others, and each new overtone is a new note to play, a new rhythm to feel and a new way to go
somewhere. Each day you go out to a land. Each night its river carries you under the ground to the
island, and what you played is set into the island's song. Bringing a land's river back joins it to
the island: its chord comes under your thumb wherever you are, its people come to the middle, the
lake rises, and more of the world remembers. When the song can go all the way round, it comes home a
comma high, as it must. What the world does with that remainder, where it is warm, where it is
frozen, and who carries the difference, has been yours to arrange the whole time.

## Seven moments, as played

Each has the moment first, then what the hands do, then what is true underneath it.

### 1. The Garden's first day

- **The moment.** Morning, and nothing is asked of you yet. You strike an aster's stalk, twice and
  then once, and it keeps that in its bells, a bar long, over and over. You call at the reeds in the
  gap and they keep the call. You run, and land on a leaf on the one. Then you hold the wheel and
  put home on the cell to the left, and everything you set going changes chord together. You have
  made something, and nobody asked for it.
- **In the hands.** A strike, a call, a jump, and R1.
- **Underneath.** While the lake is low a thing keeps what you give it for a bar or two. Home
  decides which of its notes it uses.

### 2. The first night

- **The moment.** Dusk. The hose runs, the pool is full, and where it spills over the old shore the
  water goes into the ground. Drip's tick slows. You lie down in the stream and it takes you under.
  It is black, and then your own day comes back to you off the walls: the calls, the strikes, the
  three notes with Drip, folded into eight bars, with a lower note under them than they had all day.
  It sounds like someone who was there, telling it. Roots hang in the dark and ring as you pass.
  Near the end there is a phrase you never played. Then grey light. You are lying on a mound in the
  middle of a frozen lake, and your eight bars are playing, very quietly, from a post on its shore.
  Eleven other posts are dark.
- **In the hands.** Nothing is asked. You can hum along, and what you hum is in the song tomorrow.
- **Underneath.** The same notes, with home a pure minor third lower (315.641 cents). One note of

### 3. Morning on the island

- **In the hands.** Running and sliding, a strike, a call. Nothing to solve.
- **Underneath.** The ledge is twelve pure fifths against seven octaves: 23.460 cents, which is

### 4. Underneath, awake

### Three ways tuning is felt

1. **As how much the world answers you.** Law 1: all of it when two notes match, 98.3% at two cents,
   8.7% at a comma (the width of 15 cents is my number, to tune). A mismatch is heard as dimness and
   quiet, not as beating. This is part one's 3a.
2. **As drift that does work.** A pure lap that doesn't close moves things: part one's 3b, and above
   it is what moves water.
3. **As warm and cold.** This is the new part.

### One dial: the size of the fifth

### Frozen into the ground, or carried between people

- **The remainder can sit in the ground.** That is a treaty: ice.
- **Or it can be carried live.** Singers who listen to each other keep every chord pure and take up
  the comma in the moving notes, a hair at a time. No fixed tuning does that. Things that cannot
  listen need the ice. (General knowledge: choirs and string quartets tune this way, and the drift
  that comes when nobody takes the comma up was written about in the sixteenth century. To be
  checked before anything is built on it.)
- **So the treaty is for strangers, and for things that can't listen.** With someone who is
  listening you don't need it. You carry the difference between you. The poem: "through all we keep
  between us, our gravity, I pull".
- **The old duet ending is this, as a skill.** And who takes the comma on a given lap is something
  two people can pass back and forth.

### Wolves, the frozen and the old endings

- **A wolf is not a kind of creature. It is a place:** the border that holds too much. Where the
  remainder is, something wants.
- **The frozen** are what stopped listening (the old Greys). They want nothing, so nothing brings
  them home until someone gives them a third.
- **The three old endings are three places in one space.** Everything even, with nobody listening.
  Everything on one border, with nobody sharing. And the difference carried between two.
- **None of them is an enemy in a small amount.** Ice is how strangers meet. In the cold placing,

- **How much of this a player must understand:** none of the arithmetic. They hear how much the
  world answers. They feel drift. They see and feel how warm or cold a border is. They make a trade.

## Five fights, as played

### 1. The first knot

- **The moment.** After a quiet morning you follow the hose toward where it is stopped, and
  something stumbles out of the reeds: two strands wound round each other, each tugging at its own
  rate. It lashes when you come near, and you can't find when. You hold L2. Drip hops off your head
  onto it and ticks what it hears there, and that is a mess too. You hold R1. Time thickens, the
  garden lies down, and the mess comes apart into two pulses, one in threes and one in fives. A
  thread runs from Drip to one cell, the one on the left. You put home there. A low note comes in on
  the one, and the mess is a groove: three and five, over a one that nobody was singing until you
  did. Now its lash lands on a beat you can feel. You step aside on it and time opens. You strike on
  the one, and again, each strike the next note of the chord. It loses its colour and leans. You
  hold R2 and let go on the one. It springs open into a loop of hose, and water runs.
- **In the hands.** L2, R1 and a flick, a dodge, three strikes, R2 and a release. Each is one press,
  and each has been done once already with nothing at stake.
- **Underneath.** The strands sing C and A: five against three. Both are overtones of F, its third
  and its fifth, and F is the cell on the left. The pulse two rhythms share and the root two notes
  share are one thing. This is clip 22, as a fight (sketch i).

### 2. A pack in the Alfalfa

- **The moment.** Three small things from the field circle you, and behind them a big one. You call:
  the three ring one note, and the big one rings the note above it. The small ones keep falling in
  on you. Your fork goes straight through the big one. You hold R1 and flick right. Everything on
  the screen lurches. The three small ones are with you now: they have fallen into your step, their
  lunges land on your beat and turn on your guard, and you could pick one up as if it weighed
  nothing. The big one, which could not be touched, starts to fall toward you. You let it come, step
  aside on its beat, and ring it until it goes grey. You hold R2. On the downbeat you tap L1 once:
  home falls a fifth, the big one comes home as it passes, and the three small ones go grey and lean
  after it. You let go on the one. All four fold up together, and the chord that lands has every one
  of them in it.
- **In the hands.** A flick, a dodge, strikes, R2, one tap of L1 on a downbeat, a release.
- **Underneath.** The small ones sing G and the big one D. With home on C, the G is a fifth above
  home and falls toward you, and the D is two fifths off and passes through you. With home on G, the
  small ones are kin and the D leans. Then D to G to C, each a fifth falling home: the strongest
  motion in music, and the one thing a tap of L1 does.

### 3. Surrounded, on the far side

- **The moment.** The far side: rows of hammers, dead level, everything marching in fours. Your note
  has never been further from anything, and you are surrounded. You hold R1 and point at the one
  cell you would know in your sleep, your own. For one phrase home is the garden, and from the
  garden everything here is as far away as a thing can be. You strike the nearest and it goes over
  the roofs. The next one's blow lands on you, and you go over the roofs as well, the other way,
  across the gap you could not jump.
- **In the hands.** One flick to a cell you know by heart, a strike, and letting yourself be hit on
  purpose.
- **Underneath.** F sharp is six fifths from C whichever way you count. Law 2 calls that far:
  nothing shared, and rough. "Your blows throw it, and its blows throw you." Borrowing home lasts a
  phrase.

### 4. A border that howls

- **The moment.** Dusk, at a bridge with too much on it. Things come up from under it doubled, two
  outlines a hair apart, red in the eye, and their howl is the sound between the two outlines. You
  do what has always worked. You wear one grey, put home beside it, hold, and let go on the one. It
  falls toward home, lands beside it, and howls. Again. It misses again, by the same gap every time.
  Then the one who came with you sings a single note between the two ends of the gap. Each end moves
  a hair toward it. You hold under all three and let go on the one. The whole pack lies down as a
  chord, and for as long as the three of you keep singing, the bridge is quiet.
- **In the hands.** Everything you know fails once, on purpose. Then the first lesson again: breathe
  with the one beside you, hold, let go.
- **Underneath.** The fifth across this border is 23.460 cents short, and nothing lands on a fifth
  like that. A third note between its two ends gives both something to tune to (clip 31: yes). The
  wolf fifth alone is the sound he called "nasty and hardcore, Ganon/Ganondorf energy". While it is
  carried between you it is quiet. When you walk away it is back, unless the remainder is moved.

### 5. The last one (an offer; the ending is his)

- **The moment.** Under the island, at the bottom, is the note the whole frozen lake has been
  holding. It is not loud. It is simply under everything, and it has never moved. Everything you
  have brought sings into it: the third someone gave you, the low seventh the caravan traded you.
  With those sounding over it, the great still note is not still any more. It leans, and it has
  nothing to lean on. So you do the first thing you ever did. You hold your one note, underneath it.
  On the one you let go, and the largest thing in the world comes home.
- **In the hands.** Hold R2. Let go on the one.
- **Underneath.** A note with its third and its low seventh over it (4:5:6:7) is the chord that most
  wants to fall a fifth. The lake's note is Drip's note, and Hum's is the fifth below it. The poem
  has the whole of this as a stage direction: "(V7 resolving to I)". Sketch h.

## The reason

**Everything that fights you is something that leans, with nothing under it.**

Wanting is what this world runs on: "ache IS the engine". A want with ground under it comes home,
and coming home is what makes music and moves water. A want with nothing under it does one of three
things.

| what it does | what it becomes | his words for it | what brings it home |
|---|---|---|---|
| it grabs, and holds on | a knot | "A DEMON OF FEAR", "It came from: PEOPLE", "It wants: TO BE UNDONE" | the one under its rhythms |
| it throws its ache at whatever is near | a wolf | "nasty and hardcore" | a third voice |
| it stops wanting | the frozen | "bloom, touch me, please, I'm numb" | swing, and then warmth |

- **These are states, not kinds of creature.** Anything can fall into them: a creature, a life, a
  river, a bell, a whole land, and Hum. So a land's troubles and a land's band are one cast, and
  every fight ends with one more voice in the world. His decision from the room fits this: each
  land's things knot, howl and freeze in that land's own metre and material.
- **Why there is so much of it.** The lake is low, so nothing in the world can finish its own phrase
  (part two). Every tune leans for ever. That is where knots, wolves and the frozen come from, and
  it is why there are fewer of each as the lake rises.

**Why the lake is low and frozen: three ages.** This is an offer of mine. It is the real history of
tuning, told as weather.

| age | its fifth | a lap round the ring | the lake | how it sounded | its trouble |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the age of fifths | pure | 23.460 over: rising | high: the old shorelines; it reached the ocean | clear and strong, with harsh thirds | one narrow wolf |
| the sweet age | narrowed, for sweet thirds | up to 41.059 short: sinking | falling | warm chords in every land that asked | a wolf's valley that grew with every land that was warmed |
| the treaty | 700 | closes: nothing moves | frozen where it lay | every land alike, and a little numb | none, and nothing comes home |

- **Nobody in it is wicked.** Each age mended the last one's pain. The poem says so twice: "silly
  human with their silly human crew", and "I mean, I get it."
- **The wolf is in the ice.** The treaty did not get rid of the howl. It cut it in twelve and froze
  a twelfth into every border. That is arithmetic: twelve times 1.955 is the 23.460 the old wolf was
  short by. So every pure thing made in a frozen world lets a grain of it out, and the first one is
  the fifth you lock with Drip.
- **So the trouble grows with your own kindness.** Warm one home and the far side goes cold. Warm
  many, and the sweetness is overdrawn past the twelve grains, and the debt gathers in one valley
  and howls. The game's great enemy is the sum of what was pushed away to make home sweet. That is
  his fixed thing as a plot: "the ache of the remainder is an engine, not a defect".
- **It has two faces.** Frozen, it is a held note that never moves, the sound his ears called "jail
  for ears". Thawed, it is the wolf fifth. Neither should be played at the player for long. Frozen,
  it is felt as the world answering less. Thawed, it howls for seconds at a time. And it has to thaw
  before it can come home: numb, then it hurts, then it resolves.
- **A hum and a drone are the same thing. The difference is listening.** The enemy is what a ground
  becomes when it stops moving under others. His ears already know the difference: the held synth
  tone he could not bear, against the page toy, where (in the room write-up's words) the sustained
  sound has tasteful harmony under it.
- **His worksheet's farthest sound** (an offer). Its listening list has, closest, "water dropping
  like a leaky faucet"; farthest, "the freeway"; and, for a sound that almost sounds like something
  else, "a low rumble in the atmosphere that sounds like the ocean". So his own evening has Drip
  nearest and, farthest, a rumble that sounds like the ocean and is not one. The pool wants the
  ocean.
- **Drip, and Tael.** The lake's frozen note is Drip's note: Drip is one drop of it that never
  froze. So the first evening is the last one, small: a note, and Hum's note under it. If he meant
  the brother who stays with the lost one, this is that.
- **And the player's reason.** Every trouble brought home is a voice the island's song did not have,
  and a thing in the world that now plays with you. Nothing is beaten, so nothing is used up.
- **Failing.** When Hum is shaken apart its note breaks up, the light goes, and the nearest river
  carries it to the island. It is an early night, and the land forgets the day. Nothing else is
  lost. His line again: "together we can make it home safe downstreeeeam".

## The feel

Six things a fight should put in the body, in the order they come:

1. **The lurch,** when home moves and everything in earshot changes what it is to you.
2. **The swing,** when home is right and a stumble turns into a groove.
3. **Opened time,** when you step aside on the beat.
4. **The chord,** when the last strike completes it and it is the brightest thing on the screen.
5. **The fall,** when you put home under a far thing and go to it.
6. **The landing,** when you let go on the one.

His hands have already said yes to the landing and to the timed strike. The other four have never
been built with any weight.

### The wheel must do five things every time it is touched

1. **Sound.** Entering a cell plays its chord, voice-led from wherever you were (noodles).
2. **Move the world.** Things lurch: see "Home is down".
3. **Show.** Every thing's shape and lean change as you hover, before you commit.
4. **Land.** Home moves on the beat, with weight: a still frame or two, the ring at Hum's feet
   jumping, the light changing hue.
5. **Be worth playing with nothing to fight.** Sit in the garden and play the cells, and the things
   that kept your bars follow.

Today it does the first only as a soft hum, and none of the rest. `after-the-first-plays.md` counted
his evening: about 30 holds of R1, home moved about 6 times, silently, and nothing answered. That is
why eight minutes taught nothing. It was not his hands.

## As deep and as coherent as Tears of the Kingdom

What makes that game hold together is not its size. It is that a few rules multiply, and every
ability turns the whole world into material (shape 1 has this from its makers' own talk).

### Ten rules

Seven are about sound, from `spec/laws.md`. Three are about music.

1. Things answer what they share.
2. Things fall toward the note underneath them.
3. Near rhythms fall into step.
4. A held note makes still places, and you can stand on them.
5. Opposites cancel into dark. Near-misses turn things.
6. Sound takes time to arrive.
7. Pushes in time build up.
8. A note means what is under it.
9. A rhythm and an interval are the same number.
10. What does not close leaves a remainder, and the remainder cannot be destroyed.

Everything in parts two and three is meant to follow from these. Where something doesn't, it is a
list, and it should go.

## Theory, taught and rewarded

His fixed thing stands over this section: music theory is "the physics and the play, not decoration
and not a quiz".

**Taught.**

1. **You do it before it has a name.**
2. **Drip does it first, beside you.** That is the Tael's job.
3. **The world shows the relation** as a shape, a lean and a thread. One idea has one look and one
   sound, everywhere.
4. **The first time is safe, and free.**
5. **Wonders** teach one thing each, with nothing to fight.
6. **The songbook names it afterwards, if you ask.** By the end it is a harmony book that you wrote
   by playing.
7. **Trouble arrives in the order music is learned:** rhythm, then harmony, then tuning.

**Rewarded.** Knowing more always gets you more.

1. **Outcomes.** How a thing comes home decides what it leaves. A full close springs it open. An
   amen settles it to sleep. A turn puts it beside you for a phrase (his "yes, a twist").
2. **Size.** A longer walk home lands a bigger chord: the seventh, then the ninth. He said yes to
   that.
3. **Reach.** Overtones are passports. The seventh is a road to anywhere.
4. **Short ways.** A note two lands share, held across a border. The straight way over the ice.
5. **Kindness.** The gentlest endings take the most skill: a whole pack sung to sleep in one long
   walk.
6. **Secrets.** Places that open to one particular move and no other.
7. **Company.** What you bring home stays, and plays.

**Never asked.** Nothing in the game ever asks what a thing is called. It asks whether you can get
home from here.

**Trouble as the lessons, in order.**
