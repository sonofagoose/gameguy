# 3. Theory errors and imprecisions

## A. Relative minor, wheel quality, and mode identity

1. **[THEORY ERROR] “Relative minor is three fifths down” is directionally wrong.**  
   This phrase appears in the audit instructions themselves. C major’s relative minor is A minor: on the ascending-fifths circle, C→G→D→A, so it is **`k + 3`**, three sharpward positions. Three fifths down gives E♭. “A minor third below” is the correct pitch-interval description. Britannica’s circle table independently lists C/A, G/Em, D/Bm, etc.  
   **Design consequence:** do **not** “fix” `foldWedge(k + 3)` to `k - 3`. The current wedge direction is right. The real problems are tuning, spelling, quality, and implementation.

2. **[THEORY ERROR] The old audit rejects `5/6` as a relative-minor root even though it is valid.**  
   `design/keep-revert-table-20261122.md`:18 rejects `k × 5/6`. For C, multiplying by 5/6 gives A in the lower octave: a just minor third down and therefore a valid relative-minor tonic. It differs from three pure stacked fifths, which produce 27/16, by the syntonic comma.  
   **Design consequence:** `k + 3` and `×5/6` are not interchangeable algorithms, but both can describe a valid relative-minor relationship in the appropriate tuning and register.

3. **[THEORY ERROR] The implementation does not realize the recorded “twelve major keys.”**  
   `DECISIONS.md`:153 says the outer ring is twelve major keys. `game/src/world/world.zig`:85–105 maps D/A/E to minor and B to diminished. `wedgeHz` at `:344–352` clamps input to the seven lit wedges, so the five dark labels do not have independent pitches.  
   **Design consequence:** the current wheel is a seven-mode model with twelve labels, not a functioning twelve-key major/minor system. `PROGRESS.md`:25–32 cannot honestly call that model complete.

4. **[THEORY ERROR] `k + 3` alone does not define relative-minor tuning.**  
   Three pure fifths give 27/16, a Pythagorean major sixth. The just C-major scale’s A is 5/3, different by the syntonic comma. Likewise, F-major’s relative D minor and C-major’s diatonic D minor need not use the same D.  
   **Design consequence:** a wedge index can select the pitch class, but the frequency must be derived from the active scale, tuning, and tonal context—not from a universal folded stack.

5. **[THEORY ERROR] The SCRATCH mode table misidentifies Phrygian, Locrian, and the minor subdominant.**  
   `SCRATCH.md`:300–304 calls Fierceness the minor subdominant and the darkest mode Phrygian. In the C-major/A-minor collection, tenderness is A Aeolian, fierceness is **E Phrygian**, and the darkest is **B Locrian**. E is A minor’s dominant; Dm is its subdominant.  
   **Design consequence:** the mood/power explanations built on those labels are model-written misreadings, not settled theory.

## B. Wolf intervals, commas, and triads

6. **[THEORY ERROR] The eleventh-fifths echo is not the standard Wolf fifth.**  
   `design/shape-2-volta.md`:113–132 calls eleven fifths “a fourth 23.5 cents too wide” and “the wolf.” Eleven pure fifths reduce to 521.505 cents: an augmented fourth, 23.460 cents above a pure fourth. A Pythagorean closing wolf fifth is:
   \[
   1200\log_2\!\left(\frac{2^7}{(3/2)^{11}}\right)=678.495\text{ cents}.
   \]
   The quarter-comma-meantone wolf is 737.637 cents, 35.682 cents wider than pure. These are different intervals.  
   **Design consequence:** the eleventh-dusk rule may remain a narrative choice, but it is not justified by the named Wolf interval. It needs a new mathematical explanation or a different interval/day assignment.

7. **[THEORY ERROR] Four fifths are not the fifth harmonic.**  
   `spec/laws.md`:75 says four wedges correspond to “a fifth overtone.” Four pure fifths produce 81/64; the fifth harmonic is 5/4. Their ratio is 81/80, the 21.506-cent syntonic comma.  
   **Design consequence:** “warm” can be a near-harmonic relationship with drift, but not exact fifth-harmonic gravity. If it belongs exactly, something must retune.

8. **[THEORY ERROR] The current strike’s Dm and B° ratios are not the Garden’s diatonic triads.**  
   In the implemented pure C scale, D–F = 32/27 (294.135 cents) and D–A = 40/27 (680.449 cents), not 6/5 and 3/2. B–F = 64/45 (609.776 cents), not 7/5 (582.512 cents). The current code instead uses generic 1.2/1.5 and 1.2/1.4 ratios, while the swell hardcodes a major third.  
   **Design consequence:** either the fork intentionally sings a free just triad—different pitches from the land’s scale—or it must derive actual scale members. Calling both models “home’s triad” hides the discrepancy and can produce unintended beating with land resonators.

9. **[THEORY ERROR] “Pure” does not make every diatonic fifth and third pure.**  
   The Garden’s own scale contains D–A = 40/27, a fifth 21.506 cents narrow, and D–F = 32/27 rather than 6/5.  
   **Design consequence:** “the Garden is pure” cannot mean every triad is simultaneously perfect. This is not merely a flaw to hide: it is a genuine tuning constraint the mechanics currently fail to use.

10. **[THEORY ERROR] “The comma is the slowest of all” contradicts the document’s own rates.**  
    `design/drawn-in-light.md`:49–66 lists an equal fifth at 0.89 Hz and a meantone fifth at 2.4 Hz, then calls the 3.57 Hz comma slowest. The separate two-cent example is slower still at about 0.25 Hz.  
    **Design consequence:** angular closeness and beat frequency are different quantities; the visual rule cannot infer tempo from the comma’s size alone.

11. **[THEORY ERROR] Two raw-brainstorm comma calculations are wrong.**  
    `r36s-game-brainstorm.md`:1562–1564 calls 23.46 cents “a quarter of a semitone’s quarter”; it is approximately **one quarter of a semitone**. At `:2017–2020`, twelve Pythagorean commas total 281.52 cents, not a “semitone-ish” 100 cents. These are raw model ideas, not decisions.

## C. Acoustics and third voices

12. **[THEORY ERROR] The bee’s receding interval is a minor third down, not a sixth.**  
    `DECISIONS.md`:140 and `spec/promises/sky.md`:72–73 say “a sixth low/under.” At \(v=c/5\), approach is \(5/4\), recession is \(5/6\), and the approach-to-recession ratio is \((5/4)/(5/6)=3/2\), the intended pure fifth.  
    **Design consequence:** the physical approach/recession pair is correct; the name of the receding interval is wrong. If “one-sixth less frequency” was intended, it must be written that way rather than as a musical sixth.

13. **[THEORY ERROR] The older opening’s third note is incompatible with the current Drip interval.**  
    `design/the-page-and-the-place.md`:75–76 uses Hum’s C and Drip’s F, whose difference is 87.21 Hz, low F. Current Drip is a pure fifth above Hum, C–G (`DECISIONS.md`:140; `spec/promises/first-evening.md`:73–86), whose difference is 130.815 Hz, low C an octave below Hum.  
    **Design consequence:** the older low-F passage cannot describe the current opening. The root, chord, and speaker treatment all change.

14. **[THEORY ERROR] Two near-unisons do not create a third spectral oscillator at their mean.**  
    `design/experience-fixes.md`:298–300 says the midpoint voice is “what two near-unisons sum to.” In fact:
    \[
    \cos A+\cos B=2\cos((A-B)/2)\cos((A+B)/2),
    \]
    a mean-frequency carrier with an amplitude envelope. That is not a new independent partial.  
    **Design consequence:** an explicitly added midpoint resonator can create the intended third voice and changed pulse pattern, but it is an added voice, not an emergent third sound sung by neither.

15. **[THEORY ERROR] Cancellation does not create a spherical silence zone.**  
    `spec/laws.md`:118–120 promises a small ball of silence; `spec/world.md`:332 silences broadband street noise at half a cycle. Opposite phase can cancel a pure tone at a particular observation point with matching amplitude. It does not cancel every point in a sphere or arbitrary broadband noise.  
    **Design consequence:** the mechanic needs coherent amplitude/phase matching and spatial nodes. A single mirror voice is not a universal acoustic invisibility field.

16. **[THEORY ERROR] Half-step roughness cannot be assigned universally to “over 30 Hz.”**  
    `spec/laws.md`:125–130 does so. At middle C, a semitone’s fundamental difference is 15.56 Hz; at 440 Hz it is 26.16 Hz. Roughness depends on register, critical bandwidth, and partials.  
    **Design consequence:** interval name alone cannot determine the mechanic. Octave and timbre must affect whether a pair beats slowly, shimmers, or becomes rough.

17. **[THEORY ERROR] “Fast roads” violate both material acoustics and the game’s own travel law.**  
    `spec/laws.md`:146–147 assigns metal, glass, and taut wire one 15× speed and makes a pipe’s far end effectively instantaneous. Sound speeds differ by material and wave mode; wire speed depends on tension and density; air inside a pipe still travels at air speed.  
    **Design consequence:** each medium needs a propagation rule and nonzero delay. An instantaneous pipe contradicts law 6.

18. **[THEORY ERROR] Harmonic/overtone numbering is off by conventional terminology.**  
    `spec/laws.md`:22–32,63–65,104–105 calls 3f the “third overtone” and gives the nth overtone n bellies. These are nth harmonics. The third overtone is conventionally 4f.  
    **Design consequence:** the in-house rule can work, but it must be defined as harmonic numbering or external acoustic references will be implemented off by one.

19. **[THEORY ERROR] Loudness, intensity, and amplitude are conflated.**  
    `spec/world.md`:412 says twice as far is a quarter as loud. That can describe intensity under inverse-square spreading; pressure amplitude halves, and perceived loudness has no universal quarter law. Coherent and incoherent groups also sum differently.  
    **Design consequence:** distance attenuation must name which quantity it changes, especially when several tuned sources answer together.

## D. Temperament and cadence

20. **[THEORY ERROR] “Equal temperament never locks” and “pure ratios cannot travel” are absolutes, not usable laws.**  
    `design/shape-3-the-lake-ring.md`:149–152 states both. Exact non-octave 12-TET ratios are irrational, but coupled oscillators can entrain by adjusting frequency—the premise of law 3. Purely tuned music can also modulate through retuning and pivot relations. `spec/laws.md`:43–46 itself gives the equal fifth 98% sympathy.  
    **Design consequence:** “closed and uniformly tempered” is true; “never locks” and “cannot travel” are false. These statements flatten temperament into caricature and contradict the game’s own coupling model.

21. **[THEORY ERROR] Cadence names are asserted without enough harmonic context.**  
    An unresolved dominant is normally a half cadence, not automatically deceptive. Two sharpward fifths can produce a deceptive V–vi only relative to a retained earlier tonic. A Neapolitan specifically means major ♭II, not any dark-wedge borrow.  
    **Design consequence:** the full-close and amen mappings are coherent; Turn, Slide, and Neapolitan need chord quality and tonal memory, not just wedge distance.

22. **[THEORY ERROR] Morning 8’s B♯ derivation needs an explicit enharmonic alias.**  
    B♯ is the seventh of C♯ major, not D♭ major. `design/the-way-home.md`:44–46 does explain the deliberate sharp-day/flat-land aliasing, but the main atlas simply says D♭.  
    **Design consequence:** this is defensible only if the table distinguishes the day’s accumulated C♯ spiral from the land’s D♭ identity. Otherwise the derivation is wrong.

## E. Sky, light, and scale models

23. **[THEORY ERROR] The sky’s throw formula fails above rung 266.**  
    `spec/sky.md`:80–85 says a \(144/n\) throw always clears the next rung but never the one after. In fact:
    \[
    144/n < 100\log_2(1+1/n)
    \]
    first at **n = 267**. The proof at `:618` checks only through 256.  
    **Design consequence:** the one-rung rule fails in the later sky. Using the unrounded \(100/\ln2/n\) coefficient satisfies the intended inequality for all n.

24. **[THEORY ERROR] The dark-seventh rule does not follow from the sun’s stated source.**  
    `spec/sky.md`:86–92 invokes suppression of every seventh harmonic in a string excited one-seventh along its length. At `:130–135`, the sun is a sung source through formant resonators, not that string.  
    **Design consequence:** the dark rungs need an explicit source/filter rule; the stated law does not produce them.

25. **[THEORY ERROR] Octaves are not “the even numbers.”**  
    `design/shape-1-a-musical-zelda.md`:353–355 says they are. Octave duplicates are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16…; harmonics 6, 10, and 12 also contain fifths and thirds.  
    **Design consequence:** odd harmonics are a valid source of new pitch classes, but even harmonics are not all octaves.

26. **[THEORY ERROR] Bohlen–Pierce does not mean “home never comes round again” in every sense.**  
    `spec/world.md`:612–614 specifies thirteen steps in a 3:1 span. Thirteen equal steps return exactly to 3:1. What is absent is closure at 2:1, the octave.  
    **Design consequence:** the deep can lack octave equivalence while retaining tritave periodicity. The distinction changes navigation and cadence design there.

27. **[THEORY ERROR] A golden-angle sunflower does have visible spiral arms.**  
    `spec/sky.md`:347–349 says it has no arms because its turn agrees with nothing. It has no exact rational repeat, but Fibonacci convergents produce the familiar visible spiral families.  
    **Design consequence:** “no exact closure” is valid; “no arms” is false. The sunflower can still contrast approximate rational closure with the fifth spiral.

28. **[THEORY ERROR] Five against seven does not imply a unique meeting only every thirty-fifth beat without defining the beat.**  
    `spec/world.md`:71–72 makes the thirty-fifth beat the clap point. In a conventional 5:7 polyrhythm, the patterns coincide at every shared bar. Thirty-five T is the coincidence period only if the individual oscillator periods are 5T and 7T.  
    **Design consequence:** the subdivision must be named, or the boss’s timing rule is underdetermined.

29. **[THEORY ERROR] Light is not sound raised forty octaves.**  
    The wavelength calculations are numerically sound, but electromagnetic radiation is not acoustic vibration.  
    **Design consequence:** this can be a deliberate transposition or transduction law, not a literally true acoustic wonder without a stated mechanism.

30. **[THEORY ERROR — my own earlier thread proposal] Twelve land roots sounding together are not automatically a beautiful chord.**  
    My earlier island role-play had all twelve lands answer as a chord. Twelve pitch classes form a chromatic aggregate with semitones and tritones, not an inherently consonant sonority.  
    **Design consequence:** the central finale needs voicing, register, arpeggiation, current-key filtering, or sequential form. “Everyone answers” is not itself a consonance rule.

# 4. Trap check

## 1. Musical agency saved for the ending

- **[CONTRADICTION]** The foundation already lets the player perform—call, sing, strike, swell, cadence—so the entire game is not guilty of this trap.
- **[CONTRADICTION]** `DECISIONS.md`:33 nevertheless divides “cause/read/be” before the ending from “make” afterward, and my earlier responses compounded it by proposing full independent-key control only post-ending.
- **[SETTLED — Morgan, this thread]** That deferral is rejected. The correct question is how agency grows through use, not whether the toy arrives after the credits.

## 2. Progression as checklist or disguised fetch quest

- **[SETTLED — `spec/people.md`:32–34; `spec/years.md`:143–144]** The design explicitly rejects counters and allows an unhelped player to finish.
- **[CONTRADICTION]** It simultaneously assigns 288 hand-written endings (`spec/years.md`:28–45), about 150 ties, and fixed recipes such as carrying Peg’s sock across the world (`spec/people.md`:100). Removing a quest log does not make a fetch errand emergent.
- **[BRAINSTORM]** The new “complete a land to unlock its relationship” idea can become another percentage gate. It must not be required for the thirteenth morning unless Morgan reverses the existing rule.

## 3. Quietly assuming 24 bespoke worlds

- **[BRAINSTORM]** Morgan’s major-above/relative-minor-below direction can be read as 24 full geographies, but it is not settled that each needs separate terrain and content.
- **[SETTLED — `spec/shape.md`:400–410]** The existing deep already says every layer is owed discovery, visibility, and sound verification. If that applies to every major/minor pair, the implied content is 288 wonders, 192 lives, and 576 wants before the island.
- **[PROPOSAL]** The viable interpretation is shared scores and geometry varied by tuning, material, rhythm, voice, and persistence—not 24 bespoke productions. Exact-copy and no-copy claims need to distinguish instancing from duplicated handcrafted content.

## 4. Temperament flattened into moral opposites

# 5. Missed mechanics

1. **[PROPOSAL] Use the Garden’s unavoidable tuning compromise as a real verb.**  
   In the current C scale, D = 9/8 makes C–D pure but D–A = 40/27, narrow. Changing D to 10/9 makes D–A = 3/2 exactly, while C–D moves 21.506 cents flat. A bridge or resonator could let the player choose which relationship holds and which starts to drift. This is an actual mechanical tradeoff, not a temperament quiz.

2. **[PROPOSAL] Let the player place the Wolf by choosing which fifth bears the error.**  
   In a twelve-note chain, eleven fifths can be pure while the closing fifth becomes 678.495 cents; in quarter-comma meantone, the leftover wolf is 737.637 cents. A tuning mechanism could choose which edge bears that interval, moving the Wolf’s valley rather than treating it as a fixed monster. The error cannot be deleted without changing the whole tuning.

3. **[PROPOSAL] Distinguish relative, parallel, and leading-tone transformations through shared notes.**  
   C major → A minor preserves C/E and moves G→A; C major → C minor preserves C/G and moves E→E♭; C major → E minor preserves E/G and moves C→B. These are distinct operations with two common tones and one moving voice. They can reconfigure the same terrain without requiring separate worlds, and they prevent relative minor from being confused with generic inversion.

4. **[PROPOSAL] Use harmonic minor’s raised seventh as a directed force.**  
   In A harmonic minor, G rises to G♯, creating a leading tone into A. That can power a one-way gate or ledge: the raised seventh gives an upward pull toward tonic that the natural seventh cannot provide. It also creates the augmented second F→G♯, a genuinely different traversal interval.

5. **[PROPOSAL] Use melodic minor’s ascending/descending forms for direction-dependent routes.**  
   Traditionally, ascending melodic minor raises the sixth and seventh; descending restores the natural-minor collection. A shaft, staircase, or river channel can therefore have different resonant rungs and cadence exits depending on direction—not merely a different visual treatment.

6. **[PROPOSAL] Make secondary dominants and guide tones the basis of temporary modulation.**  
   In C, D–F♯–A can tonicize G because F♯ resolves upward by semitone. Borrowing that chromatic note can create a temporary directed path to G. Releasing returns to C; sustaining or resolving it establishes a real modulation. Likewise, G7’s B→C and F→E can drive actual resolution gestures, and the respelled shared tritone explains D♭7 substituting for G7.

7. **[PROPOSAL] Require a sustained common tone for certain key crossings.**  
   Neighboring major keys share six of seven notes. A route could remain open only while one shared resonator keeps sounding across the modulation. Releasing it collapses the bridge. This turns pivot-note modulation into timing and cooperation, rather than a border text change.

8. **[PROPOSAL] Use inversions independently of root and key.**  
   Law 2 already references a thing’s lowest note. Let echoes choose root position, first inversion, or second inversion while preserving pitch set. Bass frequency, gravity, cadence smoothness, and which creatures share overtones can change without changing the key.

9. **[PROPOSAL] Use schisma and near-closure periods as long-duration clocks.**  
   Eight fifths in the other direction approach a just third within about 1.954 cents; 53 fifths miss closure by 3.615 cents. Those small residuals create slow, predictable phase returns. They can support long-hold doors, future-self assistance, and gatherings whose alignment is heard rather than counted.

10. **[PROPOSAL] Make register and critical bandwidth part of the rules.**  
    A semitone at middle C differs by 15.56 Hz; at 440 Hz by 26.16 Hz. Roughness also depends on partials and critical bandwidth, not a universal cents threshold. The same interval could be a gentle low-register pulse and a rough high-register hazard, giving voice and octave a real tactical role.

11. **[PROPOSAL] Give “relational tuning” a concrete shared-resonator algorithm.**  
    For two desired frequencies \(f_1,f_2\), a shared resonator at candidate \(f\) can score:
    \[
    S(f)=w_1e^{-(c_1/15)^2}+w_2e^{-(c_2/15)^2},
    \qquad c_i=1200|\log_2(f/f_i)|.
    \]
    Choose \(f\) that maximizes \(S\), using the existing law-1 coupling width and material weights. The residual beat or \(1-S\) can drive machinery. This is a determinate compromise rule, not a global “pure versus equal” moral choice.

# 6. Scope and buildability risks, ranked

1. **[SETTLED] Authority reset and unproven core interaction — highest risk.**  
   The current Morgan direction changes the hub, loop, geography, agency, and temperament model. Meanwhile the real Room session shows the existing wheel failing to teach itself. Building more of the old linear structure would maximize rework. This outranks hardware.

2. **[SETTLED] Verification and implementation state are not trustworthy.**  
   The full gate fails; the promise board has zero kept promises and two open Stage 2 claims; latest decisions contradict old promise tests. The guard also reads only selected SPEC material, not every decision or new thread idea. A green unit-test count cannot certify the experience.

3. **[SETTLED / BRAINSTORM] Bespoke content and human-verification volume.**  
   The base design already requires 144 wonders, 96 lives with 288 hand-written wants, twelve great instruments, duets, meetings, and generated descendants. If major/minor layers each receive full obligations, that becomes 288 wonders and 576 wants before the island. No evidence shows that one person and a model can build and human-verify this at the claimed quality.

4. **[SETTLED] Full-world runtime and streaming are unproven on RK3326.**  
   The engine targets 4,096 near bodies and 16,384 overtones (`spec/engine.md`:92–113), unique geometry, ground pictures, sky, and far lands. The benchmarks cover 2,100 overtones and 30,000 lines, not the full world. Actual play has recorded thousands of late frames, and unique-shape loading, ground drawing, and echo costs remain unmeasured (`spec/size.md`:173–181).

5. **[SETTLED] Persistent memory exceeds both storage and the replay model.**  
   A 10 GB world cannot have a complete second copy on 7,456 MB eMMC. Daily payloads fill cards in estimated years, while every dawn is promised forever. Regeneration requires every relevant program and land version; that cost is not fully accounted for. The new island and extra layers increase it.

6. **[SETTLED] Procedural music quality and meaningful agency are unverified.**  
   `ears` can measure roughness and balance, not whether a tune is good or personally meaningful. A thousand generated tunes passing measurement does not demonstrate musical agency. The current listening session already rejected several sustained sounds.

7. **[SETTLED] Two-console whole-world play is another large unbuilt system.**  
   The cable has a short packet test only. Hosting, long drift, cable removal, and long sessions remain unverified (`spec/together.md`:141–149). A persistent world for two duplicates the whole game and its storage obligations.

8. **[SETTLED] Process discipline is currently part of the scope problem.**  
   `true-actionable-vision.md`:238–258 introduces a dated Gantt/sprint schedule despite the project’s explicit prohibition on effort estimates. Combined with synthetic playtests and “done” summaries ahead of the gate, this creates false confidence about both feasibility and completion.
