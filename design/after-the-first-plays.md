# After the first plays: the view, the touch, the wheels, the room

Morgan played the first evening twice and walked part of the room on 2026-11-21. He asked for
five things. The camera should be like Tears of the Kingdom, Super Mario Odyssey or Hyper Light
Drifter: able to get above Hum, not stuck to the ground. Things should be solid. The wheels should
be clear. The room should be up to date and repeat nothing. The first evening should start pitch
black. Four research strands read the code, replayed his takes and read the craft. Their reports
are summarised here, with one order of work. Every number is either read from the code, measured
or worked out from it, or marked as an estimate to tune.

## What his hands and the code showed

- **The view.** The right stick only tilts the eye where it is: 22 m behind Hum and 2.2 m up. So
  the eye never rises above Hum.
  - The eye's height comes from the ground under Hum, so on slopes it goes into the ground. It is
    up to 9.8 m under on the spigot's rise, and inside the beds by up to 16 m at the rim.
  - The view's follow bends straight runs into circles. A stick held 10° off turns Hum about 110°
    in 10 s.
  - Everything nearer the eye than Hum less 5 m is erased, and that includes the near ground that
    shows speed.
  - The dodge and the knock-back move Hum 2.2 m and 1.6 m in one step, through anything.
- **The touch.** Every blade, clover stalk, moss cushion, crumb and fallen leaf, and all 264
  reeds, exist only in the drawing. The world knows only which cover lies at a place.
  - The fallen flower head is a world thing and still not solid.
  - There are two hose coils: the drawn one and the world's.
  - Where both the world and the picture know a thing, they disagree. A leaf rings at a line on a
    6 m grid, not at the drawn leaf. The spigot is solid as a 1.2 m round post, but drawn as 0.9 m
    and 1.7 m.
  - The hash is written three times.
- **The wheels.** Moving home, the wheel's one output, makes no sound and no light, and nothing
  shows where home is.
  - In his evening about 30 holds of R1 opened the wheel, and home moved about 6 times, silently.
  - 15 let-gos pointed nowhere and 6 landed on asleep wedges. Nothing answered either.
  - Wheel and threads:
    - About 160 hairline threads hide the knots' six.
    - Ten of the twelve wedges cannot be chosen and look almost like the two that can.
    - His quick calls (5 to 19 steps) flick the wheel open at 10 steps.
  - In the swell, the stick's nudge also turned the view (fixed, below).
  - The knots never came to him: they wait 307 m off and notice Hum only within 180 m.
- **The room.** In walks 2 to 4 his presses never reached the world (fixed in `2676fb6`).
  - The walks don't start at their moment:
    - 10 of the 23 walks used a second, hand-made Garden (the "asters" setup);
    - 13 started in the black, minutes from what they ask about.
  - The room breaks walk texts and leaves things out:
    - it cuts every walk's text at 68 characters;
    - it never shows a promise's own directions;
    - its questions use words he has never been shown ("home", "threads", "stumbling");
    - "hold R2 until it runs out" takes 87 s.
  - It mixes into his own game:
    - it writes into his saves, takes and ground light;
    - its clips are a day older than the game;
    - no log says which build ran.

## The order of work

Each part ends in something Morgan plays, with plain questions in the room.

### 1. The view goes round Hum (started 2026-11-21)

- **One dial on the right stick, up and down.**
  - Its range:
    - at the bottom, low at Hum's feet looking up;
    - in the middle, today's walking view exactly;
    - at the top, 43 m over Hum, looking down 64°.
  - It moves on one smooth curve of height, distance and width.
  - The view turns and moves faster the further the stick is pushed (its push past the dead
    middle, raised to the power 1.8), and eases in over 60 ms.
- **The anchor.**
  - The eye is anchored to the ground under Hum, and to Hum's height only past 3 m, so a jump
    never moves the picture.
  - Falling fast, the view rises to see the landing.
- **What stands between.**
  - The eye keeps over its own ground, and the line to Hum clears the ground.
  - Past a steep rise round Hum the eye comes in instead, slowly back out.
  - Thin things fade in a cone round the line of sight, instead of everything near the eye
    fading.
- **Straight runs stay straight.** The stick's directions are held while the stick is held steady.
- **The follow eases in.** It is faster at a sprint and slower gliding.
- **L2 tapped with nothing near swings the view behind Hum.** Past 100° away it cuts on the beat.
- **A fight lifts the view** to about a quarter of the way up the dial, so the ground round Hum
  shows. This follows Hyper Light Drifter.
- **In the swell the right stick does not look.**
- **Hum faces the eye**, so it stays a figure from above.
- **Still owed in this part:**
  - the lean at a tall foot in two phases;
  - lock-on with L2 near a knot, a thread of light to the target;
  - the hose's own rail.
- **Morgan's calls:**
  - which way up the stick looks;
  - whether the default sits a little higher.

### 2. Movement with weight

- **A two-stage start.** 6 m/s in 0.13 s, then 9 m/s by 0.63 s.
- **Turning.** Arcs that keep speed:
  - a radius of 1.5 m at a run and 5 m at a sprint;
  - a skid-turn, with the ground's sound, when the stick swings past 110° at speed.
- **The jump.**
  - Letting go early halves it.
  - A press up to 6 steps before landing counts, and so does one up to 6 steps after walking off
    an edge.
  - Hum squashes a little on landing. The camera never shakes.
- **The dodge and the knock-back** become short bursts through the same move as walking, so
  bodies stop them.
- **Slopes.** They pull: uphill slower, downhill faster. Over 45° Hum slides.
- **Steps.** Hum steps up 0.3 m (0.45 m on Gentle) and walks off anything deeper than 0.5 m.

### 3. What is solid: one law, give

The law: a thing gives way to a push as its own shape and material let it.
- Thin rooted things bend aside and spring back at their own rate; thick ones stop.
- Loose things move when the push beats their grip.
- What bears Hum dips or tips.
- Whatever Hum meets is struck, and law 1 carries it.

One push, worked out once, gives every cover's answer from its real sizes. Nothing is listed by
hand.

| thing | what it does |
|---|---|
| lawn blade | bends aside, springs back, wears a path |
| moss stalk | gives at once |
| clover stalk | stops Hum and shivers |
| aster stalk, reed stem | stops Hum |
| aster leaf | dips 3 m under Hum, a springboard |
| fallen leaf | tips like a seesaw |
| crumb | a boulder; a low one is stepped onto |
| fallen flower head | stops Hum and rings its C |
| rim wall | stops Hum and answers in brick |
| Drip, before you meet | holds Hum off softly, in its own note |
| knots | push apart softly |

- **The covers move into the world.** A new `world/covers.zig` makes the bodies from one hash,
  and the drawing draws those same bodies.
- **The reeds go into the score** with their real sizes, about 250 of them. They become things,
  so a call rings them, and they play as open pipes.
- **The drawn-only coil goes.** The spigot is solid as drawn.
- **One query, `nearBodies`, serves every mover:** Hum, the dodge, the knock-back, the camera, the
  walker and the knots.
- **What is kept goes in the save:** wear, dew, a leaf's tilt. It is small: about 10 to 60 KB a
  day (estimate).
- **The hose's scans** of all 864 pieces become a cell index.
- **Morgan's call:** the lawn's density, real or sparse.

### 4. The wheels made clear

- **The reading.** Every thing shows what it is to home, by the laws already written:
  - its shape (fewer lobes, nearer home);
  - still, or turning once a beat;
  - its colour's depth;
  - a grey creature's lean toward its door.

  The player first meets this in the figure between Hum and Drip.
- **Home is always shown,** as a ring at Hum's feet in home's colour. Moving home is a cue, with a
  sound and a light, whichever hand moved it.
- **R1 asks, then chooses.**
  - The wheel opens at 24 steps, above his longest tap.
  - Thick time slows Hum too.
  - The view stays nearer: about 32 m up (estimate).
  - Wedges:
    - awake wedges are coloured and carry their mood's shape;
    - asleep wedges are a hairline;
    - entering a wedge clicks in its note.
  - At most 12 threads, each to the wedge where its thing falls into step.
  - Resting on a wedge previews it: things re-form toward what they would be, and Hum hums the
    note under its breath.
- **R2 is playing.**
  - Each bar adds a band in the colour of the note it added.
  - Drip's tick on the one is fuller, and gathers light before it, so the one is seen coming.
  - The neighbours lie on Hum's ring.
  - A grey knot's shape goes toward its door bar by bar.
- **The knots come to Hum,** drawn by its voice once the place has stood.
  - They turn once for each beat between their cycle and Hum's bar, so on their root they stand
    still. "Stumbling becomes a dance" is the same "the figure stopped" the player learned with
    Drip.
- **One module, `world/home.zig`,** answers "which wedge, what relation, what shape, which door"
  for the world, the drawing, the songbook and the scripted player.
- **Morgan's calls:**
  - whether the swell's steps move to taps of L1 and L2 while R2 is held (recommended), or stay
    on the stick with the view held still (as now);
  - "Hum sings home" against "one note, seven homes", on clip 26.

### 5. The room, true and in plain words

- **Every walk starts from a save of the scripted first evening at a named moment of rest.**
  - The moments are: the dark, beside Drip, stood, at a foot, the pool's edge, the bee near, the
    knots coming, the knots in step, grey and resting, the hose open.
  - The build makes those saves every time, so the walks are always this build's game.
  - The "asters" setup leaves the room.
- **One walks file** holds each walk's title, start, what to do and its questions.
  - The tool refuses:
    - a start the evening never reached;
    - a question using a word the player hasn't been shown;
    - an either/or question;
    - the same question twice.
  - It replaces `checkpoints.txt` and the scenes in the promise book.
- **13 walks replace 23.** Each is shown whole, in plain words, and named by what is on the
  screen.
- **The room never writes the player's saves, takes or ground.** Its takes go to `results/walks/`.
- **The listening clips are cut on every build.**
- **Every log names its build.**
- **The scripted evening is reordered** so the knots come with home on C, as `SPEC.md` 6.1 tells
  it.
- **The hands are made safe:**
  - the counters are reset per scene;
  - which keys are down is read from the kernel when a scene starts and when the songbook closes;
  - no event the kernel has given is dropped;
  - the sound thread ends a hum it started if the world doesn't take it up within 100 ms.

### 6. Repetition that lets things drift

- **Hum's shape** is written three times. It becomes one `humShape`, which the drawing, the swell
  and the body all read.
- **Hum's own sounds** are written in the world and again in the hands. They become one set of
  cue builders.
- **Colours, `col8` and the hash** are each kept once.
- **The ground picture's extent** is worked out from the rim, with a header in its file.
- **The game writes `fact` lines,** and the tools read only those.
- **One hands reader** serves the game, the room and the keyboard.
- **The walker** calls the world for directions instead of repeating its sums.
- **The ground's light fades** while the lake is low, as `SPEC.md` 15 says. The first evening
  stays black because there is no yesterday.

## The sources

The four research reports, 2026-11-21. They were kept outside the repository, and these are their
conclusions.
- The camera: Nesky, "50 Camera Mistakes" (GDC 2014); Haigh-Hutchinson; the decompiled Super Mario
  64 camera; Cinemachine; Tears of the Kingdom's camera return.
- What is solid: Breath of the Wild's chemistry rules; Tears of the Kingdom's physics; Celeste's
  forgiveness; Unity's and Unreal's character controllers.
- The wheels: Ōkami, Ori, Stasis and Recall, NecroDancer, Patapon, Rez, Tetris Effect, Journey,
  Tunic, Outer Wilds, Wind Waker.
- The room: an audit of `room.zig`, `promises.py`, the walker and his session logs.
