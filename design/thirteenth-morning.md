# Thirteenth Morning (working title; Morgan names the game)

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History: an imagined ultimate form of the older design (2026-11-13), and
> the source of most of `SPEC.md`. The direction keeps Hum, Drip, the garden, the opening, twelve
> lands round a lake and the river at night. It drops the thirteen mornings each a fifth higher, the
> mood wheel of seven fixed powers, the voice wheel on L1, lock-on and guard on L2, and past selves
> as the spine. Where each piece of the older design stands now is in `design/the-game.md`, "What
> the older notes had, and where each stands".

_A small plaster creature who can sing one note walks a ring of twelve lands around a draining lake,
each morning a fifth higher than the last, learning to sing beside its own past and the one who
remembers it, until the thirteenth morning brings it home and not home._

An imagined ultimate form of the game, written on 2026-11-13 by a design agent from the three
shapes, the look and memory in rhythm. Nothing here is decided. Seven imagined players then played
it: their diaries and what they reveal are in `playthroughs/`.

## Summary

Here's the ultimate version as I imagine it. The working title is Thirteenth Morning, and the name
is yours to give. You're Hum, a little plaster creature who can only hum one note, living in a
garden on the shore of a round lake. Twelve lands ring the lake, laid out as the circle of fifths.
Each morning the world rises a fifth: the sun comes up over a different land, the colours and moods
shift, and the lands in that day's key open up.

Five odd voices lend you their singing: a drop that never finished falling, a sapling who talks too
much, an iron doorstop, a gossiping mushroom and a flying strand of patio lights. Hold R1 and time
slows into pulses while a wheel of seven moods opens. Flick and release, and your power changes
along with the music. The second wheel picks who sings, so wonder in a deep voice lifts a boulder
and in a high voice lifts you.

Fights are quick and on the beat. You dodge in the pocket and ring chords with a tuning fork. Then
you hold the swell and land the cadence, and the enemy resolves instead of dying. Every note lands
in key and in time, and dissonance only ever leans toward home.

Each night the river carries you home and sings your day back to you, and you keep up to four past
selves as a band. One creature remembers you from day to day, and only they bring the warm third
your own fifths can't make. On the thirteenth morning home comes back a comma sharp, and you choose
between sameness, purity and a duet. With JJ's console in the room, you're each other's third.

## 1. The game in one paragraph

You are Hum, a small plaster creature who can sing exactly one note. Your garden sits on the north
shore of a round lake ringed by twelve lands, and each land is a key. Each morning comes a pure
fifth higher than the last: the sun rises over a different land, and the world changes colour and
mood with it. Five odd creatures lend you their voices; you fight wolves, knots and grey soldiers
with a tuning fork and a held breath; each night the river carries you home, sings your day back,
and lets you choose which of your past selves walk beside you tomorrow. Twelve mornings on, home
comes round a hair too warm: B♯, home and not home. Sound and picture are one set of numbers, so a
chord ringing and a figure closing are the same event.

It leaves the feeling of the end of a long walk with someone, humming: calm, a little lonely, then
not.

## 2. The first ten minutes

Black. A drip, close, to the left. Low on the screen, a small bone-white shape in reeds: two dark
eyes, a glint in each. You can hear it breathe.

Press R1 and Hum calls, a plain *mm*, and a ring of pale light runs out over the ground: reeds, a
coiled garden hose, gone. Nothing answers. Call again. Far off, a ring comes back, teal against your
bone.

It repeats as you near it, muffled and smeared in time, like a voice through a wall. Click the right
stick and the camera glides into Hum's eyes. A drop hangs under a leaky spigot, never quite falling,
and between you a fine line drawing turns, a loop that won't close. Hold R2 to sustain your note;
let go on the drop's tick, press again on the next. The flams tighten into one sound, the drawing
closes, and a third note, lower than either of you, blooms between. "Oh. You're in," says the drop,
flatly. That's Drip. It falls onto your head and stays.

Dawn. The intro is your one held note, and the light comes up green: the garden, key of C, asters
towering like cathedrals. Everything is dry. The hose is knotted, and the knot is moving.

Hold R1. Time thickens, the music sinks into slow pulses, and a wheel blooms round you, twelve
points strung into a star, two wedges bright. Point the right stick at the left one and the world
re-voices itself as wonder. Release. Hold R2 at a fallen flowerpot, three notes gather on your
breath, release, and it floats; you ride it onto the awning.

The knot lashes, and three small knots unspool, stumbling five against three. Left face: your fork
rings on the beat, because the game puts it there. Right face as one lunges, on the beat, and
everything slows: three strikes, three notes, a chord. The knots drain to grey and the music swells.
Hold R2 and the swell keeps building, sweet, never sour; let go on the downbeat. The cadence falls
home and the knot springs open into a loop of hose. Water runs. The pool rises a finger's width, and
for a second a blue figure sits on its edge: someone who sat here once.

## 3. The world

**The lake ring.** A round lake, a day's walk around, with twelve lands on its rim laid out as the
circle of fifths: the garden (C) at the north, sharp keys warming clockwise, flat keys cooling the
other way, meeting at the south. From anywhere on the rim you can see your garden across the water.
Neighbours share six of their seven notes, so the music changes one note at a time as you walk; the
land opposite is a long walk round and a short sail across.

**Tuning is distance from home.** The garden is tuned pure, and everything there rings; round the
rim the lands grow more tempered, the way history did. Each plays only the music that sounds best in
its own tuning, and the songbook credits the traditions each tuning comes from.

| keys | lands | tuning | what they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | the Garden | pure | asters, the Mirror Pool, the knotted hose |
| G, F | the Alfalfa; the Porch | pure | a forest of alfalfa; leaves turning by a porch |
| D, B♭ | the Glass Hills; the Reed Mouths | fifths | ringing crystal ridges; reed deltas, drones and chant |
| A, E♭ | the Bell Meadow; the Rain Steps | sweet thirds | bell-flowers big as houses; terraces where clouds rest |
| E, A♭ | the Highway; Verbena Hollow | sweet thirds | salt flats at 2am, red only in tail lights, and the Stage under one red bulb; violet thickets, the wolf's valley at their edge |
| B, D♭ | the Chrome Nave; the Aster Steps | well-tempered | a car's interior big as a cathedral; terraced asters |
| F♯/G♭ | the Seam | equal | the treaty city on its bridge |

**The seam.** In the south the rim is cracked, and the lake drains away under a bridge city where F♯
and G♭ are agreed to be one note: everyone meets there, everything a little off, everything
connected. Beneath the bridge swings the Metronome; down in the crack lives the Wolf.

**Above and below.** The sky is the untempered harmonic series, gated by a note not on the wheel.
Through the Mirror Pool, the deep is the world reflected, major turned minor, all blue, with a
country at its bottom that has no octave. On the water, the Shimmer is a bronze grove tuned in pairs
that beat on purpose, as Balinese gamelan does: the one place where wobble is beauty.

**The lake's level.** The lake starts low: phrases end on lovely almost-endings, and songs sent over
the water fade before they come round. Each freed river raises it, and a full lake lets the music
come home.

## 4. The day and the loop

A day is about twenty minutes, shaped like a song: dawn intro, morning verse, noon chorus, afternoon
bridge, dusk chorus, night outro. The music and the sun are the clock; suspend anywhere.

The sun rises over the day's land, and the seven lands in its key are open and bright; the other
five are dim, reachable but strange, their doors wanting a borrowed chord. Over twelve days the open
arc walks once round the lake. Other lives keep their own cycles: the beloved is back at the garden
every third day, Caravan No. 27 every fourth.

At dusk the river carries you home, and your day comes back as a half-minute song of your own
melodies; after a big win it runs backwards from the win. Keep up to four takes as echoes; the rest
walk into the water. A post carved in the world's old script offers a repeat sign or a bracket.
**Repeat:** live this day again; its events restart, what you resolved stays resolved, and your kept
selves walk beside you. **Onward:** a fifth up. Relive a day the same way and grey paths wear into
the ground: the Grid grows on sameness.

Some nights you dream, and at dawn a way you never walked is traced in blue. What you practised
comes a little easier.

**The thirteenth morning.** After twelve onwards the sun rises over the garden again, but the note
is B♯: on a piano, C; here, a comma sharp, the green seven degrees warm. Every life's cycle lines
up, and everyone is at the garden.

## 5. Controls

Face buttons by position.

| input | tap | hold |
|---|---|---|
| left stick, L3 | move; click to hush (stop humming, go dark) | |
| right stick, R3 | camera, aim, steers the mood wheel; click to focus | |
| bottom face | jump | glide |
| right face | dodge (on the beat: the pocket) | sprint |
| left face | strike with the fork | charge, release on the beat |
| top face | grab, talk, plant | carry; in focus, keep a phrase |
| L1 | last voice | voice wheel, on the left stick |
| R1 | call: a ring of light | mood wheel, on the right stick |
| L1 and R1 | the clap | both wheels at once |
| L2 | next target | lock on and guard |
| R2 | a short phrase | sing: gather notes, release on the beat |
| D-pad | gather echoes; sit and listen; play a kept song | |
| Select, Start | the songbook; pause | |

**Thumb audit.** Moving and singing: left thumb, right index on R2, right thumb aiming. Moving and
fighting: left thumb, right thumb on the face buttons, left index holding lock-on so the camera
needs no thumb. Wheels: index fingers hold, sticks steer. Nothing needs the right stick and a face
button at once.

**Camera.** Third person, Hum in the lower third, sky above. Cuts land on the beat; a hit freezes
the picture two or three frames, a staccato in place of the rumble this device lacks. **Focus**
glides into Hum's eyes over one beat and narrows the sound to what you look at. It is for voices,
pools, phrases and careful shots.

## 6. The wheels

**R1, the mood wheel.** Hold it and time slows to an eighth; the music sinks until chords become
pulses, and every enemy and object throbs at its own rate. Twelve points ring Hum, strung into a
star; across the top glow seven wedges, today's notes. Where you put home among them is a mood, and
each mood is a power. From the left: wonder (Lift), sunny home (Bind), bluesy drive (Drive), cool
soul at the top (Flow), tenderness (Remember), fierceness (Strike), and at the right the darkest,
rare and costly (Break): the seven modes, bright to dark, never named.

The powers never move under your thumb; behind them the ring of keys turns a notch each morning, so
the wheel is also compass and calendar. Hovering previews the mood in the music at once; release
commits on the next beat; centre cancels. Wedges are wide, and the stick snaps.

The five dark wedges below are borrowed chords. Flick to one for a super: the world borrows that
chord for one phrase, huge and in tune, then resolves home, at the cost of a whole breath and your
built-up phrase. It brings the land it borrows from: borrow from the Rain Steps and it rains.

**L1, the voice wheel.** Five petals, bright to dark clockwise from the top: Patio (ee), Puffball
(eh), Box Elder (ah), Drip (oh), Doorstop (oo). The centre is you.

**Multiplying.** Mood is what; voice is who, and how big. Low voices move heavy things and the
ground; high ones move light things, the air and you. Lift with Doorstop raises a boulder, with
Patio raises you; Strike with Doorstop is a ground-pound, with Patio a needle of light.

**Staying in tune.** You never pick a pitch: you choose mood, voice and moment, and the engine draws
every note from today's key and the chord beneath you, landing it on the nearest beat. Echoes are
always retuned into today. Out of phase sounds loose or hollow, never sour.

## 7. Abilities and building

- **Lift:** float, rise, levitate; two voices facing on one note make a standing wave that holds
  things up.
- **Bind:** set things pulsing in time with you and they move as one machine; a part nudged off the
  beat flams and falls away.
- **Drive:** wheels, fans, your own dash; parts moving three against two walk.
- **Flow:** ride water, slip through, heal; a sound's mirror image cancels it into silence.
- **Remember:** rewind a thing's motion; call your echoes; sing a still pool its note and it plays
  the past in sun-print blue.
- **Strike:** force and heat; graft an interval on your fork: a fifth splits a swing, a third makes
  a sung shot seek.
- **Break:** shatter; hold a thing's own note and glass gives.

Underneath, everything has a home note, and what happens between two things is the interval between
them. Kin are weightless to you; a thing a fifth above falls toward you, and toward one a fifth
below, you fall; thirds warm and grow; the far side of the circle repels.

Find a giant's rhythm (a bell-flower, a hanging bridge, a sleeping beast) and push on the beat until
it rings or swings you across. Keep yesterday holding a plate down while today crosses, or set three
of yourself singing a chord to open a gate: which days you keep is composing.

Moods wake as lands are set right; voices join as you meet them. One breath serves singing, wheels,
sprint and glide, and silence refills it. At first a breath holds three notes; each longer breath
adds a colour, the seventh, then the ninth, up to seven.

## 8. Combat

Every hit rings the enemy at its own note, so you learn foes by ear. Your heartbeat sets the tempo,
and a fight quickens it.

- **The pocket:** dodge on the beat and time slows into a flurry; each strike is a note of a chord,
  and a finished chord rings out.
- **Unison guard:** hold L2 and meet a blow as it lands; its own note rings back at it.
- **Wearing down:** hits drain an enemy's colour. Grey, it leans, a held chord aching to resolve.
- **The cadence:** hold R2 and the build-up swells as long as you dare; release on the downbeat and
  it resolves. Nothing dies; it transforms.
- **The clap:** after a full phrase in the pocket (a drum joins each bar to tell you), L1 and R1
  together are the drop: every enemy near you resets to the same point in its cycle, so one dodge
  beats them all. Without the phrase it is only a clap.

**Wolves** are made of the comma, the part nobody shared: doubled outlines, red eyes, a beating
howl, packs at dusk. Resolved, each folds into a pup that follows you and drums. **Knots** are fear,
tangled rhythms that lash; strike on the pulse they share and they come undone. **Greys** are the
Grid's soldiers, chrome and grainless, marching exactly on the grid; strikes laid just behind the
beat break them, and colour comes back to a lost creature.

**Bosses are pieces of music.** The Great Knot, the garden hose itself, ties five against seven:
clap the downbeat they share. The Metronome locks the arena to its grid and learns to predict you;
swing breaks it. The Wolf at the Seam is every echo you never released, tamed by letting them go,
one howl resolving at a time.

**Gentle or fierce,** chosen at the river any night. Gentle widens the timing and lets most fights
end by comfort: sing the note that lets them rest. Fierce tightens it, adds syncopating foes and
keeps your best takes to beat. A missed beat sounds plainer, never wrong.

## 9. Voices

You meet each voice by getting in phase. It mumbles, smeared in time, until in focus you match its
pulse and breath; then its words come clear, its inks snap into register, and a third note blooms
between you.

- **Drip** (oh, low-middle): a drop that never finished falling. Keeps a living, slightly swung time
  and rides on your head.
- **Box Elder** (ah, middle): a sapling on two roots, stuck in the Alfalfa, who talks too much and
  sings the tune.
- **Doorstop** (oo, bass): a squat iron wedge holding a gate open in the Glass Hills, for nobody.
  One word a day.
- **Puffball** (eh, upper-middle): a mushroom in the Rain Steps whose spores hang in the air as held
  notes; it is wired to everything underground and gossips about it.
- **Patio** (ee, high): a strand of patio bulbs found dangling from the Stage's red bulb. It flies
  like a kite and lights the night when it sings.
- **You**, at the centre: at first a closed *mm*. Over the game it opens, until the centre is the
  plain *uh* of someone singing without thinking.

On the road you also meet **the white cat**, waiting at forks: sit beside it and each way is sung
ahead in a quick phrase. **Jeanne**, a long-eared traveller of nineteen, rides with **Caravan No.
27**, carries a compass, won't say where she's been, and warns you not to ride your luck too hard.
Once she trusts you, her compass lets you choose where the river takes you at night: where home is,
and how high. The Caravan trades notes off the wheel for echoes you'll let go. And **the fly**
insists on your knee whenever you sit to listen.

## 10. Story and heart

You have no voice of your own at first, so the voices lend you theirs; the game is finding your own.

**The beloved.** One creature remembers across the days, faintly: a flowering vine you never quite
see clearly, blurred and doubled when you are out of tune with them, sharp when you are in. They
keep a three-day life round a triangle across the lake and hum yesterday's tune back to you a little
wrong, the way someone remembers a song. Their voice lives on the third, the warmth no stack of your
own fifths can make exactly: your echo band rings bright and open, and with them it turns warm. Sing
together and your heartbeats drift into step.

**The Grid** is the loop closing perfectly: it would temper everything until day twelve equals day
zero, forever. **The Wolf** is purity that won't share its error, so the error piles up in the crack
and howls. Keep an echo eleven days without releasing it and at dusk it leaves your band, howls for
a few seconds, and goes to the Wolf.

What it is about is never said: being heard; losing your note in someone else's key and finding it;
singing beside someone without disappearing into them; keeping the small difference on purpose. The
world teaches by consequence: hoarded pasts hunt you, repeated days go grey, freed rivers bring the
lake home.

**The thirteenth morning** makes three offers.
- **The Grid's:** seal the seam, and every day is the same. The credits loop perfectly, in grey,
  until you press a button.
- **The Wolf's:** keep everything pure. The lake drains through the crack, and the music stays
  beautiful and alone.
- **The duet:** you and the beloved hold the gap between you. For a few deliberate seconds you hear
  the comma, two tones almost one, swelling. Then a third voice, home, sounds between you, sung by
  neither. The Mirror Pool goes downstream to the ocean. The fourteenth morning is the first day
  that isn't a repeat: each night you choose up a fifth, down a fifth or stay, and past the lake a
  new direction opens, lands tuned by thirds that never repeat.

After either of the first two, the river offers the thirteenth morning again.

## 12. Sound and picture as one

One model of frequencies, phases and ratios drives both; nothing is matched by hand.

| quantity | heard as | seen as |
|---|---|---|
| pitch class | note | hue, by fifths; purple at the seam, where the eye invents its own closure |
| octave, loudness | register, volume | height, brightness; silence is dark |
| ratio | interval | the figure between two voices, still and saturated when pure |
| near-miss | beating | inks out of register, flickering at the beat |
| harmonic recipe | timbre | material |
| phase | together or loose | moving together |

Signature moments: the first answer in the dark; holding the wheel until a fifth slows into
three-against-two you can watch; inks snapping into register; the beloved coming into focus; in the
sky, raising a held A forty octaves until it arrives as orange light; the comma's few seconds on the
thirteenth morning.

## 13. Two consoles

Alone, the game is whole. Together, in one room:

- **The treaty.** Both players hold R1 and point at the same station. A four-count plays on both,
  both clap on the one, and the games share key and tempo.
- **Side by side.** Each plays their own world. The parts split: one game sings roots, fifths and
  its tune, the other thirds and its tune. You are each other's third, and neither sounds thin
  alone.
- **In the air.** Sing a pure interval on the same beat and a third, lower tone can sound in your
  ears, made by neither machine (untested on these speakers).
- **Drift.** The games can't hear each other, so their clocks slowly slip; you hear flams, out of
  time and never out of tune. Clap on the one to re-sync.
- **SD card.** A take is only its inputs, a few kilobytes. Trade echoes and they walk your friend's
  lake in blue.
- **USB cable,** optional, if a link between units proves out: each sees the other's creature live,
  across the water.

## 14. Hidden depths

The old machines under the Chrome Nave think in rhythm: a hum on the beat is 1, off it 0, and three
hums vote. The garden's bells, on cycles of three, four and five beats, meet every sixty. A door
shut above opens in the mirror. Past the turn, the garden's note is never C again. The coda climbs
every octave, from a petal's standing waves to the young universe ringing.

## 15. What it asks of the hardware

- Native code on SDL2 and OpenGL ES 3.2 with a deterministic fixed-step simulation: a take is its
  inputs, so echoes, replays and trades are nearly free.
- 17.1 ms a frame; aim for 11 ms of GPU. Light per vertex, haze for distance, 3D at 75% resolution
  when needed, ink, grain and glow at half or quarter resolution, the lake a moving mesh rather than
  a full-screen water shader.
- Lands built from rules, few textures; all sound synthesised live, about 200 of the 800 voices one
  core can run; under 300 MB of RAM.
- Built against the hotter purple unit. The mono speaker is the floor: low voices are built from
  upper harmonics, and the ear supplies the root.

## Decisions taken

- **Which wheel R1 opens (shape 3: key wheel or mood wheel)** One circle: seven lit wedges are moods
  (modes) as powers, and the five dark wedges are borrowed-chord supers. Keys change by travel and
  by the day, never by flick, so the gesture means one thing.
- **Does the wheel rotate** The seven powers stay fixed under the thumb and the ring of twelve keys
  turns behind them each morning. Combat muscle memory beats a literal chart, and the lake map keeps
  the fixed geography.
- **Lands and keys (shape 3)** Twelve lands are twelve fixed keys, and temperaments span mirrored
  pairs: pure near home, then fifths, sweet thirds, well-tempered, and equal at the seam. Tuning
  reads as distance from home.
- **Volta's calendar and the ring** Each day the open arc of seven lands moves one station clockwise
  and the sun rises over the day's land. The spiral becomes both a geography and a schedule.
- **Whose key wins (shape 1)** The day's key sets what is in tune, the land's key sets what is
  native there, your mood sets your home within the day, and a borrowed chord lasts one phrase. That
  gives clear precedence that can be heard.
- **Direction of the spiral** Sharpward only until the thirteenth morning, then free each night. The
  first turn stays legible, and the freedom is the ending's gift.
- **Length and reliving** About twenty minutes a day, suspendable. Each night offers Repeat (relive
  the key: events restart, resolutions persist) or Onward (a fifth up). That gives a long game and
  knowledge-loop depth, and the spiral stays twelve steps.
- **How many echoes** Four kept takes, like noodles' four tracks, which is enough for chords without
  chaos.
- **Echo tuning and age** Always retuned into today. Age shows as fading blue and lower, softer
  parts, never sourness, per Morgan's ear rule.
- **Paradoxes** An echo whose path breaks waits and hums in tune, and Drive can nudge it on. Nothing
  explodes.
- **Hoarding echoes** At eleven days an unreleased echo leaves at dusk, howls for a few seconds and
  joins the Wolf. The comma is felt as consequence, not sustained sound.
- **One breath for everything** One breath for singing, wheels, sprint and glide, refilled by
  silence. Notes per breath (three to seven) is the main growth axis, which is noodles' ladder as
  progression.
- **Health and HUD** No HUD. Health is steadiness heard as a wavering tone, and the time is the song
  section and the sun.
- **What the Grid means now** Sameness: it grows on days relived identically and fields Greys and
  the Metronome, and swing beats it. The game is in tune by default, so the Grid cannot mean 'in
  tune'.
- **Where the Grid and Wolf live** Both at the seam: the Metronome hangs under the treaty bridge and
  the Wolf lives in the crack. One place holds both failures of closure.
- **Voices: how many and what kinds** Five, one per vowel from bass to treble, loosely Rock, Water,
  Leaf and Fungal plus a light voice, with you at the centre. Vowels make each voice audibly
  recolour everything.
- **Who the beloved is** An NPC with a three-day life on a triangle across the lake, who carries the
  third. In duet the two players are also each other's third.
- **Which hidden layers to keep** Sky, mirrored deep and the boat shortcut across the lake, with the
  thirds frontier only after the ending and finer equal temperaments as islets. Each layer carries
  weight; the rest was cut.
- **The lake's level and memory** A low lake forgets: songs fade before they come round and phrases
  end on almost-endings, and freeing rivers raises it. This makes wasatch's rule and the delay-line
  memory one thing.
- **The downbeat's cost** The clap needs a full phrase in the pocket, and otherwise it is only a
  clap, so it is never a panic button.
- **Dream shortcuts** Placed by hand and revealed only when your day touched both ends. Safe for
  level design, and still feels found.
- **What sleep improves** Timing windows for practised moves widen slightly, capped, and never gate
  progress. That rewards rest without forcing it.
- **Combat arithmetic** Hits drain colour toward grey, a grey foe leans on a held dominant, and a
  cadence on the downbeat resolves it. One readable loop, seen and heard.
- **Difficulty** Gentle or fierce, chosen any night: gentle allows comfort instead of fighting, and
  fierce tightens timing and keeps best takes. This serves both the calm player and the hungry one.
- **Two consoles that can't hear each other** They trust the count-in; drift is heard as flams and
  fixed with a clap, and each game is complete alone. There is no mic or network, so ritual stands
  in for sensing.
- **Logic depth and reading the bells** The rhythm machines are optional, and the bells on three,
  four and five beat cycles teach the schedules by sound. That gives depth for the obsessive, with
  nothing on screen.
- **Stepped animation** Creatures move on twos when calm, and attack wind-ups play on ones. Handmade
  feel without hurting readability.
- **Engine and architecture** Native code on SDL2 and GLES3 with a deterministic fixed-step
  simulation, as the toys prove. Echoes, replays, trades and duets then become input logs.
