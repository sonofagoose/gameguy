_Every part of the design so far, in one place, at its full ambition. Written on 2026-11-17, when
Morgan asked for "a seriously AAA game... like the scope of Tears of the Kingdom", asked "what about
"river" and the "time loop" and all that?", and asked me to "ensure you've got the full

## The brief

- **The first ask** (`shape-1-a-musical-zelda.md`): "a Tears of the Kingdom ruled by music where the
  nature of existence and love and humanity and the very fabric of space and time is revealed
  through the lens of music and harmony and math and sound and love and dissonance".
- **Who it's for:** Morgan first, JJ second, anyone with an R36S third.
- **What it should be:** "deep and beautiful and challenging and calming and puzzling", for one
  console or two, "without trade-offs".
- **Today:** "a seriously AAA game... like the scope of Tears of the Kingdom".

## Scope, in one breath

Tears of the Kingdom's scope is space: one enormous map in three layers. This game's scope is space,
time and key. A ring of lands in three layers is lived across thirteen mornings, and each morning
moves the whole world a fifth. Any morning can be lived again, as often as you like, and the world
remembers every day. So the garden is seven different places on the seven mornings it is open, six
across the hours of one day, and different again with your past selves walking in it.

## What playing it is like

### The first evening

Black, and a drip. You call, and a ring of light runs out. Something answers. You breathe with it,
letting go on its tick, until a third note sounds that neither of you sang: "Oh. You're in." Until
then the world has been flat, a page seen from above. Now it stands up around you. The garden is at
dawn, in the key of C, with asters as tall as cathedrals. The hose is knotted, and the knot is
moving. You hold the wheel and the world lies back down so you can read it. You stand where the
knot's strands meet, strike on the beat and let the cadence fall home. Water runs, and for a second
a blue figure sits on the pool's edge. At dusk the river carries you home and sings your day back to
you, and you keep a take. (Canon section 2; `the-page-and-the-place.md`; `the-wheel.md`.)

### Going out: mornings one to seven

- **Each morning the sun rises over the next land, a fifth higher:** the Alfalfa and Box Elder, the
  Glass Hills and Doorstop, the Bell Meadow and the first pack, the Highway and the vine at the
  Stage, the Chrome Nave and its old machines, and the Seam with the Metronome under its bridge.
- **Each morning brings a new voice, a new mood and a new land.** Seven lands are open and bright.
  Five are dim, reachable but strange, their doors wanting a borrowed chord.
- **You live mornings again to finish them.** A Repeat restarts the day's events, keeps what you
  resolved, and your kept selves walk beside you.

### The way home: mornings eight to twelve (`the-way-home.md`)

- **The sun rises doubled,** two discs a hair apart that take a little longer to agree each morning.
- **8, the Aster Steps:** your own garden under glass. Hum's voice cracks.
- **9, Verbena Hollow:** the close that misses, the vine across the pack, chords as triangles.
- **10, the Rain Steps:** a cloud that kept everything. It rains what you let go.
- **11, the Reed Mouths:** be the ground. Your band fights while you hold one note.
- **12, the Porch:** the evening before. Your oldest echo at the door, and the only night that
  doesn't close.

### The thirteenth morning

The garden comes back a comma sharp, and everyone is there. On the wheel you walk the whole turn in
one breath, a land a bar. Then there are three gestures: the clap (the Grid's offer, every day the
same), the step (the Wolf's, pure and alone) and the hold (the duet with the vine). The two bleak
ones can be taken; afterwards the river offers the morning again (`the-wheel.md`, canon section 10).

### After: days that don't repeat

- Each night you choose up a fifth, down a fifth, or stay.
- Past the lake, lands tuned by thirds that never repeat.
- The sky, the deep, the Shimmer and the islands, for whoever goes looking.
- The old machines that think in rhythm, the Fierce takes to beat, and the songbook to fill.

### How long

- **The imagined players** took five to eleven weeks of evenings and breaks to reach the thirteenth
  morning (`playthroughs/`).
- **At Tears of the Kingdom's scope** a morning holds more than one day can do, so most mornings are
  lived several times, as Majora's Mask's three days are. Six to nine times each, at twenty minutes
  a day, makes about 25 to 40 hours to the ending. That is a target, not a measurement.
- **After the ending** the days don't run out.

## The loop and the river

The loop is the most designed part of the game. It comes from Morgan's first and sixth favourites
(Majora's Mask and Outer Wilds, brainstorm 7), his stage ("bass and guitar as the loops stack"),
noodles' four tracks and the Generative Self's repeat sign (`shape-2-volta.md`).

### A day

- **About twenty minutes, shaped like a song:** dawn intro, morning verse, noon chorus, afternoon
  bridge, dusk chorus, night outro. The music and the sun are the clock. You can stop anywhere, and
  you can end a day early by sleeping at the river.
- **Each morning comes a pure fifth higher.** The sun rises over the day's land, and the arc of
  seven open lands moves one station round the lake.
- **Lives keep their own cycles.** The vine lives a three-day life round a triangle and shares your
  sun every fourth morning (1, 5, 9, 13). The caravan shares it every third (1, 4, 7, 10, 13). Lives
  on cycles of three and four meet every twelfth day, and the garden's bells, on three, four and
  five beats, meet every sixtieth beat. Learning who is where, when, is progress
  (`memory-in-rhythm.md`, idea 9; `the-way-home.md`).
- **Drip hums you back in.** After days away, Drip hums your last few minutes in ten seconds
  (`experience-fixes.md`, 7).

### At dusk

- **The river carries you home** and sings your day back as a half-minute song of your own melodies,
  fast, never higher. After a big win it runs backwards from the win, so you hear which moves earned
  it (`memory-in-rhythm.md`, idea 1).
- **You keep up to four takes.** The ring floats on the water with your takes on it. Hover one and
  you hear the chord it would make with the ones you are keeping (`experience-fixes.md`, 9).
- **The rest walk into the water, and the water keeps them.** A still pool plays a released take for
  one phrase when you sing it the pool's note. The vine can only hum back what you let go. On the
  tenth morning it rains them.
- **A post carved in the world's old script** offers a repeat sign or a bracket:
  - **Repeat:** live this day again. Its events restart, what you resolved stays resolved, and your
    kept selves walk beside you.
  - **Onward:** a fifth up.

### What the loop remembers

- **Echoes.** A kept take walks beside you, in sun-print blue, retuned into today's key, so the band
  always sounds good. Echoes hold plates, sing the other notes of a chord to open a gate, and land
  strikes where they landed before. Which days you keep is composing.
- **Echoes age by mornings travelled.** Each is a blue dot on the wheel, on the wedge of the day it
  was sung. It sets on its third morning and rises again on its eighth, doubled. A Repeat doesn't
  turn the wheel, so it doesn't age an echo (`experience-fixes.md`, 1).
- **The eleventh dusk.** At eleven fifths from where it was sung, an echo is the wolf interval. It
  steps in front of you and waits one bar. Let it go into the water, sing it again (your singing
  becomes today's take), or turn away, and it goes to the Wolf.
- **The Grid is sameness.** On a Repeat, wherever you retrace the earlier telling in step, the
  ground pales underfoot. At night the river sings both tellings together. Only a day that changed
  nothing sets grey, Greys walk only on grey, and living the day differently lifts it
  (`experience-fixes.md`, 2).
- **Dreams** join two places your day touched into a way you never walked, traced in blue at dawn,
  or wake you humming a tune made of your own motifs.
- **Sleep** makes what you practised a little easier: wider timing windows, capped, never needed to
  progress.
- **Pools** hold what happened beside them. Sing one its note and the past plays on its surface.
- **The lake** is a memory that circles. A song sung at dawn comes round across the water at noon. A
  low lake forgets: songs fade before they come round, phrases end on almost-endings, and afterglow
  dies fast. Each freed river raises it, and a full lake lets the music come home
  (`drawn-in-light.md`, rule 4).

### The two mornings that break the pattern

- **The twelfth night** is the only one whose song doesn't close. It stops on the chord before home
  and waits until morning.
- **The thirteenth morning** is B♯: on a piano C, here a comma sharp. Every life's cycle lines up
  and everyone is at the garden. After the duet, the fourteenth morning is the first day that isn't
  a repeat.

## The world

### The ring, in the order the sun visits it

| morning | land | key | tuning | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the Garden | C | pure | asters, the Mirror Pool, the knotted hose |
| 2 | the Alfalfa | G | pure | a forest of alfalfa; Box Elder |
| 3 | the Glass Hills | D | pure fifths | ringing crystal ridges; Doorstop holding a gate open for nobody |
| 4 | the Bell Meadow | A | sweet thirds | bell-flowers as big as houses; the first pack |
| 5 | the Highway | E | sweet thirds | salt flats at 2am, red only in tail lights; the Stage under one red bulb; Patio |
| 6 | the Chrome Nave | B | well-tempered | a car's interior as big as a cathedral; old machines that think in rhythm |
| 7 | the Seam | F♯/G♭ | equal | the treaty city on its bridge; the Metronome beneath it; the Wolf in the crack |
| 8 | the Aster Steps | D♭ | well-tempered | terraced asters; a garden under glass |
| 9 | Verbena Hollow | A♭ | sweet thirds | violet thickets; the wolf's valley |
| 10 | the Rain Steps | E♭ | sweet thirds | terraces where clouds rest; Puffball |
| 11 | the Reed Mouths | B♭ | pure fifths | reed deltas, drones and chant |
| 12 | the Porch | F | pure | leaves turning by a porch; a chair still rocking |
| 13 | the Garden | B♯ | pure, a comma sharp | home and not home |

Tuning is distance from home: pure at the garden, more tempered round the rim, equal at the Seam,
the way history went. Each land plays only the music that sounds best in its own tuning. In light, a
pure interval's figure stands still and a tempered one turns, so you see the tuning too
(`drawn-in-light.md`, rule 2).

### Beyond the ring

- **The lake:** a day's walk round and a short sail across. The far shore is the circle's hidden
  shortcut, a ferry. Islets in it are tuned in finer equal temperaments, treaties that almost work.
- **The sky:** the untempered harmonic series, gated by a note that isn't on the wheel. Raise a held
  A forty octaves and it arrives as orange light.
- **The deep,** through the Mirror Pool: the world reflected, major turned minor, all blue, and at
  its bottom a country with no octave.
- **The Shimmer:** a bronze grove on the water, tuned in pairs that beat on purpose. The one place
  where wobble is the beauty.
- **The forty octaves:** the coda climbs every octave, from a petal's standing waves to the young
  universe ringing.
- **The lands of thirds,** after the ending, which never repeat.

## What you can do

- **One note, and the wheel.** Hum sings one note; the wheel decides where home is, and everything
  else is what it is to you by how far round the ring it sits (`the-wheel.md`). Hold R1: time
  thickens, the place folds to the page, and you put home on a wedge.
- **Seven moods are seven powers:** wonder (Lift), sunny home (Bind), drive (Drive), cool soul
  (Flow), tenderness (Remember), fierceness (Strike), the darkest (Break). They never move under
  your thumb; the ring of twelve turns behind them each morning, so the wheel is also compass and
  calendar.
- **Five voices are who sings,** bass to treble: Doorstop, Drip, Box Elder, Puffball, Patio. Mood is
  what and voice is who: Lift with Doorstop raises a boulder, with Patio it raises you.
- **Under the wheel, borrowing.** The five dark wedges are the notes that have set. Borrow one and
  the land comes with it for a phrase: borrow the Rain Steps and it rains. The first borrow, home on
  the third morning, is free.
- **The swell** (hold R2): it grows a colour each bar, and a nudge on the downbeat walks home one
  wedge, bringing what you pass with it. Where home is when you let go decides which of four
  cadences lands. One breath holds three homes at first and grows by one each time you land a longer
  walk, up to seven, and the ferry makes eight.
- **Building is synchrony.** Things pulsing in time with you move as one machine; parts in three
  against two walk; your past selves hold plates and sing the rest of a chord.
- **The world's physics is acoustics:** resonance, standing waves (two voices facing on one note
  hold things up), cancelling, the Doppler effect. Find a giant's rhythm and push on the beat until
  it swings.
- **Leading, not forcing.** Walk steadily and creatures fall into step until there's a parade. Clap
  (L1 and R1) after a full phrase in the pocket and everything near resets to the same point in its
  cycle.
- **Talking is getting in phase.** Strangers mumble until you breathe with them. Every line is sung,
  and the seven moods are seven answers.
- **The songbook** keeps the phrases you capture. Names wait there to be asked for.

## Fights

- **On the beat.** Every hit rings the enemy at its own note. Dodge on the beat and time slows into
  a flurry where each strike is a note of a chord. A strike you time yourself rings fuller than one
  the game placed.
- **Nothing dies; it transforms.** Hits drain colour, a grey creature leans, and a cadence on the
  downbeat brings it home. The mood you bring it home in is what it leaves: an updraft, company,
  speed, water, a memory, sparks, or something shattered open.
- **Wolves** are made of the comma, and resolved they become pups that drum. **Knots** are fear,
  tangled rhythms; stand where their strands meet. **Greys** are the Grid's soldiers, marching
  exactly on the grid; swing breaks them, and standing a third away brings their colour back.
- **Bosses are pieces of music:** the Great Knot (the hose, five against seven), the Metronome (it
  learns you, and swing breaks it) and the Wolf at the Seam (every echo you turned away from). The
  way home has a real test each morning on Fierce.
- **Gentle or Fierce,** chosen any night. Gentle widens the timing and the ledges, leans home for
  you, and lets most fights end by comfort. Fierce tightens it and keeps your best takes to beat.

## Who you meet

Drip, the drop that never finished falling and keeps time on your head. Box Elder, a sapling who
talks too much and sings the tune. Doorstop, an iron wedge with one word a day. Puffball, a mushroom
wired to everything underground. Patio, a strand of patio bulbs that flies like a kite. The vine,
the one who remembers you: blurred and doubled out of tune, a body in tune, carrying the third your
fifths can't make. Jeanne and Caravan No. 27, who trade in notes off the wheel and in what you let
go. The white cat at the forks, the fly on your knee, the old machines, the blue figure on the
pool's edge, and someone in the Porch's chair. Who the vine is, who sat on the pool's edge and who
sat in the chair are Morgan's to say.

## Sound and picture

- **One set of numbers drives both.** Pitch class is hue, by fifths. Octave is height, loudness is
  brightness, and silence is dark. An interval is the figure between two voices, still when pure and
  turning when tempered. Timbre is the texture of a line, and phase is things moving together (canon
  section 12, `drawn-in-light.md`).
- **Drawn in light.** The world is drawn the way an oscilloscope draws a sound, a beam on black.
  Afterglow is memory. Depth is the third note. Hum is the only solid thing, plaster in a world of
  light. The Grid is the pixel, and the Wolf is a doubled beam with the only red in the world in its
  eyes.
- **The page and the place** are one world on one hinge. Hold the wheel and it lies down to be read;
  let go and it stands up around you.
- **For the ears:** consonant by default; dissonance brief, always going somewhere; the comma felt,
  heard only in a few earned seconds; echoes always retuned into today.
- **All of music theory, without a lesson** (canon section 11, `the-wheel.md`), from intervals and
  the circle of fifths to cadences, borrowed chords, polyrhythm, counterpoint, temperament and the
  comma.

## Tears of the Kingdom's scope, on this handheld

### What it has, and what answers it here

| Tears of the Kingdom | this game |
|---|---|
| a physics engine | intervals as physics (kin is weightless, a fifth above falls toward you, straight across repels), and acoustics: resonance, standing waves, cancelling, the Doppler effect |
| a "chemistry engine": fire burns grass | borrowed weather (rain, ringing ridges, 2am), and what a creature leaves by the mood it came home in |
| abilities on a hold-to-slow wheel | the mood wheel: seven powers, multiplied by five voices |
| building with Ultrahand | binding by rhythm, walkers in three against two, and your own past selves as the other hands |
| shrines | gates that want chords, pools, the old machines' logic, giants that swing |
| sky islands, surface, depths | the sky, the ring, the deep |
| one huge map | a ring of about twenty-five places, across thirteen mornings and six hours of the day, each morning in a new key |
| not in it | time that loops and spirals, sound as the world's law, your own past as a band, and another console as your third |

What shape 1 judged out of reach still is: thousands of physics objects at once, and a painted look
at television resolution. Lines of light were chosen for this screen (`drawn-in-light.md`), not as a
fallback.

### How big it would be

Tears of the Kingdom needs 16.3 GB on a Switch. Most of that, as with any large modern game, is the
stuff of its look and sound: textures, models and animation, recorded music and voices, cutscenes,
and languages (general knowledge, not measured). Here the look is drawn from rules, the sound is
made live, there is one line of text and no video. What stays is the world itself, built by hand.

My earlier estimate (`the-card.md`, this morning) put the whole game under 150 MB. It left out
exactly that: the places, built by hand at this density. Folding them into "code" was wrong.
Estimated again:

| part | size | how it's estimated |
|---|---|---|
| the program: rules, sound, drawing | 5 to 30 MB | each toy is 3.9 to 4.6 MB with its debugging information |
| the places, built by hand: the twelve lands, the lake and its islets, the sky, the deep, the Shimmer, the Chrome Nave's machines, the Seam above and below, the coda | 0.1 to 0.5 GB | about 25 places at 5 to 20 MB each. A square kilometre of ground with a height every metre or half metre is 1 to 8 MB. Its drawn lines, 0.2 to 1 million points, are 2 to 8 MB. What stands where, 10,000 to 50,000 things, is 0.5 to 3 MB. Schedules, events and songs come to under 1 MB. |
| the lands of thirds, after the ending | under 1 MB | made from rules and a seed |
| sound, all made live (the canon) | nothing stored | |
| recorded sound, if Morgan wants it | 0.1 to 3 GB | ambience for every place and hour (25 × 6 × 2 minutes) is 0.3 GB as Opus, 1.5 GB lossless. Instruments or voices sampled from his own recordings are 0.1 to 1 GB. |
| type and words | about 1 MB | |
| **the game** | **0.1 to 0.5 GB with sound made live; up to about 4 GB with recordings** | |

On top of that, the card keeps the world's memory: every take, the dawn saves and the book. That is
under 4 GB a year at an hour a day (`the-card.md`). It also keeps every version of the program (5 to
30 MB each), and of any place that changed, so any old take still replays.

So a game with Tears of the Kingdom's scope fits on the card several times over. That's not a
smaller ambition: the same scope is carried by rules and sound instead of by stored pictures. If
Morgan wants the card to be full of something that matters, the thing that would fill it is his own
recorded sound.

### What really limits it here

| limit | measured | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| memory | 918 MiB, about 815 free when a game starts; the canon's budget is 300 MB | a place and its neighbours in memory at once, the rest read from the card as you walk |
| drawing | the page toy draws about 1,400 marks a frame with room for about 22,000; the meadow drew 60,000 to 90,000 triangles at full rate | how much of a place can shine at once |
| the processor | the rules and the picture's points: 1.3 to 3.6 ms of a 17.1 ms frame; 800 to 1,000 synth voices on one core | room for many creatures and a large band |
| reading the card | 43 MB/s, and about a hundredth of the processor's work that the built-in storage costs | a 5 to 20 MB place loads in under half a second, in the background |

Storage isn't the limit. Memory, the frame and the screen are.

### Where it lives: on its own card

Morgan's instinct is right: the card should be the game's.

- **Size.** At the top of the range the game outgrows the built-in storage, where games go (`/roms`:
  774 MB free on the purple).
- **Smoothness.** Reading the built-in storage costs the processor 1,100 to 5,000 interrupts per 256
  KB, and that is what stalled frames earlier (`docs/hardware-target.md`). Reading the card costs 12
  or fewer. A world that streams in as you walk has to stream from the card.
- **Safety.** A good card of the game's own, not the stock card shared with thousands of other
  games, which can fail (`the-card.md`). The handheld still keeps its own copy of the world.
- **For anyone with an R36S,** the card can be the cartridge: the game, its world and its book on
  one card, sold or given as one thing. The launcher in Ports is a few kilobytes and finds it.
