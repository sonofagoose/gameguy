# Promises of the Garden

_Part of the promise book (`../promises.md`). The Garden is the first land and the thirteenth
morning's (`../world.md`, 1). Its eight lives are listed from `../people.md` and are sized when
stage 2 begins._

### `garden.land` The Garden, home

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "Asters, reeds, the Mirror Pool, a brass spigot and a knotted hose."
  "tuning | pure" "made of | water and stems: pools, the hose, bell-headed asters, reeds" "shows
  best | all seven laws, gently, one at a time" "metre | four, with Drip's swing" "why they sing |
  the morning song" "the songbook credits | the pure tuning of people singing with no instruments"
  "its river | the hose's water, which fills the Mirror Pool" "lends | everything rings, and asters
  come up through whatever ground you are on" "new here | Drip; wonder and sunny home; stand where
  they meet; the first close; the river"
- **Stage:** 2
- **Scene:** asters
- **The moment:** Every morning of the game begins here, so it has to be a place you are glad to
  wake in. A yard after a storm, a kilometre and a half across: the brass spigot standing over it
  like a monument, the hose lying across it like a green river you can hear, the Mirror Pool black
  and still, reeds as tall as towers at its edge, and asters everywhere, their bells answering
  whatever moves. It is tuned pure, so nothing in it beats: chords here stand perfectly still. It
  counts in four, with Drip a little late on the off-beats. By the end of the first week you could
  draw it from memory, and it still has corners you have not stood in.
- **Far:** from any other land, the Garden is the green light on the north shore.
- **At its foot:** the spigot, ten metres of brass, dripping.
- **Inside:** the Hose, the Mirror Pool, the crowns of the asters.
- **After:** on the thirteenth morning you come back to it a comma sharp, and know every stalk.
- **A cheat would be:** a tutorial level: small, flat, and left behind after the first hour.
- **Full size:**
  - `fact asters.count >= 60` The proof field holds this many asters; the Garden's score will place
    its own.
  - `owed` The Garden's score: about three square kilometres (`size.md`), every thing in it at its
    real size times a hundred, and a run across it of at least five minutes.
  - `owed` Pure tuning, measured: every chord its things can make holds still, to within a hundredth
    of a beat a second.
  - `owed` Its metre: four, with Drip's swing, in everything that moves there.
  - `owed` Its river freed fills the Mirror Pool and raises the lake.
  - `owed` Borrowed from another land, it lends what the atlas says: everything rings, and asters
    come up through the ground you are on.
  - `owed` What every land owes (`promises.md`): something calls you from everywhere; every piece
    seen; every sound with a yes.
  - `owed` The songbook's credit, checked against sources before it is written.
- **Asks:** Is the Garden somewhere you are glad to wake up?

### `garden.ground` What the Garden's ground wears

- **Said:** `spec/shape.md` "bends away as you pass and springs back in your wake, shedding dew that
  lands a moment later: your path is heard just after you" "On moss your call goes half as far and
  you are dim" "Each leaf is a drum skin: rain plays it, and when you hum under one its figure shows
  on its underside" "puddles are mirrors, and each can be crossed on its still lines when you hum
  its note" "A dry leaf rattles when you step on it and tips like a seesaw" "No cover is the main
  cover of two lands."
- **Stage:** 2
- **Scene:** evening
- **The moment:** You never walk the same ground for long. Through the lawn the blades are trees
  around you, and behind you, a moment after you pass, their dew lands with small high notes, so
  your path is heard trailing you. Then a field of last year's leaves, each a curled raft longer
  than a bus, and every step plays one: each leaf its own note, so crossing the field is a tune you
  didn't choose. Then you walk in under clover, a green roof on threads, and the sky goes. On moss
  everything hushes: your call goes half as far, you dim, and it is the place to rest. Crossing the
  Garden is learning what its ground does.
- **Far:** the leaf field glows amber in the middle distance, the clover is a low dark roof, the
  lawn a forest of blades.
- **At its foot:** each cover at Hum's height: a blade as wide as a door, a leaf longer than a bus,
  a clover stalk you could climb.
- **Inside:** under clover, a roof you hum up into; on moss, a quiet you can hide in.
- **After:** you choose your way across a land by what its ground will do.
- **A cheat would be:** five textures that look different and do nothing; sounds of footsteps
  changed by surface.
- **Full size:**
  - `test on moss a call carries half as far`
  - `test fallen leaves ring underfoot and lawn sheds dew behind you, each seen at once and heard as
    late as it is far`
  - `frame evening-far` Lawn, leaf fall and clover in one view, each drawn as itself.
  - `frame evening-foot` The great aster's foot under the clover's roof.
  - `owed` Clover: each leaf a drum skin; rain plays it; humming under one shows its figure on its
    underside.
  - `owed` Puddles in the bare soil: mirrors, crossed on their still lines (`garden.w04`).
  - `owed` A leaf tips like a seesaw when stepped on, and the lawn's blades bend away and spring
    back.
  - `owed` Moss as a place to hide: whatever hunts by pitch loses you there.
  - `owed` The Garden's own shape: a bowl a kilometre and a half across, with the Mirror Pool at its
    lowest point (`shape.md`). The proof field is flat.
- **Asks:** Did the ground under you feel different from one patch to the next?

### `garden.asters` Asters as tall as cathedrals

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "Asters are as tall as cathedrals."
- **Stage:** 2
- **Scene:** asters
- **The moment:** The page has just stood up, and what were small circles on the ground are towers.
  The nearest aster is a mast as thick as you are tall, going up out of sight. You stop at its foot
  and the view leans back by itself, and back, until the crown is overhead: dozens of flowers, each
  one a bell wider than you are tall. You call. Your ring runs out along the ground, and it also
  climbs: a band of light goes up the stalk, leaf by leaf, for more than two seconds before it
  reaches the flowers. Then the crown lights in its own colours, and as long again after that, the
  sound of its bells comes down to you, the low ones first. You have just measured a cathedral with
  your own voice.
- **Far:** the asters are the skyline: thin masts with lit crowns, fainter the farther they stand.
  When any bell among them rings you see it at once and hear it late, the way far fireworks are.
- **At its foot:** the stalk is a metre through. Leaves five to ten metres long leave it every few
  metres, in a spiral, all the way up. Walking, you can't see the top; it has left the frame.
- **Inside:** the leaves are a stair. You climb the spiral, and each leaf dips under you and sounds
  its own low note. The crown is a belfry: branches you can walk, hung with bells, and from up there
  the lake and every land's lights round it.
- **After:** the skyline is a set of bells you know by height and by how late they answer. Ring one
  from its foot and its kin answer across the garden, each when your sound has reached it and its
  own has come back.
- **A cheat would be:** a tall model. A flower scaled up beside the path, the same height as every
  other, with one bell sound that plays the moment you press, a top you can see without looking up,
  and nothing to climb.
- **Full size:**
  - `fact asters.tallest_m >= 150` As tall as the tallest churches ever built: Cologne Cathedral is
    157 m and Ulm Minster's spire 161.5 m. A real New England aster is 0.9 to 1.85 m, and the world
    is one to a hundred.
  - `fact asters.shortest_m >= 90` None is merely big.
  - `fact asters.count >= 60` A skyline of them, no two the same height.
  - `fact asters.great_climb_s >= 2.4` How long your call takes to climb the great one, at 60 m a
    second.
  - `fact asters.top_out_of_frame == 1` Walking at its foot, the crown is above the top of the
    screen.
  - `fact asters.hum_px_walking <= 26` Walking, Hum is small on the screen: under a twentieth of its
    height.
  - `fact asters.leaned_bell_px >= 24` Leaned back with the gaze at the crown, the smallest bell is
    a shape on the screen and not a spark.
  - `test asters stand as tall as cathedrals, one to a hundred`
  - `test a call climbs a tower, and its bells are heard late by their height`
  - `take` The scripted player walks to the great aster, stops at its foot, calls, and is still
    standing there when the bells are heard.
  - `frame evening-far` The skyline, from where the page stood up.
  - `frame evening-foot` At the foot, walking: the stalk leaves the top of the frame.
  - `frame evening-lean` Stopped: the view leaned back, the crown overhead.
  - `frame evening-answer` The crown lit by your call, in its own colours.
  - `fact handheld.asters.late == 0` On the handheld, every frame of the walk and the call is on
    time.
  - `owed` Climbing by the leaves; each leaf's own note; the crown as a place to stand; the lake
    seen from it.
  - `owed` The swing (`garden.w07`): the tower sways at its own rate and its bells ring when it
    does.
  - `owed` Each aster's shape from the Garden's score, by rule and by hand. The proof field is made
    by rule alone.
- **Asks:** Is it as tall as a cathedral?

### `garden.great` The Hose

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "Its great instrument: the Hose." "They meet every thirty-fifth beat,
  and that is the one you clap." "A pipe stopped by its own knot." "Inside, its standing wave is the
  floor plan" "Kinks change its length, and its length changes which points are still." "At its
  heart the Great Knot ties five against seven." "Opened, the hose sounds an octave higher and every
  overtone comes back."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** You walk into the open end of the hose standing up, because it is as wide as a
  door. Inside it is a tunnel that goes on for two and a half kilometres, curving, lit only by what
  sounds. The air in it moves: every few seconds a wind comes down the pipe hard enough to carry you
  off, and between the winds there are places where the air doesn't move at all. Those places are
  evenly spaced. You learn to run from one to the next. A kink ahead shortens the pipe, and all the
  still places move. At the heart of it the Great Knot is counting five against seven, and you clap
  it on the one beat in thirty-five where they meet. Then the water comes, and the whole pipe, with
  you in it, sounds an octave higher.
- **Far:** a green line across the whole garden, from the spigot to the pool, humming one low hollow
  note you can hear from anywhere.
- **At its foot:** its mouth, a door's height, breathing in and out.
- **Inside:** its standing wave is the floor plan.
- **After:** the pool fills, the lake rises a little, and the garden's lowest note is an octave up
  and bright.
- **A cheat would be:** a corridor level with fans on timers; a rhythm game at the end; a swapped
  sound when it is over.
- **Full size:**
  - `test the hose runs from the spigot to its end in the reeds, at least a kilometre and a half`
    It lies across the Garden from the spigot on the back rise to its coiled end in the reeds, its
    curve worked out from the score's points when the game is built: 1,519 m, its bore 1.8 m, a
    door's height (`game/scores/garden.txt`, `game/src/world/hose.zig`).
  - `owed` Hum walks inside it upright, and it takes at least four minutes.
  - `owed` The still places and the winds are the pipe's own standing wave, worked out from its
    length. Change its length at a kink and they move to where the sum says.
  - `owed` Stopped, it sounds its odd overtones only; opened, all of them, an octave higher. A test.
  - `owed` Five against seven meet every thirty-fifth beat, and nowhere else. A test.
  - `owed` The scripted player crosses it by the still places, and is blown back when it doesn't.
  - `owed` On Gentle it can be sat with: the Knot comes undone by breathing with it.
- **Asks:** Inside the Hose, could you tell where it was safe to stand before the wind came?

### `garden.w01` The answering field

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "Ring one aster and every aster kin to it answers across the garden."
- **Stage:** 2
- **Scene:** asters
- **The moment:** You ring one tower. Nothing, for a breath. Then its kin begin to answer from
  across the garden, the near ones first, in their own colours, and the answers keep arriving for
  half a minute, from further and further away, each one softer, until the last is a single low bell
  from a place you haven't been.
- **A cheat would be:** every aster flashing at once; a chord sample.
- **Full size:**
  - `test kin answer a call and strangers don't`
  - `fact asters.field_m >= 1000` The proof field is over a kilometre across, so the farthest
    answers take more than fifteen seconds to come back.
  - `owed` Ringing a tower itself, by a strike at its foot, and its kin answering it, not only Hum's
    voice.
  - `owed` The answers are heard softer and duller with distance, and never louder together than the
    first.
- **Asks:** Did the answers coming in from far away make the garden feel big?

### `garden.w02` The seed that follows

### `garden.w04` The puddle bridge

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "Hum a pool's note and walk its still lines."
- **Stage:** 2
- **Scene:** asters
- **The moment:** (From the room: it is behind you as the day begins, half a minute's walk south;
  turn round with the right stick.) A puddle from the storm is a lake two hundred metres across, and
  black, with gold
  pollen scattered on it. You hum, and lines of light come up on the water: a ring, and a cross.
  They
  are where the water isn't moving, and the pollen streams onto them. You step out on one. Either
  side
  of you the water is heaving. Half a minute later you are across. Hum the note above and the bridge
  redraws itself somewhere else.
- **A cheat would be:** a bridge that appears when a button is held near the water.
- **Full size:**
  - `test a round skin's still rings and spokes are where the Bessel functions are nought` The
    figure is the real one: a round skin's shapes are Bessel functions, and the still lines are
    their
    zeros, the same overtones the skin in the sound has.
  - `test a pool's figure is still on its spokes and rings, and heaves to one at its highest` The
    garden's note stands in the bridge puddle as a ring and a cross, with a belly toward the singer.
  - `test a pool hummed at its note stands in that shape, and holds Hum on its still line across`
    Humming builds the shape (law 7); out on the still line Hum is held there all the way over, in
    under forty-five seconds.
  - `test a belly throws Hum back onto the still line, and still water floats Hum to the shore`
    Walking up into a belly is turned back; when the note stops and the water stills, it floats you
    ashore.
  - `test pollen on a standing pool gathers on its still lines, and stays there when it stills`
    Whatever is on the water is thrown onto the still lines, and a puddle keeps the figure of the
    last note that stood in it.
  - `test a call makes a puddle stand for a moment in the shape of the call's note` A call passing
    over a puddle makes it flash its figure and sing it as it dies away.
  - `fact ears.pool_beats_hz <= 0.1` Read back from the sound: the water sings the very note it is
    sung, so the two never beat.
  - `fact ears.pool_near_note_db <= -30` Nothing a little off the note, which would be heard as
    roughness.
  - `fact ears.pool_adds_db >= 3` The note blooms as the water stands: measured at about four
    decibels louder. Whether that is too much or too little is for a person to say.
  - `take` The scripted player hums at the bridge puddle's edge until the figure stands, walks round
    the shore to the end of a still spoke, and crosses on it.
  - `frame evening-pool` The puddle standing in its shape, the still lines drawn on the water and
    the pollen gathered on them, seen from over the shore.
  - `frame evening-bridge` Hum out on the still line, the water heaving either side.
  - `test a puddle silent to the garden's note stands when home is moved to wonder` Moving home
    on the wheel changes Hum's note, and the puddles redraw: the small one stands as a cross, and
    the
    fifth, sized for F, stands only then.
  - `frame evening-wonder` Home on wonder: the F puddle standing as a ring and a cross.
  - `owed` The swell's step to the next wedge on a downbeat (`swell.walk`), so the bridge redraws
    while you are on it.
  - `owed` A pool of any outline has its own figures, worked out from its outline by
    `game/tools/modes`. The proof's puddles are round.
  - `owed` Lose the note and the lines fade under you, slowly enough to run for it: the water holds
    for about three and a half seconds after the note stops; whether that is enough is for a person
    to say.
  - `owed` The water mirrors the lights near it (`spec/shape.md`): Hum's light is not yet in it.
- **Asks:** When the lines came up on the water, did you trust them enough to step out?

  note until the two fight, and at one exact place the drone vanishes, and with it the light: a ball

- **A cheat would be:** a mute button with a dark circle.
- **Full size:**

  - `owed` It is dark there because nothing is sounding there, by the same number.

### `garden.w06` The far shore, late

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "Call across the water and count until the answer."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** You stand at the edge of the Mirror Pool and call. Your ring goes out over the
  water and you watch it go, getting small. Five seconds. It touches the far reeds and they light.
  Five more. Then their answer reaches you. You call again and count, and it is the same count, and
  you understand that you have just measured the pool.
- **A cheat would be:** an echo with a delay somebody typed in.
- **Full size:**
  - `test an answer is heard when it has travelled there and back`
  - `owed` The Mirror Pool is 300 m across where you first stand: ten seconds there and back. From
    other places on its shore the count is different, and right.
- **Asks:** Did you count?

### `garden.w07` The swing

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "Push an aster on its own beat until it rings."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** You lean on a tower with your voice and it moves a hand's width and comes back.
  You push again as it comes back. A 150-metre aster sways once in about five seconds, so this takes
  patience: six pushes, half a minute, each one on time. Then the crown is travelling metres through
  the sky and its bells begin to ring by themselves, and the whole garden's kin answer.
- **A cheat would be:** a bar that fills; any timing accepted.
- **Full size:**
  - `owed` A push in time adds and a push out of time takes away (`whole.law7`), and the tower's own
    rate comes from its height.
  - `owed` The bells are rung by the swing itself, each when its own clapper meets it, so a wider
    swing rings more of them.
  - `owed` From the top (`garden.asters`, inside) the swing can be ridden.
- **Asks:** Did swinging something that big feel like timing, not strength?

### `garden.w09` The drip's secret

- **Said:** `spec/world.md` "A falling drop makes no sound." "The plink is the bubble it traps,

- **The moment:** The view leans in on the spigot and time thickens. A drop the size of your head
  lets go and falls in silence, and meets the water in silence. Then a bubble closes under the

- **Full size:**
  - `owed` The note sounds when the bubble closes, not when the drop lands: late by a moment you can

  - `owed` A bubble twice as wide sings an octave lower. A test.

### `garden.w11` Three against two
