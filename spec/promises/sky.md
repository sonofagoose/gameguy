# Promises of the sky

_Part of the promise book (`../promises.md`). What `../sky.md` says, sized as it is built._

### `sky.dawn` The first dawn

- **Said:** `spec/sky.md` "Rung n hangs at 100 × log2(n) metres." "Ring n is 1,850 ÷ n metres in
  radius." "Every seventh rung is dark." "Every rung at or below the sun sounds." "Rungs 2 to 6 are
  the five notes a bugle has, over the drone." "A rung takes the hue of its note, by fifths, like
  everything else." "Dawn travels at the speed of sound."
- **Stage:** 2
- **Scene:** evening
- **The moment:** The world has just stood up, and it is still nearly night. The asters are black
  shapes against a sky that is beginning to glow low down, toward the lake. A line of green light is
  running toward you across the ground from the shore: the dawn, at the speed of sound, lighting
  things as it passes them, far ones first. Over the lake a thin green ring lies along the shore. A
  minute and a half later a second ring lights a hundred metres up, as wide as the lake; then,
  higher and smaller, a yellow-green one, a green one, one thin red one, a yellow-green one. Each
  lights the instant the sun reaches it, and its note comes over the water later, as late as it is
  far, so the sky's chord is seen first and heard after, the way fireworks are. Nothing lights where
  the seventh ring should be. Above them all a thread of light goes up out of sight.
- **Far:** the stack of rings over the lake, from anywhere on the shore.
- **At its foot:** the shore at sunrise, with the first ring a hundred metres overhead.
- **Inside:** standing on a ring's still places, and being thrown by its bellies.
- **After:** you tell the hour by how many rings are lit.
- **A cheat would be:** a sky gradient on a timer, a sun sprite, and a fanfare played the moment the
  screen brightens.
- **Full size:**
  - `test the horn's rungs stand at the heights of the overtones, and the seventh never sounds` With
    the sum `sky.md` rests on: a belly's throw always clears the gap to the next rung and never the
    one after.
  - `test the sun climbs the horn: each rung lights where it stands at once, and is heard late by
    its distance`
  - `test nothing sounds without its light: every sound in a scripted take was told as a cue` Every
    rung's note is a cue, with its light.
  - `take` The scripted player turns to the lake, watches the rungs light from one to six, and hears
    the sixth's note arrive.
  - `frame evening-far` The world standing up into the dawn: black shapes against the first light,
    and the dawn's edge crossing the ground.
  - `frame evening-horn` The horn over the lake: six rings lit in the hues of their notes, the
    seventh dark, the thread above.
  - `owed` The sun's figure changing with home: three lobes by two with home a fifth under it, a
    rosette in the dark moods. It is drawn now as a plain ring, rising at your feet on the first
    morning and climbing the horn, which is right only while home is on the day's note.
  - `owed` The sun's held note, and the whistle that steps upward all morning. Whether a drone under
    every day sounds good is for Morgan's ear.
  - `owed` The afternoon: the sky closing from the top. The rings as places, with still places to
    stand on and bellies that throw.
  - `owed` The reefs: what the air holds after a storm, hanging in the still places.
  - `owed` On the handheld: every frame of the dawn on time.
- **Asks:** Did you stop to watch the sky come up?

### `sky.bee` The bee

- **Said:** `spec/sky.md` "Speed is an interval." "a fifth for a bee" "Size is pitch." "followed.
  Its line leads to what is ripe."
- **Stage:** 2
- **Scene:** evening
- **The moment:** Something small and bright is working the clover, low over the roof of leaves, and
  you hear it before you find it: a hum on the garden's own note. It comes your way and the hum
  rises; it goes past and the hum drops, a fifth from coming to going, as plainly as a car passing
  on a road. It hovers over each flower in a little figure of eight, and when it lands among a
  crown's bells they answer it, softly, in its note. You follow it, because where it goes something
  is ripe.
- **Far:** a small light moving low over the clover, and a hum that comes and goes.
- **At its foot:** a bee as long as you are, hovering at the height of a two-storey house, its wings
  drawing figures of eight.
- **Inside:** riding it: the slow bus of the crowns (`sky.md`), owed.
- **After:** you know a flyer by how far its note falls as it passes.
- **A cheat would be:** a bee sprite on a looping path with a recorded buzz panned across.
- **Full size:**
  - `test the bee's note falls a fifth as it passes` Read from its buzz: coming, a third high;
    going, a sixth low.
  - `test nothing sounds without its light: every sound in a scripted take was told as a cue` Every
    buzz is a cue, with its light at the bee in its own note.
  - `take` The scripted player hears the bee within sixty metres.
  - `frame evening-bee` The bee hovering over the clover, its wings' figures of eight.
  - `owed` Its dance: a figure of eight whose tilt is the bearing from the sun and whose length is
    the distance, pointing to what is ripe.
  - `owed` Riding it from crown to crown; the bumblebee's buzz that shakes a flower open.
  - `owed` The other flyers of `sky.md`, each its own kind: midges, mosquitoes that tune a fifth,
    the dragonfly, swallows, the thrush, crickets, fireflies, bats.
- **Asks:** When the bee went past, did its note drop?
