# Promises of the laws

_Part of the promise book (`../promises.md`). What `../laws.md` says, law by law, sized before the
stage that finishes it. `whole.md` holds the promise that the laws are one model; these hold each
law to its own words._

### `laws.things` What a thing is

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "Every thing in the world is a body with a place, and any number of two
  kinds of part:" "Resonators. A resonator has a family (the shape it is), a lowest note, and how
  long it rings." "Its family gives its overtones." "The sound thread renders each overtone as one
  small filter; the world keeps each overtone's loudness, 60 times a second, for the rules and the
  picture." "Pendulums. A pendulum has a rate, counted in beats, and a place in its cycle." "A
  creature's gait, a blinker, a rocking chair, a swinging bell-flower and a life's round of mornings
  are all pendulums at different sizes of time." "Hum is a body too: one resonator (its voice) and
  one pendulum (its pulse)."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** A bell-flower sways on its stalk and rings when it swings; the same thing is a
  pendulum and a resonator, and you can push either.
- **A cheat would be:** separate systems for sounds and for things that move.
- **Full size:**
  - `test a thing answers by its own overtones, whatever its family` A family gives the overtones.
  - `test the same cues make the same samples` Each overtone one small filter in the sound thread.
  - `owed` The world keeps each overtone's loudness sixty times a second, for the rules and the
    picture; today it keeps one loudness for each thing.
  - `owed` Pendulums: a rate in beats and a place in the cycle, for gaits, blinkers, chairs,
    bell-flowers and rounds of mornings, one kind of part for all of them.
- **Asks:** nobody: the tests answer it.

### `laws.sympathy` Law 1: sympathy

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "Sound flows between things that share overtones." "How much two
  overtones share. If they are d cents apart, they share exp(-(d/15)^2): everything at a pure match,
  98% at 2 cents (an equal-tempered fifth), 43% at 14 cents (an equal-tempered third), under a tenth
  at 23 cents (a comma), nothing at a semitone." "The width, 15 cents, is to tune." "How sound
  flows. Each step, a sounding overtone feeds every overtone it shares with, in proportion to its
  own loudness, how much they share, the receiving material, and how near they are (halving at 8
  paces, to tune)." "Felt, petals and moss take almost nothing; glass, chrome and taut strings take
  a
  lot." "Ring one thing and its kin answer across the place, each in its own light: a different
  constellation for every note." "Pure lands ring. Tempered lands are duller, and at the Seam" "You
  can only move what your voice shares
  something with." "Doorstop's oo has few overtones and reaches octaves and fifths." "Patio's ee
  has many and reaches far more, faintly."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** You call in the dark and a scatter of lights answers across the field, each in its
  own colour: a constellation that belongs to your note. Ring one of them and a different
  constellation answers.
- **A cheat would be:** answers chosen from a list for each note.
- **Full size:**
  - `test kin answer a call and strangers don't` Sound flows only where overtones are shared.
  - `test a rung bell rings its kin, and they ring theirs, more faintly each time (law 1)` Between
    things, not only from Hum.
  - `test on moss a call carries half as far` Moss takes the sound in.
  - `owed` A test: how much two overtones share is exp(-(d/15)^2), with the numbers the law gives at
    2, 14 and 23 cents and a semitone.
  - `owed` Every overtone feeding every overtone it shares with each step, by loudness, share,
    material and nearness, rather than each thing as a whole (`laws.things`).
  - `owed` Pure lands ring and tempered lands are duller, measured with `ears` (stage 3).
  - `owed` Doorstop's oo reaches octaves and fifths; Patio's ee far more, faintly (`voices.five`).
- **Asks:** When you called, did the answers feel like your note's own?

### `laws.standing-waves` Law 4: standing waves

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "A driven thing shows still lines and bellies." "Along a line (a string,

  note dies the path goes with it, so a path lasts as long as someone or something keeps singing:
  you, an echo, a loop, a machine." "Between two voices facing each other on one note: still places

  - `test a pool hummed at its note stands in that shape, and holds Hum on its still line across`

  - `test a belly throws Hum back onto the still line, and still water floats Hum to the shore` A

  - `frame evening-pool` Drawn as lines of light, as bright as the shape is loud.

### `laws.building-up` Law 7: building up

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "Push a thing at its own rate and it grows." "The rule. A push within an
  eighth of a cycle of the right moment adds its whole size." "The gain falls to nothing at a
  quarter of a cycle off, and beyond that the push takes energy out." "Between pushes the thing
  loses a share that depends on how long it rings." "Three thresholds for each thing: it rings (it
  lights and sounds), it swings (it moves what is on it), it gives (glass breaks, a knot opens, a
  seed-head scatters)." "The same law at both sizes of time. A bell-flower's sway is pushed on the
  beat." "A wine glass is pushed 500 times a second by holding its note." "Taking it out. Pushes
  against the swing calm it." "Gentle fights end this way."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** You push a bell-flower on the beat and its sway grows until it swings and rings;
  push against it and it calms. Hold a glass's note and it grows until it gives.
- **A cheat would be:** a counter that fills while a button is held.
- **Full size:**
  - `test a pool hummed at its note stands in that shape, and holds Hum on its still line across`
    Held at its note, a puddle's shape grows to full in about a second and a half, and dies away
    when the note stops.
  - `owed` A test: a push within an eighth of a cycle adds its whole size, at a quarter of a cycle
    nothing, and beyond that it takes energy out; between pushes a share is lost by how long the
    thing rings.
  - `owed` A test: each thing's three thresholds, ring, swing and give, met in turn by pushes on the
    beat, and calmed by pushes against it.
  - `owed` The same rule for a pendulum pushed on the beat and a resonator held at its note.
- **Asks:** Did pushing on the beat feel like it mattered?

### `texture.materials` What a thing is made of

- **Said:** `spec/texture.md` "The material is the part of a sound that is not its note: how it
  starts, how fast it dies, how much is noise." "So it is the part of a line that is not its
  shape." "water | soft start, wavering, bubbles | a line that is never quite still, with no
  corners" "stem and leaf | a soft knock, quickly gone | a soft-edged line, pale, in its note's
  colour, with a short glow" "glass | a hard start and a very long ring | a line with a hard edge
  and a spark at each corner, whose glow lasts half a minute" "bronze and brass | a warm start; two
  notes a hair apart in every bell | a warm line that shimmers slowly: it beats against itself"
  "silk | high and thin, ringing long | a fine line beaded with dew" "wood | a knock, short and warm
  | a line of short strokes along the grain, with almost no glow" "reed | a breath with a buzz in
  it, odd overtones | two walls with a blur of breath at the mouth" "chrome | bright, hard,
  everything back at once | a line and its copy" "concrete | dull, dead on the beat | a line drawn
  in
  stairs: the Grid's line" "tarmac and salt | a click or a hiss, no note | dots, in no colour" "moss
  and petal | nothing: they take sound in | short soft strokes that give no glow and dim what is
  near
  them" "A click has no colour. The shorter a sound, the less it has a note (world.md, the Alfalfa's
  twelfth wonder), so the less it has a hue." "Salt, chaff and gravel are bone-white dots." "A long
  pure tone is a full colour." "Two things of one shape and different stuff are told apart at a
  glance and by ear, by the same difference."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** Two bells of the same shape stand side by side, one bronze and one glass. Before
  you ring them you can see which is which, and when you ring them you hear the same difference.
- **A cheat would be:** materials as colours.
- **Full size:**
  - `owed` A material in the world: how a thing's sound starts, how fast it dies and how much is
    noise, and the same numbers drawing its line's edge, glow and grain, for each row of the
    table, one sum for both.
  - `owed` A test: the shorter a sound, the less colour its light has; a long pure tone is a full
    colour.
  - `frame evening-pool` Water: a line never quite still, with no corners.
- **Asks:** Could you tell what a thing was made of before you rang it?

### `laws.gravity` Law 2: gravity

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "An overtone falls toward the note that contains it." "The rule. Take a
  thing's lowest note and your home." "If the thing's note is home's 3rd, 5th or 7th overtone (in
  any octave), the thing belongs to home, and home pulls it." "If home is the thing's 3rd, 5th or
  7th overtone, you belong to it, and it pulls you." "The pull is one third, one fifth or one
  seventh of full strength." "On the wheel this is the canon's table:" "on your wedge | kin | the
  same note, or its octave | weightless to you; it keeps your time" "one wedge right (a fifth above)
  | it leans to you | it is home's third overtone | it falls toward you; a full close brings it home"
  "one wedge left (a fifth below) | you lean to it | home is its third overtone | you fall toward
  it; an amen sings it to sleep" "two wedges | passing | a ninth overtone: too far to hold | you
  pass
  through each other" "three or four wedges | warm | at four, a fifth overtone; at three, an
  overtone
  shared with neither containing the other | it doesn't move: it takes colour and grows" "five
  wedges | grinding | a half step: rough | it shakes, and pushes away a little" "straight across |
  far | nothing shared, and rough | your blows throw it, and its blows throw you" "Moving home
  changes everything at once. That is the wheel's whole power, and it is why the same boulder is
  weightless one morning and falls toward you the next." "The seventh. A thing that sits on home's
  seventh overtone is off the wheel, 31 cents flat of the piano's seventh." "It is pulled faintly
  and from far away." "The caravan trades in these."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** A boulder you couldn't shift yesterday falls toward you this morning, because home
  has moved a fifth; one wedge round it was weightless.
- **A cheat would be:** a table of which things move toward Hum.
- **Full size:**
  - `owed` A test: a thing whose note is home's 3rd, 5th or 7th overtone, in any octave, is pulled
    toward home at a third, a fifth or a seventh of full strength; when home is the thing's
    overtone, Hum is pulled toward it.
  - `owed` A test: the canon's table comes out of the rule for each distance round the wheel, with
    nothing looked up.
  - `owed` A test: moving home changes every pull at once; the same boulder is weightless one
    morning and falls toward Hum the next.
  - `owed` The seventh, 31 cents flat of the piano's, pulled faintly from far away (stage 3, the
    caravan).
- **Asks:** Did things moving toward you or away feel like the music, or like a rule?

### `laws.in-step` Law 3: falling into step

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "Things at nearly the same rate pull each other together." "Pendulums.
  Each step, a pendulum's place in its cycle moves by its own rate, plus a pull toward each
  neighbour it can feel: pull × sin(the gap between them)." "Two lock when the difference in their
  rates is smaller than the pull." "Pulls, to tune: Hum's pulse on a creature in reach, once Hum has
  kept steady for a bar: 0.5." "Between creatures of one kind: 0.05." "Between parts Hum has bound:
  1." "The clap sets everything in reach to the start of its cycle." "Voices. A voice marked as one
  of a crowd (reeds, a choir, a pack that has lain down) that is within a quarter tone of a louder,
  steady note slides onto it over a few beats." "Nothing else in the game ever slides in pitch by
  itself, so the music stays in tune." "What follows: a parade behind steady steps; parts that move
  as one machine while they share a beat; a bank of reeds finding your drone; hearts in step."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** You walk steadily for a bar and the creatures behind you fall into your step, a
  parade; a bank of reeds slides onto your held note and hums it with you.
- **A cheat would be:** followers that copy Hum's position.
- **Full size:**
  - `test four breaths on the tick bring the drip's fifth true, and the figure stops` After the
    lock the figure falls into step: it settles on the nearer of its two open places (law 3).
  - `owed` A test: pendulums move by their rate plus pull × sin of the gap, and two lock when their
    rates differ by less than the pull; the pulls the law gives.
  - `owed` A test: the clap sets everything in reach to the start of its cycle (`fight.looper`).
  - `owed` A test: a crowd's voice within a quarter tone of a louder steady note slides onto it over
    a few beats, and nothing else in the game slides in pitch by itself.
- **Asks:** Did you ever notice things falling into step with you?

### `laws.cancelling` Law 5: cancelling and beating

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "Opposite sounds make silence, which is dark." "Near-misses beat, and the
  beating turns things." "Cancelling. The same overtone from two sources half a cycle apart sums to
  nothing." "Hum can sing the mirror of a sound it has leaned in to, and for as long as its breath
  lasts there is a small ball of silence round it: dark, unheard, safe." "Beating. Two overtones a
  little apart beat at the difference between them." "The figure between the two turns once per
  beat." "Sharp turns it one way and flat the other." "Turning does work. A turning figure bound to
  a
  thing turns the thing." "none: a pure pair | locks. A clamp, a held door, a bridge that stays |
  the
  Garden, and pure pairs anywhere" "under 8 a second | turns: wheels, winches, walkways, a slow fan
  |
  fifths in tempered lands (0.9 a second in equal temperament at middle C, 2.4 in sweet thirds) and
  the comma (3.6)" "8 to 30 a second | shakes: a sieve, a shimmer, sand that creeps | thirds in
  tempered lands (10 in equal temperament, 16 in pure fifths)" "over 30 a second | roughness: it
  pushes the two apart | half steps, and straight across" "So tuning is machinery. In the Garden
  nothing turns by itself." "In the Glass Hills fifths clamp and thirds shake." "At the Seam
  everything turns, and the city runs on it: the error it agreed to share is its engine."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** Beside Drip the figure between your notes turns, once for each beat, slower as you
  come into tune, until it stops and locks. Later, in a tempered land, a fifth that never quite
  locks turns a winch for you.
- **A cheat would be:** a turning animation with no beat behind it.
- **Full size:**
  - `test four breaths on the tick bring the drip's fifth true, and the figure stops` The figure
    turns at the beat rate and stops when the pair is pure.
  - `fact ears.breath1_beats >= 1.6` The beating read back from the sound itself.
  - `owed` A test: the same overtone from two sources half a cycle apart sums to nothing, and the
    silence is dark; Hum's mirror note makes a ball of silence round it while its breath lasts.
  - `owed` A test: a turning figure bound to a thing turns the thing, one way when sharp and the
    other when flat; under eight beats a second it turns, from eight to thirty it shakes, over
    thirty
    it pushes apart.
  - `owed` The Garden, where nothing turns by itself; the Glass Hills and the Seam (stage 3).
- **Asks:** When the figure stopped turning, did you feel it?

### `laws.travel` Law 6: travel

- **Said:** `spec/laws.md` "Sound moves at a speed you can see." "Speed. About ten times Hum's run
  (to
  tune)." "A call's ring crosses a clearing in a breath and the lake in about a minute (62 seconds,
  size.md)." "Late arrivals. What happens far away is heard late, and seen when the sound arrives."
  "Echoes. A wall sends a sound back after twice its distance." "The return lands on the beat: the
  engine moves it to the nearest bar line, so an echo wall is a looper whose length is how far away
  you stand." "Fast roads. Sound in metal, glass and taut wire moves fifteen times faster." "Sing
  into a pipe and its far end sings at once." "Speed bends pitch. Something coming at you sounds
  higher and something leaving sounds lower." "At a ninth of the speed of sound the bend is a whole
  step, at a fifth it is a pure third, at a quarter a fourth, at a third a fifth, at a half an
  octave." "Things that pass (a truck, the black train) bend as they pass." "Hum's own note bends
  only on rides built for it, where the ride's speed is one of those fractions." "Night over water.
  Sound carries further across the lake at night, when the air over the water is cool." "The lake's
  own slow wave is separate: a song sung on the shore travels round the lake and comes back across
  the water about a quarter of a day later, softer and lower (design/memory-in-rhythm.md, idea 5)."
- **Stage:** 2
- **The moment:** You call, and watch the ring of light run out over the ground at a speed you can
  follow; a bell far off flashes when the ring reaches it, and you hear it a moment later. The bee
  goes by and its hum falls a fifth.
- **A cheat would be:** sounds that play instantly wherever they happen.
- **Full size:**
  - `test an answer is heard when it has travelled there and back` Late arrivals.
  - `test a call climbs a tower, and its bells are heard late by their height` A speed you can see.
  - `test the bee's note falls a fifth as it passes` Speed bends pitch: at a fifth of the speed of
    sound, a third high coming and a sixth low going, a pure fifth from coming to going.
  - `fact sound.speed_m_s == 60` Ten times Hum's run.
  - `owed` A test: a wall sends a sound back after twice its distance, moved to the nearest bar
    line, so an echo wall is a looper.
  - `owed` Fast roads: in metal, glass and taut wire sound moves fifteen times faster; a pipe's far
    end sings at once.
  - `owed` Rides built for Hum's own note to bend; night over water carrying further; the lake's
    slow wave a quarter of a day round (stage 3).
- **Asks:** Did the sound's speed feel like part of the world?
