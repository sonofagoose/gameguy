# Two consoles

_Part of the specification (`../SPEC.md`, section 14). `design/together.md` holds the first design
and its reasons, and stands wherever this file is silent. Morgan, 2026-11-19: "we can't count on two
unconnected console's speakers or two untrained people's ears to carry the weight of the multiplayer
experience... let's not hold ourselves back here". So this file puts the whole weight on what is
measured and certain: two consoles joined by a cable, running one world._

## One rule

A friend in the room is a live echo, and an echo is a friend from another day. So anything two
people can do together, one person can do with an echo, later and with more care, and the game stays
whole alone. No door, fight or land ever needs the second console.

## What it never leans on

- **Not on two speakers in a room.** Nothing depends on the sound of two unlinked consoles meeting
  in the air, or on anyone's ear being trained enough to hear it. The comma in real air, which the
  specification had, is out.
- **Not on a microphone.** The game never listens to the room. The idea that it might hear a player
  through a headset is out.
- **Not on rumble.** These handhelds have no motor (Morgan).
- **Not on a network.** There is none.

## The cable

Measured (`docs/hardware-target.md`): a round trip between two units in 0.47 ms, with none lost in
5,050.

- **One world.** Both consoles run the same world from both players' hands, step for step, each
  waiting for the other's hands before it steps. A take made on one is the same take on the other.
- **One clock.** The guest's sound follows the host's.
- **Two pairs of eyes and ears.** Each screen is one player's eyes and each speaker is one player's
  ears. So you hear your friend late by exactly how far apart you are, and you see their call coming
  across the ground toward you before you hear it. Being far from each other is something you can
  feel.
- **Two homes.** Each of you has your own home on the wheel. A creature's shape is its interval to
  your home, so the same creature is a hoop on one screen and a pretzel on the other, and where it
  goes is pulled by both of you. Put home on the same wedge and you see one world. Hold different
  wedges and the gap between you is something to use.
- **Nothing is lost when the cable comes out.** Both consoles hold the whole world. The one who is
  gone turns blue, finishes the phrase as an echo, and waits, humming in tune.

## Three ways to be in one world

### A visit

One of you steps into the other's day (`design/together.md`, "A visit, from the couch").

- **The host's world, the guest's self.** The day, the key, the lands and whatever changes are the
  host's. The guest brings their own Hum: their home, their voices, their breath, and up to four of
  their echoes.
- **The guest's own day waits,** sun and all, and their echoes don't age while they are away.
- **At the host's dusk the river sings the day with both voices.** The host can keep the guest's
  walk as one of their four echoes. The guest can keep their own.
- **Both books get the page.**

### A world for two

New here, and the largest thing two consoles do: a whole world that belongs to both of you.

- **It begins in the dark, apart.** Two Hums wake in the same black field, out of sight of each
  other. The first thing either of you does in it is call. The second call is always answered, and
  this time it is a person answering. You find each other by how late the answers are.
- **It is the whole game:** the first evening, the thirteen mornings, every land, every life, the
  ending. Nothing in it is cut down for two and nothing in it is shut to one.
- **It lives on both cards** and moves on only when the two of you are linked. Between visits it
  waits exactly where you left it.
- **Each of you keeps your own four echoes,** so its band is ten: two of you and eight past selves.
- **Its lives know two people.** A life's three wants (`years.md`) can be met by either of you, and
  a tune one of you carried across the lake can be finished by the other.
- **Its thirteenth morning is held by two.** The hold, which alone is a duet with the vine, is here
  a duet between two people, with both vines beside you.
- **Either console can host it,** once both can host (below).

### The water you share

After the ending, for two people who each finished alone: one stretch of the lands by thirds that
lives on both cards and grows when they are linked. Thirds are the interval the other person brings
(`design/together.md`).

## Apart

- **Letters.** A take is a few hundred kilobytes. On a card, by hand or by post, a friend's takes
  walk your lake in blue, and age like any echo.
- **Each other's worlds, kept.** After any link, each card keeps the other's world as it stood that
  day (`size.md`). You can open it from the book and walk in it when they are not there. Their
  echoes are in it, walking their old days. Their light is on its ground. You change nothing there,
  with one exception: a phrase played at their river is waiting for them the next time you link, and
  it grows a plant of yours in their garden.
- **A friend's echo can stand in your band.** One of your four can be somebody else's past self.

## Two seats

- **A second Hum.**
- **Drip.** The second console plays the drop that rides on Hum's head: it keeps the time, points
  the way with a ring of light, sings the thirds and holds a note under Hum. There is nothing to
  steer. It is for a parent with a child, for someone who has never held a controller, for anyone
  who wants to be in the music without the thumbs. Alone, the game plays Drip.

More than two, through a hub, is later (`../SPEC.md`, 16).

## What two can do

Each line comes from a law (`laws.md`). The right-hand column is how one player does the same thing
alone.

| together | the law | alone |
|---|---|---|
| **Find each other in the dark** by calling, and by how late the answer is | travel | call for Drip |
| **Hold something in the air between you:** two Hums facing on one note make a standing wave that carries a bridge, or lifts a boulder from both sides | standing waves | you and an echo, facing |
| **One lifts and one draws:** kin to one of you a boulder is weightless; a fifth from the other it falls toward them | gravity | move home between the lift and the pull |
| **Drive a wheel between you:** one holds a note pure, the other leans a hair off it, and the wheel turns once for every beat | cancelling and beating | a tempered pair in the world |
| **Take a strand each:** a knot is two rhythms that stumble. Each takes one, and you strike together where they meet. The Great Knot: one holds the five, the other the seven | falling into step | the wheel puts both strands on one home |
| **Swing a giant in half the time,** pushing turn and turn about | building up | every push your own |
| **Sail by two homes:** the boat is pulled by both, so two can hold a course one can't | gravity | tack by moving home |
| **Question and answer:** a door that wants a phrase that stops halfway and an answer that comes home | sympathy | your echo asks, you answer |
| **Carry the cadence:** when one swell runs out of breath the other's lands it | | a longer breath |
| **One holds, one sings,** then swap | | your echo holds |
| **Two bands:** ten voices for one chord | | five |

## Trading fours

The one contest: four bars each, back and forth, the way musicians both compete and play together.
The land's creatures drift toward whoever's phrase comes home best, and the round ends in one
cadence you both release. The tug and the pocket duel in `design/together.md` stay out unless Morgan
wants them.

## The loop, with two

As `design/together.md` has it:

- **In a visit** each of you keeps your own loop: your mornings, your echoes, your river.
- **In a world for two** there is one river, and it sings the day back with both your voices.
- **Onward together** keeps your mornings in step.
- **A day you shared can be lived again:** the friend's echo walks it, or the friend comes back
  live.
- **A friend's echo ages like your own,** and on its eleventh dusk it steps in front of you like any
  other.
