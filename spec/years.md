# The years

_Part of the specification (`../SPEC.md`, section 11). Morgan, 2026-11-19: "I played in Majora's
Mask for 7 years before 100% the game... what we're building should vastly vastly absolutely
immensely surpass that game as far as content and design elegance and like everything and like the
emotional attachment that people may feel while playing it and like the feelings of Discovery and
like sadness and happiness like all feelings really". This file is how the game is deep enough to
live in for years. It adds to `people.md` and takes nothing from it. The craft is learned from that
game; nothing of its world, its people, its music or its look is used._

## What a small world known by heart does

Five things make a small world hold a person for years, and none of them is size.

1. **People keep their own time.** They are where they are whether you come or not, and you learn
   them by watching.
2. **Helping takes being there:** the right place, the right morning, with the right thing in hand.
3. **You can't be everywhere.** Choosing one kindness is missing another.
4. **You know it by heart,** and it still has corners.
5. **Some things are only noticed the tenth time.**

This game already has the first two: every life is a sum from the morning's number, and helping is
the day's five steps (`people.md`). What follows gives it the rest, and goes further in four ways no
world made of written scenes can: a life is music, so it can grow, meet others and be handed down by
rule; a take is its inputs, so any day can be walked again; the laws hold for everyone, so you can
be anyone; and every dawn is kept (`size.md`), so nothing you saw is ever gone.

## Three wants

A life in `people.md` has one want: an ending its tune can't reach. That is its first. Each life has
three, one inside the other.

| want | what it is | when it can be met | what meeting it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **of its tune** | the ending its phrase can't reach | from the first morning you hear it | its part joins the land's dusk song; it lends a small voice (`people.md`) |
| **of its tie** | something between it and another life, in another land | once both their tunes have their endings | the two rounds change: they now meet, where before they missed; their duet joins both lands' songs |
| **of its years** | what it is becoming | late: once its ties are settled and it has grown old enough | it becomes something that stays (below), and its whole song, sixteen bars, is sung at the thirteenth morning's gathering by everyone |

- **A met want changes the tune.** A phrase that has found its ending grows a second phrase, and
  that one stops short too. A life you have helped three times sings a whole song, and you can hear
  your own three evenings in it.
- **Nobody asks,** as before. A life sings what it sings. The second and third wants are never
  announced: they are heard, in a tune that has started to lean toward somebody.
- **Ninety-six lives, three wants each:** 288 endings, written by hand in the lands' scores.

## Ties

Every life is tied to two, three or four others, and at least one tie crosses the lake.

- **What a tie is:** kin, a teacher, a lost friend, a rival, a debt, the other half of a duet, a
  thing left behind. Peg's sock under a seat in the Chrome Nave is one (`people.md`).
- **A tie is heard before it is understood.** Two tied lives share a few notes. Keep both phrases
  and on the page they overlap.
- **Ties are why the lake matters.** One life's ending is the other's beginning, so a tune carried
  across the water is the commonest errand in the game, and nobody ever sends you on it.
- **The web is written in the scores,** one line for each tie. About 150 ties (an estimate: 96 lives
  with about three each, each tie counted once).

## Meetings

When two lives' rounds put them in the same place on the same morning, they sing together.

- **Every pair that can meet has a duet.** It is made from their two tunes by the rules musicians
  use for two voices: together on the strong beats, apart between them, never crossing. It is made
  in that land's tuning and metre.
- **Tied pairs have a written scene** as well: what happens between them, sung, with one line of
  caption at most.
- **The almanac shows meetings to come,** the way a calendar shows eclipses. Some are every twelve
  mornings. Some are every 420. The brood and its cousins wake together every 221 (`world.md`).
- **A meeting happens whether you are there or not.** Be there and you can keep it. Miss it and it
  is past: the ground remembers that they met (`size.md`), and a pool beside the place will play a
  little of it back, the way a pool does. You get the memory and not the moment.
- **How many pairs ever meet** is a sum over the rounds in `people.md`. It is counted when the
  lands' scores are written; no number is claimed here.

## Being them

Once a life's first want is met, you can live a morning as that life.

- **How.** At night, sing its tune back at the river. You wake as it, where it wakes, and at dusk
  the river brings you home as yourself.
- **Its body.** You move the way it moves, at its size.
- **Its ears.** You hear what it hears. Moss hears only what is low. A fly hears only what is high
  and close. The same garden is a different piece of music to each of them.
- **Its time.** Every life lives at its own octave of time, and this is the forty octaves made
  personal (`world.md`). To something slow, a morning is a minute, and the world's rhythms run so
  fast they are notes. To something quick, a breath is an hour, and every note comes apart into its
  beats.
- **Its round.** You go where it goes, and you meet who it meets, from its side.

| kind of body | which lives | what being it is |
|---|---|---|
| a walker | Pill, the travellers | your own size; Pill curls at every surprise and rolls |
| a crawler | Snail | slow: the garden's song is a blur of high notes, and a morning is over in two minutes |
| a flyer | the fly, Patio | quick: one bell stroke lasts a minute, and you can fly between its overtones |
| rooted | Moss, the Twins, Verbena | you can't go anywhere. You sway, you ring, and everything comes to you |
| turning | Sprinkler | you go round in five while the land counts four, and feel where they meet |
| many as one | the Ants, the Brood | you are forty. You steer by agreeing |
| carried | Peg on its line | you go where the wind takes you, and sing what it makes of you |

- **You never choose a note as them either.** You choose moments. Their notes are theirs.
- **Some wonders can only be found from inside another life.** The songbook has a kind of page for
  them: the thing, as that one hears it.
- **It costs the engine three numbers:** a rate for time, a band for hearing, and a kind of body.
  The laws do the rest.

## Generations

"Nothing dies. Things transform" (`../SPEC.md`, 5). Over years, the lives do.

- **A life has a span,** counted in mornings: most between two hundred and two thousand. The Brood's
  is thirteen. Nobody's ends inside the first turn.
- **When it ends, the life becomes a thing in the world that keeps its tune.** Snail's shell is a
  horn on the east shore that sounds its long line when the wind is in it. The Twins' stem comes
  down across a stream and hums one tune a beat apart when you cross it. What it becomes depends on
  which of its wants were met.
- **Somewhere a new life begins.** Its tune is made from the tunes of two lives that were tied, by
  the ways tunes are varied: their halves exchanged, one turned upside down, one at half speed. It
  is in its land's tuning and metre and sung in its own vowel.
- **What you did is handed down.** An ending you helped a life reach is in its child's tune from the
  first note. An ending it never reached is the child's first want.
- **The first ninety-six are written by hand. Those after are made by rule,** and each made tune has
  to pass the same tests a written one does (`game/tools/ears`: in metre, singable, nothing rough
  that isn't going somewhere).
- **After years the lake is ringed with the grandchildren of the lives you first met.** You can hear
  your own first weeks in what they sing.
- **And the first ones are not gone.** Every dawn is kept (`size.md`). Open the book at the morning
  you met Snail and walk it again: there it is, a morning late for everything.

## What is kept and what is past

This is where the sadness is, and it is never a punishment.

- **Nothing you did is undone.** What you resolved stays resolved. There is no reset.
- **But time is real.** Meetings pass. Lives change. You can't be on both shores at dusk.
- **What you were there for can be walked again** for as long as the card lasts: your take, in that
  morning as it was.
- **What you missed can be heard about and not seen:** in the ground's light, in a pool, in a
  child's tune.
- **On Gentle** the almanac marks a meeting you are about to miss for good, once, quietly.
- **Nothing can be failed for good by a slip.** No step of any want has to be done right the first
  time, and no want is closed by a mistake.
- **Nothing is counted for you.** The songbook shows what you have found and never how much is
  left.

## The great year

The calendar is a temperament: rounds that almost agree (`world.md`).

| what lines up | how often |
|---|---|
| rounds of three and four | every 12 mornings |
| three, four and five | every 60 |
| three, four, five and seven | every 420 |
| the brood and its cousins | every 221 |
| all of them | every 92,820 mornings: more than two hundred and fifty years of a morning every day |

- **Nobody lives to see the great year.** The almanac can show it. A rooted life old enough can
  dream it: be an old tree for one breath and the whole turn goes by as a chord.
- **Each kind of lining-up has its own gathering,** written once and sung by whoever is alive then.

## What a person finds in year three

The first turn is sixty to a hundred hours (`../SPEC.md`, 6.5). The years after are not a grind laid
on top of it: they are what the calendar's own sums take, playing days that are worth playing.

| page of the songbook | how many | what it takes |
|---|---|---|
| wonders | 144 | finding each by doing it |
| great instruments | 12 | going inside, and setting each right |
| lives' wants | 288 | three for each of the first ninety-six |
| meetings kept | a sum over the rounds | being there |
| the thing, as that one hears it | one for each life you can be | a morning as each |
| what a life became | 96, and then their children's | years |
| the far gardens | 5 | nights of a fifth each: 41, then 53, then 306, then 665: nearly two years of a night every night for the last |
| the gatherings | one for each lining-up | waiting for the calendar: 12 mornings, 60, 221, 420 |
| the islets, the sky's rungs, the deep | 8, and no end | going looking |
| lands past the lake | no end | a fifth, a third or a seventh at a time |

These lengths are sums, not measurements. Whether the days in them are worth playing is for the room
to find out, a stage at a time.

## What stays Morgan's

- **Whether lives change over years at all.** This file says yes. If he would rather the ninety-six
  stay as they are for ever, the rest of this file stands without it.
- **Any life he wants to write himself,** or name, or take out.
- **Who the vine is.** The vine does not have a span, does not change, and is nobody's parent.
