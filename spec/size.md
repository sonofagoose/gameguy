# How big

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** History, from the specification of 2026-11-19: how big the world is and
> what the card is for. Not decided by the direction. Morgan's words in it stand as his ambition.
> What the game is: `design/the-game.md`.

_Part of the specification (`../SPEC.md`, sections 12 and 13). Morgan, 2026-11-19: "we can be
maximal instead of minimal... why shouldn't it take up all of 32GB/64GB? just trying to make the
best game that will ever be made is all", and "I don't want anything to be held back just for the
sake of optimization". This file decides the size of everything: one scale for the world, the size
of the ring, what it is measured against, and what the card is for. A number here is measured, with
its source, or marked as an estimate._

## One to a hundred

The world is a real garden at one scale. Hum stands one metre tall, which is one centimetre of the
real garden. Every thing is its real size, times a hundred. Nothing is sized by eye.

| in the real garden | real size | to Hum |
|---|---|---|
| an aster (New England aster) | 0.9 to 1.85 m | 90 to 185 m: Cologne Cathedral is 157 m and the spire of Ulm Minster, the tallest church spire ever built, 161.5 m |
| one of its flower heads | 2.5 to 4.5 cm across | a bell 2.5 to 4.5 m across |
| its stalk; one of its leaves | under 1 cm through; 5 to 10 cm | a mast a metre through; a blade 5 to 10 m long |
| a reed (cattail) | 1.5 to 3 m | 150 to 300 m |
| alfalfa | 60 cm to 1 m | a forest 60 to 100 m high |
| a blade of grass | 4 to 9 cm | a tree 4 to 9 m tall, as wide as a door |
| a crumb of soil; a marble | 1 to 3 cm; 1.6 cm | a boulder 1 to 3 m; a ball taller than Hum |
| a drop of water | 4 mm | 40 cm: Drip sits on Hum's head |
| a pill bug; a fly | 10 mm; 7 mm | as big as Hum; a little smaller |
| a garden hose | 15 to 30 m long, 13 to 19 mm in the bore | a tunnel 1.5 to 3 km long that Hum walks through upright |
| a brass spigot | 10 to 15 cm | a monument 10 to 15 m tall |
| a puddle from the storm | 1 to 3 m | a lake 100 to 300 m across |
| a flowerpot | 15 to 30 cm | a hall 15 to 30 m high |
| a guitar; its sound hole | 1 m; 10 cm | 100 m long; a door 10 m across |
| a piano key | 2.3 cm wide, 15 cm long | a floor 2.3 m wide and 15 m long |
| the inside of a parked car | 2 to 2.8 m long, 1.2 m floor to roof | a nave 200 to 280 m long and 120 m high |
| a porch step | 18 cm | a cliff 18 m high |

The aster's height and the two churches were checked against sources on 2026-11-19 (below). The
other real sizes are from general knowledge and are checked when their land's score is written.

**What follows from it:**

- **Height is heard as lateness.** Sound travels 60 m a second here (`laws.md`, law 6). A call takes
  almost three seconds to climb the tallest aster, and its bells are heard as late again.
- **Every big thing has parts Hum's own size:** the joints of a stalk, a drop of dew, a thread on
  the spigot.
- **Small things are places:** the bore of the hose, the cup of a flower, the hollow of a pot.
- **A score gives real sizes** and the score tool multiplies. A thing in a score with no real size
  is refused.

## The ring

Twelve lands on the rim of a round lake (`world.md`).

| | size |
|---|---|
| the lake | 3.7 km across: 11 square kilometres of water |
| each land | about 3 square kilometres: a kilometre of shore, two kilometres deep, two kilometres along its outer edge |
| all twelve | about 36 square kilometres of ground |
| the whole world | 7.7 km across: 47 square kilometres |

- **A land is as big as a country to Hum.** A run straight across one takes five and a half minutes
  at 6 m a second. Nobody sees a whole land in one twenty-minute day.
- **The lake is the hub.** Its shore is 11.6 km round: more than half an hour's run, more than a
  day. Straight across by boat is a few minutes. Sound crosses it in 62 seconds, so what rings on
  the far shore is seen at once and heard a minute later; a note that is ridden (law 6) carries Hum
  over in the same minute.
- **Why this size and no smaller.** A place this rich, at a find every minute, holds five to eight
  hours of play in three square kilometres, which is what sixty to a hundred hours over twelve lands
  asks for (an estimate, from how the lands are written: eight lives, twelve wonders, a great
  instrument, a river, pools and packs in each). And things as tall as cathedrals need room to stand
  apart and be seen from far off.
- **Past the lake** each land that is made after the thirteenth morning is another three square
  kilometres, with no end to them.

These replace the estimate the atlas had before (a ring of about 3 square kilometres), which let a
"country" be crossed in a minute and a half.

## What it is measured against

Morgan asked for something bigger than the largest worlds there are. Here is what "bigger" can
honestly mean, with the numbers.

| | the largest hand-made worlds | this game |
|---|---|---|
| ground to walk | Tears of the Kingdom's surface is 61 to 84 square kilometres by players' own measurements (its maker has published none), with a second layer the same size under it; Skyrim's is about 37 to 39 | 36 square kilometres scored by hand and by rule, 11 of water, the insides of twelve great instruments, a sky and a deep; then lands without end |
| height | towers and mountains at a person's scale | every plant a tower; a reed 300 m; the sky a ladder whose rungs never end |
| scale | one | forty octaves of it: inside a petal, the planets, the beginning (`world.md`) |
| things to find, counted | Tears of the Kingdom: 152 shrines, 1,000 seeds, 120 roots of light, 147 caves, 58 wells, 139 side quests, 60 side adventures | 144 wonders, 96 lives with three wants each, 12 great instruments, 8 islets, 5 far gardens; then what the laws allow and nobody counted |
| what can be done to a thing | what its makers allowed | whatever sound would do: seven laws that hold for every thing (`laws.md`) |
| time | one long story | thirteen mornings; then a calendar that does not repeat for 92,820 mornings; lives that hand their tunes down (`years.md`) |
| memory | a save | every dawn kept: any morning of a player's life in it can be walked again |
| on the card | 16.3 GB of pictures, models and recorded sound | below |
| who made it, and on what | hundreds of people, for a three-hundred-dollar console | one person and a model, for a handheld that costs about forty dollars |
| what a player carries out | the memory of it | the memory of it, and an ear that hears things it could not (`../SPEC.md`, 6.4) |

**Where it is smaller, said plainly.** Less hand-placed ground than Tears of the Kingdom's three
layers. No recorded voices, no filmed scenes, no faces. Far fewer written quests: its people are
deep by rule and by time, not by the page (`years.md`). Whether any of this adds up to a better game
is not something a table can show. Only people playing it can, and `promises.md` is how their
answers are collected.

## What the card is for

**Why nothing on the card was a picture.** Two decisions made the game light. Its look is lines of
light on black, and a line is two points and a colour: thirty thousand of them, a whole view, are
one megabyte. Its sound is made from resonators as it plays, so nothing is recorded. A game built
that way has nothing to paint and nothing to tape, and the whole of it fits in a few gigabytes
however large its world is.

**That lightness is room, and the room is spent.** Not on padding: on the two things a light game
can afford that a heavy one can't.

### Nothing is a copy

No thing in the world is an instance of a model. Every aster, every leaf, every blade of grass and
crumb of soil is its own shape, made once by the score's rules and kept on the card, and it stays as
it is left. The blade you bent on the first evening is still bent seven years later. A path you walk
every morning wears into the grass. A leaf that was struck too hard hangs lower for good.

### Nothing is forgotten

- **The ground remembers.** This is the one picture on the card, and the player paints it. Wherever
  something sounds, it leaves its light on the ground beneath it, in its note's colour. With the
  lake low the light fades in a breath, as trails do now. Each river that is freed lets the ground
  hold more, and with the lake full it stays for good. Over months the black ground becomes a slow
  photograph of everything that has been sung on it: your own paths as bright rivers, the place a
  fight was won, the ring where a land gathered at dusk. Under it is the old light, which was there
  before you: the rounds of the lives, worn faintly into the ground where each one walks, so the
  almanac can be read by looking down. A friend who visits leaves their colour on your ground for as
  long as the card lasts.
- **Every dawn is kept.** The save made each dawn is never thrown away. Any morning of your life in
  the game can be opened from the book and walked again as it was, with your own past self walking
  it beside you, because a take is its inputs. Someone you met in the first week and who has since
  changed (`years.md`) is still there on the morning you met them.
- **Your book is kept as sound and as paper.** Each day's river song is written to the card as
  sound, with its separate voices beside it, and its page at print size. Put the card in a computer
  and it is an album and a book with a song and a page for every day you played.
- **Your friends' worlds are kept.** After a visit (`together.md`) each card holds the other's world
  as it stood that day. You can walk in it when they are not there.
- **Every place has its own true echo.** Each place's echoes are worked out from its shape on the
  laptop, slowly and properly, and kept: a few delays for every spot of open ground, and a whole
  second of real echo for every few metres inside the great instruments and the caves.

### What that comes to

All estimates, to be measured as stage 1 and stage 2 build each part.

| on the card | size |
|---|---|
| the program, the scores, the sounds as data | under 0.1 GB |
| every thing's own shape, at every distance it is seen from: about 12,600 pieces of world | 4 GB |
| the ground's old light: a 256 by 256 picture for each piece, 25 cm to a dot | 3 GB |
| the echoes | 2.5 GB |
| **as it arrives** | **about 10 GB** |
| each day played: the take, what the dawn keeps of the world's changes, the song as sound with its voices, the page | 25 to 45 MB |
| each friend's world | 1 to 4 GB |

| card | room for days | at a day every day |
|---|---|---|
| 32 GB (the two units have these now) | 420 to 760 days | one to two years |
| 64 GB | 1,100 to 1,960 days | three to five years |

- **A card is a number of years.** When one fills, the oldest dawns are thinned to their takes
  alone, a few hundred kilobytes each, from which any of them can be made again. Nothing is ever
  lost; the oldest mornings just take a while to open.
- **The handheld can carry it** (measured, `docs/hardware-target.md`): the card reads 43 MB a second
  with next to no interrupts; a picture the size of one piece's ground uploads in about 1.3 ms; the
  screen needs 18 million dots of it a second and the GPU samples 530 million; forty-nine pieces of
  ground in memory are 13 MB of the 815 that are free.
- **The same world on every card.** What the rules read is the same on a 32 GB card and a 64 GB one.
  Two consoles with different cards make the same take.
- **Recorded sound stays Morgan's call** (`../SPEC.md`, 17). Nothing needs it. If he ever wants his
  own playing in the world, the room is there: an hour of it is 0.7 GB.
