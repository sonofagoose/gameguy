# Two games, as people say they feel

> **Status, 2026-11-26.** Material (2026-11-20): what players and critics say Majora's Mask and
> Tears of the Kingdom feel like. Not the direction. What the game is: `design/the-game.md`.

_Morgan, 2026-11-20: "maybe a web search/s about how the human experience actually is for Majora's
mask and Tears of the Kingdom actually are... would help". Searched and read that day. This is what
players and critics say those two games feel like, what the makers of one of them learned about
building a world, and what each point means for this game. It is for craft only: nothing of either
game's world, people, music or look is used (`SPEC.md`, rule 11). Quotations are the writers' own
words; the sources are at the end._

## Majora's Mask, as felt

- **It is remembered as people, not as places.** One writer calls it "a collection of vignettes" and
  says "more than anything, to me, at least, it's about love". The same writer says it "made me look
  at the world in a different light", and takes its message to be "be loving to people".
- **Its sadness comes from routine.** A reviewer puts it as a "density of interlocking human
  routine, all of it doomed and looping". People's moods change across the three days, and the
  town's cheerful music turns tense by the last.
- **The days were kept few so they could be learned.** A week was planned and cut to three days so
  that people's schedules could be remembered. (This is from a fan wiki and not confirmed by its
  makers.)
- **The quest people remember most pays nothing.** It is optional, long and easy to fail, and its
  reward is seeing two people together. Doing it costs every other kindness of that cycle. Some
  players find it too vague and too easy to ruin to enjoy.
- **Being noticed matters.** One of its people can't be seen at all until you have the means to see
  him, and the scene is about that.
- **Help is the way forward.** Calming someone lets you go on. What you are given for helping is
  both a tool and a keepsake of the person.

**What it means here**

| what was learned | in this game | where |
|---|---|---|
| a round has to be short enough to know by heart | most lives go round three, four, five or seven places, one a morning. Thirteen and seventeen are events, not routines | `spec/people.md`; `spec/years.md` |
| the reward is the scene | a life you help joins its land's song, which you then hear every dusk. Nothing is handed over but a colour of a voice | `spec/people.md` |
| a kindness should cost something | you can't be on two shores at dusk. Choosing one meeting is missing another | `spec/years.md` |
| it should never be ruined by a slip | nothing can be failed for good. What you resolved stays resolved, a missed meeting leaves its memory in the ground and the pools, and the almanac shows what is coming | `spec/years.md` |
| sadness comes from time, not from punishment | lives change over years, and every dawn is kept, so the morning you met someone can always be walked again | `spec/years.md`; `spec/size.md` |
| being noticed matters | the first step of helping anyone is to stop, lean in, and keep their phrase | `spec/people.md` |

## Tears of the Kingdom, as felt

- **Alive, and not about you.** One long review: "In a million little ways, Tears of the Kingdom's
  world is alive and responsive to my presence", and also "wonderfully indifferent to me".
- **Delight in trying.** The same reviewer describes "a tactile pleasure to the assembly process"
  and "a childlike delight" in trying an idea to see what happens, and says "you truly feel free to
  solve the challenges it puts before you in countless ways".
- **Wonder that lasts.** After more than a hundred hours the reviewer still finds "new sights that
  take my breath away", and says the game "put me in touch with feelings of wonder that I haven't
  felt in a long time".
- **Looking down.** Reviewers credit the view from above: falling from the sky and seeing the land
  laid out is what sends them somewhere.
- **Where it tires people.** The underground layer is called "mostly empty" and "a chore"; the sky
  is thinner than promised; goals come in counted lists; small tasks repeat; "everything feels
  temporary", from weapons to the things you build; and some ask whether it simply got too large.
  Returning to a map already known dulls the first wonder for some.

**What its makers learned about building a world** (from their own 2017 talk, as reported)

- **Paths lead people by the nose.** Their first plan put discoveries along routes between tall
  towers. Tracking their own testers showed about four in five walking those routes, as if on "an
  invisible, but obvious guide rope", and the rest wandering at random. They threw the plan away.
- **Landmarks that answer a need pull people.** Each kind of place gives something a player wants,
  and each is made to be seen from far off: lit, smoking, or under one huge shape.
- **Hills hide and show.** Their land is built of hills and peaks. A hill gives a choice, over or
  round. The eye goes to its top, so something is put there. It hides what is behind it, so only a
  few things call at once, and coming round it shows the next.
- **One thing leads to three.** On the way to any landmark two or three more come into view.
- **Night changes what calls.**

**What it means here**

| what was learned | in this game | where |
|---|---|---|
| answer the player, and go on without them | the laws answer every touch; the lives are sums that keep their rounds whether you come or not | `spec/laws.md`; `spec/people.md` |
| the first minutes should be a toy | you call, a ring runs out, and things answer, before any goal exists | `SPEC.md` 6.1 |
| no layer may be a thinner copy | the sky, the deep and the lands past the lake each owe everything a land owes | `spec/shape.md` |
| no counted lists | the songbook shows what was found and never how much is left | `spec/shape.md` |
| what you make should last | what you build stays where you built it, and the ground keeps what you wear into it | `spec/shape.md`; `spec/size.md` |
| no rope | most of what is worth finding is off the paths, and a person's own take is checked: no more than half their steps on a path | `spec/shape.md`; `spec/promises.md` |
| things call by need and from far | each kind of thing has its own far sight and far sound, and here a landmark can call round a corner and in the dark, because it sounds, and its phrase arrives as a ripple of light from its side, so the way to it is seen even on one speaker | `spec/shape.md` |
| hide and show | tall things are black shapes; only two or three things call from any place | `spec/shape.md` |
| size has to be earned | a land is three square kilometres only if something calls from every piece of it | `spec/size.md`; `spec/promises.md` |
| watch where people really go | a take is a list of hands, so every session is its own map of where a person went and stopped; and the ground's kept light draws the same map in the world | `spec/promises.md` |
